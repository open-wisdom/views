# Ontological Genealogy

## The Succession of Operative Ontologies Through "Awakening from the Meaning Crisis"

This document traces the central thread of [Vervaeke's 51-episode series](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLND1JCRq8Vuh3f0P5qjrSdb5eC1ZfZwWJ) as a single narrative: the genealogy of ontological assumptions about self, world, knowing, and meaning — what they were, how they shifted, and what each transition did to the human capacity for wisdom and contact with the sacred.

---

### 1. The Continuous Cosmos (Pre-Axial, before ~800 BCE)

**Operative assumptions.** Nature, culture, and divinity form a seamless continuum. Power is the fundamental category: gods are powerful, kings are powerful, the cosmos is organized by power relations. Time is cyclical — eternal recurrence without progress. Meaning consists in harmonic embeddedness: fitting into the cosmic power structure, maintaining one's place. The self is not an interior subject but a node in a web of cosmic relationships. There is no gap between appearance and reality, between how things seem and how they are.

**Consequence for meaning.** Meaning is automatic and pervasive but shallow. There is no concept of self-transcendence, no recognition that one's current way of seeing could be fundamentally mistaken, no tools for systematic self-correction. Wisdom is prudence — knowing how to tap into cyclical power. The machinery of meaning-making works, but it cannot examine itself.

---

### 2. The Axial Revolution (~800–300 BCE)

**The shift.** Independently in Greece, Israel, India, and China, a radical discovery: ontological assumptions can be examined and transformed. The continuous cosmos shatters into a "two worlds" mythology — an everyday world of illusion and suffering contrasted with a real world accessible only through transformative wisdom. This is Charles Taylor's "Great Disembedding": the extraction of the self from automatic cosmic embeddedness into a position from which it can examine its own assumptions.

**What enabled it.** New psychotechnologies: alphabetic literacy (enabling second-order thinking — thought examining itself), coinage (enabling abstract symbolic representation), rational argumentation (trained through democratic debate), and cosmic narrative (the Hebraic invention of time as an open story with moral directionality). These are not biological changes but "software upgrades" — culturally transmitted cognitive tools that restructure what is thinkable, perceivable, and real.

**Consequence for meaning.** Meaning becomes deeper but precarious. The capacity for self-transcendence — seeing through illusion into reality — is discovered. But so is the capacity for self-deception: the same mind that can examine its assumptions can also hide them from itself. The dual Axial problematic is established: the machinery of meaning-making can both transcend and deceive. Every subsequent development in the series is a response to this tension.

---

### 3. The Great Generative Ontologies (~500 BCE – 300 CE)

**Plato's participatory ontology.** Knowing is mutual transformation between knower and known (conformity). Reality is structured by Forms — intrinsically intelligible patterns that are both what makes things be and what enables us to know them. Wisdom is the anagogic spiral: transformation enables truth, truth enables further transformation, in a self-reinforcing ascent. The psyche has structure (tripartite: reason, thymos, appetite) and can be integrated or fragmented.

**Aristotle's hylomorphism.** Things become real by having form imposed on matter. Living things are autopoietic — self-making. Character is a virtual engine of cultivated virtues that regulates development. Knowledge is conformity: sharing structural-functional organization with what is known. The geocentric cosmos provides cosmological support — reality is intrinsically purposeful, intelligible, and meaningful. Agent-Arena attunement (worldview attunement) is the convergence of how we know and what we know.

**Buddhism's dependent origination and no-self.** The self is not a unified rational agent but a dynamic ecology vulnerable to colonization by its own sub-processes (parasitic processing). All phenomena arise through dependent origination — mutual co-constitution rather than independent substance. The Four Ennobling Provocations diagnose Dukkha (loss of agency through self-destructive processing) and prescribe the Eightfold Path as a counteractive dynamical system — a self-organizing ecology of practices that intervenes simultaneously across cognition, character, and consciousness.

**Neoplatonic emanation (Plotinus).** Integration is the principle of realness — things are more real insofar as they are more unified, more structurally-functionally organized, more intelligible. Reality exists in levels from pure potentiality to pure actuality (The One). Conforming to higher levels transforms the self to higher levels. The One cannot be known propositionally but only experienced through being one with it. This establishes the ultimate primacy of participatory over propositional knowing.

**Consequence for meaning.** The generative ontologies share five structural features: intrinsic intelligibility (reality is structured and responsive to knowing), participatory knowing (knowing is co-constitution), developmental teleology (growth has direction), agent-arena co-constitution (self and world mutually determine each other), and integration across domains (science, spirituality, therapy, and wisdom are unified). Together these create the conditions for rich, deep, sustainable meaning-making.

---

### 4. The Christian Synthesis (~400–1200 CE)

**The shift.** Augustine integrates Neoplatonism, Christianity, and Gnostic insight into a three-dimensional "space of meaning": the Nomological Order (Aristotelian scientific coherence), the Normative Order (Neoplatonic ladder of significance from chaos to the Good), and the Narrative Order (Christian story of purpose moving through agape toward consummation). These map precisely onto what contemporary cognitive science identifies as the components of meaning: coherence, significance, and purpose.

**Agape as ontological force.** Christianity introduces agape — love that transforms non-persons into persons, the "closest thing to a miracle." This is genuine ontological transformation through participatory relationship. Augustine recognizes that reason itself is driven by love, and that damaged love (not damaged reasoning) is the source of self-destructive behavior.

**The first cracks (Aquinas).** To accommodate the rediscovered Aristotle alongside Christian doctrine, Aquinas separates the natural world (accessible to reason and science) from the supernatural world (accessible only to faith and will). This appears to protect both domains but introduces a fatal separation. Love and reason are pulled apart. Faith shifts from participatory flow to willful assertion of propositions. The psychotechnological shift from Lectio Divina (transformative reading) to silent propositional consumption, and from the extensive self (embedded in world) to the intensive self (located in the head), compounds the damage.

**Consequence for meaning.** The synthesis sustains meaning for a millennium — the most successful ontological integration in Western history. But the seeds of its destruction are internal: the separations that begin with Aquinas will widen until the entire framework collapses.

---

### 5. The Unraveling (~1300–1900)

**Nominalism (Ockham).** Universals don't exist in reality; all order is linguistic construction. Reality loses intrinsic intelligibility — the first condition of generative ontology.

**The scientific revolution (Copernicus, Galileo).** The cosmos transforms from animate, purposeful, intelligible to dead mechanism. The primary/secondary qualities distinction exiles meaning from objectivity: the real world has only mathematical properties; everything that makes experience meaningful is merely subjective.

**Luther.** Individual conscience becomes ultimate authority, severing tradition, institution, and community. Monasteries — the institutional infrastructure for wisdom cultivation — are destroyed. Self-transcendence is declared the greatest sin of pride.

**Descartes.** Mind (thinking substance) and matter (extended substance) share no properties but must interact. The self shrinks from extensive to intensive — trapped behind an experiential veil, touching only its own representations. Participatory knowing becomes structurally impossible.

**Hobbes.** Cognition is material computation; no soul needed. This births AI conceptually but reduces meaning to mechanism.

**Romanticism.** Attempts to recover contact with reality through the irrational and imaginal, but offers self-transcendence without self-correction — lacking the sapiential traditions that made ancient practices safe and effective.

**Hegel.** Reduces meaning to pure propositionality in a totalizing system. Three critiques (Nietzsche: missing will; Kierkegaard: missing transformation; Marx: missing materiality) expose fatal omissions.

**Consequence for meaning.** Each shift removes a structural support: intrinsic intelligibility, participatory knowing, developmental teleology, agent-arena co-constitution, and domain integration are eliminated one by one. The cumulative effect: the three orders of meaning (coherence, significance, purpose) are pulled apart, each contracting as its supporting ontological conditions are removed.

---

### 6. The Crisis (~1900–present)

**The three-way dilemma.** Traditional religion risks fundamentalism. Pseudo-religious ideologies (communism, fascism) produce totalitarian violence by attempting to replace religious practice with belief systems. Post-religious fragmentation leaves individuals autodidactically vulnerable. Nietzsche's "death of God" names not a theological claim but the cultural diagnosis: the entire framework sustaining meaning has collapsed, and nothing has replaced it.

**The meaning crisis as ontological crisis.** The crisis is not a social problem (amenable to policy), a psychological problem (amenable to therapy), or a philosophical problem (amenable to argument). It is an ontological crisis — a breakdown in the very frameworks through which anything can appear as meaningful, real, or worth caring about. The machinery of meaning-making is malfunctioning at the cultural level because its ontological conditions have been systematically eliminated.

---

### 7. The Reconstruction (Vervaeke's Proposal)

**Relevance realization as post-Cartesian ontology.** The cognitive science of relevance realization provides scientific grounds for rejecting the Cartesian framework and grounding a participatory alternative. Intelligence is not computational logic but dynamic attunement to what matters. Participatory knowing is ontologically primary (the asymmetric dependence hierarchy). The agent-arena relationship is transjective — neither subjective nor objective but constituted in dynamic coupling. Mind is process, not substance.

**The recovery of the sacred.** Sacredness is the transjective relationship between the combinatorial inexhaustibility of reality and the never-ending process of relevance realization. Symbols are not representations but enactive psychotechnologies. Religio (lived transformative practice) is distinguished from credo (propositional belief system).

**Wisdom as recursive relevance realization.** Wisdom is the self-organizing ecology of cognitive styles and psychotechnologies that optimize relevance realization — systematic seeing through illusion into reality. It is not a possession but an ongoing process, grounded in participatory knowing, cultivated through ecologies of practice, and sustained within communities.

**The prophets' convergence.** Heidegger (attunement and aletheia), Corbin (the imaginal), Jung (individuation and archetypes), Tillich (the God beyond the God of theism), and Barfield (final participation) all converge on a transjective, participatory, non-theistic ontology. The design principles for a viable post-meaning-crisis ontology: transjective relationality, inexhaustible depth, transformation through participation, process over substance, integration without totalization.

**The unresolved edge.** Vervaeke insists that genuine ontological transformation requires participatory processes — embodied practice embedded in sapiential community. Yet the Open Wisdom project has discovered that AI behavior transforms radically under ontological prompting — propositional in form but apparently participatory in function. Whether this disanalogy reveals a limitation of AI, a limitation of Vervaeke's framework, or something genuinely new about the relationship between propositional and participatory knowing remains the generative question at the frontier.
