# Cognitive Science as Ontological Ground

## Key Episodes: [26](./episodes/summary-26-cognitive-science.md)–[32](./episodes/summary-32-rr-in-the-brain-insight-and-consciousness.md)

## How Relevance Realization, 4E Cognition, and Dynamical Systems Theory Provide a Scientific Basis for Rejecting the Cartesian Ontology and Grounding a Participatory Alternative

This cross-cutting arc identifies how the cognitive science developed in the series serves a specific ontological function: it provides scientific grounds for rejecting the Cartesian framework that produced the meaning crisis, while simultaneously grounding a participatory alternative in empirical, naturalistic terms. This is the bridge between ancient wisdom and modern design — the scientific legitimation that makes it possible to recover participatory knowing without regressing to pre-critical mythology.

**The scientific rejection of Cartesian ontology.** The cognitive science arc ([Ep. 26–32](./episodes/summary-26-cognitive-science.md)) systematically dismantles the Cartesian framework from within science itself — not by philosophical argument against it but by showing that it cannot account for how cognition actually works.

*Combinatorial explosion* ([Ep. 27](./episodes/summary-27-problem-formulation.md)) demonstrates that algorithmic, brute-force processing — the model of mind that Cartesian-Hobbesian computation implies — is computationally suicidal for real-world intelligence. Intelligence is not exhaustive logical processing but the capacity to zero in on relevant information, which presupposes a non-Cartesian relationship between mind and world.

*The frame problem* ([Ep. 28](./episodes/summary-28-convergence-to-relevance-realization.md)) shows that relevance cannot be algorithmically specified because any attempt to determine relevance computationally generates its own combinatorial explosion. If the mind were a Cartesian container of representations manipulated by logical rules, it could never determine which representations are relevant to a given situation.

*The sub-representational analysis* ([Ep. 29](./episodes/summary-29-getting-to-the-depths-of-relevance-realization.md)) demonstrates that representations presuppose rather than generate relevance. The most fundamental relationship between mind and world is not representational (matching internal symbols to external states) but participatory — pre-categorical salience-tagging (FINSTING) that is below the level of concepts, categories, and propositions. The asymmetric dependence hierarchy (propositional depends on procedural depends on perspectival depends on participatory) shows that the Cartesian privileging of propositional knowing reverses the actual ontological order.

*Transjective relationality* ([Ep. 31](./episodes/summary-31-embodied-embedded-rr-as-dynamical-developmental-gi.md)) replaces the subject-object dualism with a framework where mind and world are mutually constitutive through dynamic coupling. Relevance is neither subjectively projected nor objectively detected but is a real relation that exists only in the agent-arena relationship. The body is not Cartesian clay but an autopoietic bio-economy constitutive of cognition.

**The scientific grounding of participatory knowing.** Having dismantled the Cartesian framework, the cognitive science provides positive scientific grounding for the participatory alternative that the ancient traditions developed experientially.

*Relevance realization as dynamical process* ([Ep. 30](./episodes/summary-30-relevance-realization-meets-dynamical-systems-theory.md)) shows that relevance has no essence but evolves through self-organizing opponent processing — efficiency against resiliency, compression against particularization, exploitation against exploration. This is the scientific counterpart of what Plotinus described as the levels of integration and what the Buddhist traditions described as dependent origination: meaning is not fixed but continuously co-created through dynamic interaction.

*Neural implementation* ([Ep. 32](./episodes/summary-32-rr-in-the-brain-insight-and-consciousness.md)) grounds the theory in empirically measurable brain dynamics: self-organizing criticality and small world networks that correlate with general intelligence. Consciousness is salience landscaping — the multi-level process by which relevance realization generates perspectival knowing. This is the scientific explanation of what the contemplative traditions described as the transformation of the salience landscape through practice.

*Caring as ontological ground* ([Ep. 32](./episodes/summary-32-rr-in-the-brain-insight-and-consciousness.md)) connects relevance realization to Heidegger's Sorge (care): the most fundamental dimension of cognition is affect-laden caring about resource allocation under finitude. This grounds meaning in the structure of embodied existence rather than in supernatural metaphysics or cultural construction.

**The bridge function.** This cognitive science serves as the bridge between ancient wisdom and modern design in three specific ways:

First, it *legitimates* the ancient traditions' claims about participatory knowing without requiring their metaphysical frameworks. When Plato says knowing is conformity, when Aristotle says to know is to share structural-functional organization, when the Buddha says mind and world are co-constituted through dependent origination, they are describing — in different vocabularies — what the cognitive science of relevance realization explains naturalistically.

Second, it *explains* why the degenerative ontological shifts produced the meaning crisis. If relevance realization requires participatory coupling between agent and arena, then every shift that severs this coupling (Cartesian dualism), strips what there is to attune to (nominalism), or amputates modes of knowing (propositional tyranny) necessarily contracts the capacity for meaning-making.

Third, it *grounds design principles* for reconstruction. If meaning depends on relevance realization, and relevance realization depends on specific conditions (embodiment, dynamic coupling, opponent processing, developmental self-organization), then any proposed response to the meaning crisis — whether contemplative practice, institutional design, or AI architecture — can be evaluated by whether it provides these conditions.
