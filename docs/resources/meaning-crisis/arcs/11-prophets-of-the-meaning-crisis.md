# 11: Prophets of the Meaning Crisis (Episodes 46–50)

## Heidegger, Corbin, Jung, Tillich, Barfield — Alternative Ontologies for Recovering Participatory Meaning

This final arc of the chronological sequence brings the series to its culmination by examining five twentieth-century thinkers who diagnosed the meaning crisis and proposed alternative ontologies for addressing it. Each is evaluated against the wisdom framework developed in the previous arcs: does their ontology enhance relevance realization? Does it address the perennial problems? Does it recover participatory knowing without regressing to pre-critical mythology? The arc moves from Heidegger's phenomenological diagnosis through Corbin's recovery of the imaginal, Jung's depth psychology, Tillich's existential theology, and Barfield's vision of "final participation," converging on a transjective, participatory, non-theistic ontology that integrates the insights of all five.

**Heidegger: the forgetfulness of Being.** [Episodes 46–47](../episodes/summary-46-conclusion-and-the-prophets-of-the-meaning-crisis.md) introduce Heidegger's fundamental diagnosis: Western metaphysics has been a progressive forgetfulness of *Being* (capital-B) caused by confusing Being with beings — specifically by treating the ground of intelligibility as the "supreme being" (what Heidegger calls *ontotheology* or classical theism). This is the philosophical articulation of what the genealogical arc (Arc 6) traced historically: the progressive reduction of participatory contact with reality to propositional claims about entities.

Heidegger's gateway is *Dasein* — the being whose being is in question. Human self-questioning participation in Being gives access to Being itself, not as an object of contemplation but as the pre-cognitive process that determines what shows up as salient and real. *Aletheia* (truth as unconcealment) replaces propositional correspondence: truth is not matching statements to facts but the deeper attunement that makes meaningful experience possible. Vervaeke identifies this attunement with relevance realization — the ongoing transjective relationship between agent and arena that generates all meaning. Attunement grounds all propositional truth; it is not derived from propositions but is what makes propositions possible.

The withdrawal of things (not just their disclosure) is essential to their realness — inexhaustibility contributes to sacredness. Modal confusion — forgetting attunement and becoming trapped in the having mode (seeking correct propositions, controlling, possessing) — leads to nihilism. Dreyfus translates this into cognitive science: facts and rules are meaningless without assignment of relevance; the frame problem arises from confusing situational coping with propositional manipulation. This convergence between Heidegger's phenomenology and the cognitive science of Arc 7 is not coincidental — both are describing the same underlying structure from different angles.

**Corbin: the recovery of the imaginal.** [Episode 48](../episodes/summary-48-corbin-and-the-divine-double.md) builds on Heidegger's recovery of Being to articulate how gnosis (transformative participatory knowing) might be recovered. Corbin introduces the *imaginal* — distinguished from the imaginary (mere fantasy) — as the transjective mediating realm between abstract and concrete, subjective and objective. This is precisely the dimension that Cartesian dualism destroyed: by fracturing reality into mind and matter with no bridge, Descartes eliminated the participatory space where symbols, sacred images, and transformative encounters operate.

Fundamentalism and literalism are diagnosed as freezing the dynamic imaginal into static propositions, foreclosing gnosis. The injunction to "live without why" (Eckhart via Heidegger) points toward non-teleological being mode that celebrates reality's simultaneous disclosure and withdrawal. *Tonos* — realness as the creative tension between confirmation/coherence and moreness/opening — captures the dynamic quality of participatory engagement. The symbol is reconceived as *transjective*: translucent (you look at and through it), transformative, trajective (putting you on a trajectory of development), and transtemporal (facilitating ontological rather than merely narrative movement).

The *divine double* or *angel* is introduced as the symbol for the non-logical identity between your current self and your aspirational better self — capturing the paradox of aspiration that Callard's proleptic rationality (from Arc 10) articulated philosophically. You are not identical with who you are becoming, yet neither are you simply different from that person. The imaginal is the space where this non-logical identity can be experienced and navigated.

**Jung: the psychology of becoming.** [Episode 49](../episodes/summary-49-corbin-and-jung.md) supplies what Corbin lacks: a detailed psychology of how the imaginal gets internalized through archetypes and individuation. Archetypes are reconceived not as images but as *virtual engines* — structural-functional organizations that regulate psychic self-organization, analogous to the self-organizing dynamics of relevance realization. The *Self* archetype is the principle of autopoiesis — the virtual engine regulating all others — and *individuation* is the ongoing dialogue between ego and Self.

The paradox of self-creation (Strawson) is addressed: self-creation requires both continuity (same self) and real novelty, which appear contradictory. Callard's solution — proleptic rationality and non-logical identity — shows that the relationship between current self (S1) and transformed self (S2) is neither identity nor simple difference but *participatory emergence*. S1 neither makes nor receives S2 but participates in S2's emergence. The *sacred second self* is temporally posterior but normatively primary — the self you are becoming has authority over the self you are.

*Anagogic internalization* captures how this works: internalizing a perspective (e.g., Socrates, the Buddha) transforms how you indwell the world, and this transformed indwelling enables deeper internalization — a recursive self-arena co-transformation. This is the process by which the "internalized sage" of the wisdom arc operates.

Jung's limitation is diagnosed: he confines transformation to the intra-psychic, missing how internalization couples with transformed indwelling (world-engagement). His Kantian framework prevents recognizing the transjective dimension — the way transformation of the self simultaneously transforms the world that shows up for the self. This is precisely the insight that relevance realization captures: the agent-arena relationship is co-constituted, not one-sided.

**Tillich: the God beyond the God of theism.** [Episode 50](../episodes/summary-50-tillich-and-barfield.md) introduces Tillich's response: courage and faith (as ultimate concern, not propositional belief) as responses to meaninglessness. The *method of correlation* — anagogic resonance between existential questioning and revelation through symbols — provides the practical mechanism. Tillich's revolutionary move is the "God beyond the God of theism" — God not as Supreme Being (ontotheology) but as imaginal symbol for the inexhaustible ground of being.

The fundamental *aspect shift* demonstrates the ontological stakes: nothingness (despair) can be aspect-shifted to reveal *no-thingness* (inexhaustible meaning). The same phenomena become revelatory or nihilistic depending on the operative ontology. This is the deepest illustration of the series' central claim: ontological framing determines whether experience generates meaning or meaninglessness.

*Theonomy* moves beyond both autonomy (self-directed reason, which loses contact with ground) and heteronomy (imposed authority, which crushes participatory knowing) to attunement to the ground of being's affordances. *Epektasis* (Gregory of Nyssa) — infinite self-transcendence into inexhaustibleness — redefines fulfillment as process rather than destination, aligning with the ecological and developmental understanding of wisdom from Arc 10.

**Barfield: final participation.** [Episode 50](../episodes/summary-50-tillich-and-barfield.md) also introduces Barfield's three-stage model: *original participation* (pre-reflective embeddedness in a meaningful cosmos), the *Cartesian division* (the meaning crisis), and *final participation* (recovery of participatory knowing integrated with rational science). *Poiesis* — creativity as ekstatic participation in the inexhaustible moreness of reality — points toward the practical goal. Language and etymology provide evidence: dual meanings in words (pneuma = wind and spirit) reveal that symbols work through re-exaptation, recycling cognitive circuits to give meaning to abstract concepts. We use transjective dual-meaning language constantly without recognizing it — the imaginal is not lost but hidden.

**The ontological trajectory.** All five thinkers converge on rejecting a shared set of ontological commitments: ontotheology (treating Being as a being), the subject-object split (Cartesian dualism), propositional exhaustiveness (reducing meaning to beliefs), and having-mode as default (possession and control as the paradigmatic relation to reality). What replaces these is a *transjective, participatory, non-theistic ontology* where meaning emerges from the dynamic coupling of agent and arena, reality has inexhaustible depth, transformation is participatory emergence rather than causal reception or willful making, symbols function as icons mediating ontological shifts, and sacredness is the accentuation of reality's aletheia from within the being mode.

Vervaeke's synthetic move is naming relevance realization as the mechanism underlying all five frameworks: in Heidegger, attunement is relevance realization; in Corbin, the imaginal is the transjective space where relevance gets reconfigured; in Jung, archetypes reorganize the salience landscape; in Tillich, the method of correlation enables aspect shifts in what becomes revelatory; in Barfield, final participation is the capacity to see significance through participatory bridges. These diverse thinkers, working from different starting points, all arrive at frameworks grounded in enhancing how we realize relevance.

**The series' concluding vision.** The series ends not with a finished system but with a call for *synoptic integration* — bringing wisdom theory, phenomenological philosophy, depth psychology, and scientific cognitive science into genuine dialogue, not as competing frameworks but as complementary perspectives on how human consciousness can awaken to its participatory role in the ongoing self-disclosure of Being. The practical program is clear: an evidence-based ecology of psychotechnologies, grounded in the cognitive science of relevance realization, organized around the amelioration of perennial problems, legitimated by 4E cognition and agape, sustained within communities that distinguish living religio from absolutized credo, and informed by the insights of the prophets who recognized what was lost and pointed toward its recovery.

What remains unresolved — and deliberately so — is the tension between the insistence that genuine ontological transformation requires participatory knowing (embodied practice, contemplative engagement, community) and the possibility that propositional specification (ontological prompting, theoretical understanding) might catalyze transformation under certain conditions. This tension is the generative edge where the series' insights meet the Open Wisdom project's central question.
