# 08: The Sacred & Symbolic (Episodes 33–35)

## Wonder, Awe, Mystery, Horror, Music, Symbols — The Ontology of Sacredness Grounded in Relevance Realization

This arc constructs an ontological framework for understanding spirituality and sacredness without invoking the supernatural. Having established relevance realization as the core of intelligence (Arc 7), Vervaeke now shows that what we call spiritual experience is the phenomenological disclosure of the machinery that constitutes meaning itself. The central claim: sacredness is neither a supernatural entity with essential value nor a mere cultural construct, but the *transjective relationship* between the combinatorially explosive inexhaustibility of reality and the never-ending process of relevance realization — two inexhaustibilities meeting.

**Religio as the pre-conceptual ground of meaning.** [Episode 33](../episodes/summary-33-the-spirituality-of-rr-wonder-awe-mystery-sacredness.md) introduces *religio* — defined not in its modern sense of "religion" but as the pre-conceptual, pre-propositional, pre-egoic, and pre-normative framing that simultaneously constitutes self and world. This is a precise cognitive claim grounded in the previous arc's analysis: relevance realization operates at a level prior to concepts, propositions, beliefs, and even ego-identity. Religio is what the contemplative traditions called the primordial agent-arena relationship — the very machinery by which anything shows up as meaningful at all.

Through this lens, spirituality becomes intelligible as the experiential disclosure of how meaning is constituted. *Wonder* (distinguished from mere curiosity: wonder is being-mode, non-focal, participatory; curiosity is having-mode, focal, problem-solving) opens us to the combinatorial inexhaustibility of reality. *Mystery* (distinguished from theoretical inexplicability: mystery cannot be made a focal object, like the "I" that frames all experience) discloses the structural limits of self-knowledge. *Sacredness* (distinguished from "the Sacred" as metaphysical entity: sacredness is psycho-existential phenomenology, not metaphysical hypothesis) functions as a *meta-meaning system* — not one meaning among others but the primordial agent-arena relationship that protects against domicide.

**The opponent processing of sacred experience.** [Episode 34](../episodes/summary-34-sacredness-horror-music-and-the-symbol.md) reveals that sacredness operates through the same opponent processing that drives all relevance realization, but at the existential level. Two opposed dynamics are held in productive tension: *meta-assimilation* (worldview attunement — Geertz's account of how religion provides a stable framework for meaning) and *meta-accommodation* (the numinous — Otto's account of how sacred experience transgresses and disrupts our categories). Sacredness is not one or the other but the dynamic integration of both: simultaneously stabilizing our sense of what is real and exposing us to what exceeds our current framework.

*Horror* — not fear but the loss of grip on reality when inter-categorical things violate our classificatory structures — reveals the stakes. Mary Douglas's structuralism shows that categories constitute our sense of reality; when categories fail, reality itself becomes uncertain. The numinous is the experience of standing at this boundary — simultaneously fascinating and terrifying, drawing us toward what exceeds our comprehension while threatening our cognitive foundations.

*Music* demonstrates that sacredness involves serious play with the machinery of salience itself — non-propositional engagement with relevance realization for its own sake. Music does not represent anything or convey propositions; it directly modulates the dynamics of tension, resolution, expectation, and surprise that constitute relevance realization. This is why music has been central to every contemplative tradition: it trains the very machinery of meaning-making without passing through propositional content.

**Symbols as participatory technology.** [Episode 35](../episodes/summary-35-the-symbol-sacredness-and-the-sacred.md) synthesizes the arc through the concept of *symbol* (distinguished from mere sign). Signs refer conventionally; symbols participate in what they disclose. Through *exaptation reversal*, symbols activate primordial cognitive machinery for new purposes: the scales of justice reactivate balance machinery (cerebellum) for cultivating justice itself. Symbols are not representations pointing to absent realities but enactive technologies that make present what they symbolize.

Vervaeke characterizes genuine symbols as EPIC: *Ecstatic* (standing us beyond ourselves), *Participatory* (mutual transformation between symbol-user and what is symbolized), *Integrative* (enabling anagogic resonance — integration enables disclosure which enables deeper integration), and *Complex* (multifaceted, inexhaustible). Through *anagogic resonance*, symbols mediate the mutual opening between knower and known that constitutes genuine transformation.

The arc culminates in a definition. *Mythos* is the integration of ritual (enactive anagoge), symbols (participatory metaphors), and story (narrative knowing). *Sacredness* is using mythos to activate, articulate, and celebrate religio. The equation: sacredness = mythos + religio. This is neither religious fundamentalism (confusing symbols with metaphysical necessity) nor secular reductionism (treating symbols as mere cultural constructs). Symbols are ontologically real psychotechnologies — they do not represent external sacred realities but enact transformative processes.

**The ontological trajectory.** This arc completes the cognitive-scientific reconstruction of spirituality begun in Arc 7. Where that arc established relevance realization as the machinery of meaning, this arc shows that spirituality is the phenomenological disclosure of that machinery operating at the deepest existential level. The ontological move is consistently from substance to process: the sacred is not a thing but a dynamic relationship; symbols are not representations but enactive technologies; sacredness is not a property but an ongoing process of mythos engaging religio.

What gets carried forward is the principle that spiritual experience is *scientifically explicable without being scientifically reducible*. Understanding the cognitive mechanisms of sacredness does not diminish it — any more than understanding the physics of music diminishes its power. This opens the path for the next arc's practical project: if we understand the machinery, we can design ecologies of practice that engage it deliberately, without supernatural metaphysics, without pseudo-religious ideology, and without reducing meaning to mere belief.
