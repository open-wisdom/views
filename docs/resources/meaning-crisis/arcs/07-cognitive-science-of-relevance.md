# 07: Cognitive Science of Relevance (Episodes 26–32)

## Problem Formulation, Relevance Realization, Dynamical Systems, 4E Cognition, Consciousness — Building the Non-Cartesian Ontology of Mind

This arc is the constructive pivot of the entire series. Having traced the genealogy of the meaning crisis through its historical causes (Arcs 1–6), Vervaeke now builds the scientific foundation for addressing it. Across seven episodes, he develops a non-Cartesian ontology of mind grounded in cognitive science — one that explains the machinery of meaning-making (relevance realization) in naturalistic, non-dualist terms, thereby healing the rift between the scientific worldview and the very intelligence that generates it. The result is a framework that grounds a return to ancient wisdom within contemporary science rather than against it.

**The methodological framework.** [Episode 26](../episodes/summary-26-cognitive-science.md) establishes cognitive science not as any single discipline but as *synoptic integration* — philosophically astute bridging across neuroscience, AI, psychology, linguistics, and anthropology that creates "multi-apt" constructs preserving disciplinary differences while revealing identities across them. The criterion is *plausibility*: balancing convergence (multiple independent evidential sources reducing bias) with elegance (integrative explanatory power across domains). This is not eclectic sampling but disciplined theoretical unification — the same cognitive act, Vervaeke notes, that Plotinus identified as the principle of understanding.

The episode also diagnoses why we are trapped: unable to return to religion, unwilling to embrace pseudo-religious ideologies, yet needing psychotechnologies that transform consciousness. Political solutions fail because they ignore the participatory knowing that generates meaning. Cognitive science offers a scientific way to study the "meaning machinery" while simultaneously healing the fragmentation that the scientific worldview itself produced.

**The problem of relevance.** [Episode 27](../episodes/summary-27-problem-formulation.md) introduces the central concept. Intelligence cannot pursue algorithmic certainty because *combinatorial explosion* makes brute-force search computationally suicidal — chess alone has roughly 30^60 possible positions. Intelligence is therefore not exhaustive logical processing but the capacity to *zero in on the tiny relevant subset* of information in any situation. This is *relevance realization*: the dynamic, self-organizing process by which cognition selects what matters.

Crucially, most real-world problems are *ill-defined*: initial states, goal states, operators, and path constraints are not given but must be formulated. Newell and Simon trivialized this because of essentialist assumptions — they assumed problem structure was given by the nature of the task. But insight research (the mutilated chessboard experiment) demonstrates that intelligence operates primarily at the level of *problem formulation*, not problem solution. Reformulating a problem (from covering strategy to parity strategy) makes solutions obvious. This is an early articulation of participatory knowing: we do not solve pre-given problems but co-constitute problem spaces through how we frame them.

The No Free Lunch Theorem then delivers a fundamental insight: heuristics (which make us adaptive by enabling tractable search) inevitably introduce bias. Intelligence and vulnerability to self-deception are two sides of the same coin — exactly what the Buddhist diagnosis of parasitic processing described.

**Convergence to relevance realization.** [Episode 28](../episodes/summary-28-convergence-to-relevance-realization.md) builds the convergence argument: multiple independent lines of investigation all point to the same underlying mechanism. Goodman's argument reveals that categorization cannot be explained by logical feature-matching (any two objects share indefinitely many properties) but requires context-sensitive attunement to what matters. Dennett's frame problem shows that determining the consequences of action generates combinatorial explosion — relevance cannot be algorithmically applied. Grice demonstrates that conversational meaning is coordinated relevance-making between minds. Attention, working memory, and problem-solving all depend on relevance realization. The conclusion: relevance realization is not one cognitive function among many but the *self-organizing, recursive process that makes intelligence itself possible*. Meaning — the connections between mind-body, mind-world, mind-mind, mind-itself — can be understood as relations of relevance.

**The sub-representational turn.** [Episode 29](../episodes/summary-29-getting-to-the-depths-of-relevance-realization.md) asks whether relevance can be explained without presupposing it. The answer is no — not at the representational level. Representations are *aspectual*: they select relevant features from the combinatorial explosion and organize them as co-relevant. They presuppose rather than generate relevance. Computation requires selecting relevant inferences. Modularity points to a place but does not explain the process. Relevance operates at a *sub-representational, sub-semantic level* — below concepts, categories, propositions.

Vervaeke introduces *FINSTING* (Fingers of Instantiation): pre-categorical salience-tagging of "hereness and nowness" — the mind's most basic act of putting its finger on something without yet categorizing it. This enactive demonstrative reference (like the word "this") is salience without categorization. It reveals that the most fundamental relationship between mind and world is not representation but *participatory contact*.

This grounds the *asymmetric dependence hierarchy*: propositional knowing depends on procedural knowing, which depends on perspectival knowing, which depends on participatory knowing. Participatory knowing is ontologically primary. This is not a historical claim about the Greeks or Buddhists but a structural claim about cognition itself: the participatory co-constitution of agent and arena is the foundation upon which all other knowing rests.

**Relevance realization as dynamical process.** [Episode 30](../episodes/summary-30-relevance-realization-meets-dynamical-systems-theory.md) resolves how relevance can be scientifically tractable despite having no essence. Nothing is intrinsically or stably relevant — relevance shifts continuously with context, goals, and state. Just as biological fitness has no final design but evolves through natural selection, *cognitive interactional fittedness evolves through ongoing self-organization*. We cannot have a scientific theory of relevance (it lacks the homogeneity, stability, and intrinsic properties required) but we can have a scientific theory of *relevance realization* — the dynamical process that generates it.

The key mechanism is *opponent processing*: two systems pursuing opposed goals but integrated in function, creating a self-organizing dynamic that continuously adapts without predetermined design. Efficiency (compression, exploitation of current location) opposes resiliency (particularization, exploration of new territory). The trade-off between these generates a *virtual engine* — a self-organizing dynamic analogous to biological evolution. The governing norms are *logistical* (efficiency and resiliency in bio-economical resource distribution), not semantic or syntactic — grounding the theory at the sub-propositional level.

**Embodied, embedded, transjective.** [Episode 31](../episodes/summary-31-embodied-embedded-rr-as-dynamical-developmental-gi.md) rejects subject-object dualism in favor of *transjective relationality*: relevance is neither subjectively projected nor objectively detected but a real relation co-created between embodied organism and environment. The body is not Cartesian clay but an autopoietic bio-economy constitutive of cognition. Mind and world are mutually constitutive through dynamic coupling.

The episode then makes a striking identification: relevance realization *is* general intelligence — Spearman's g-factor, the strong positive manifold showing that performance across diverse cognitive domains is systematically correlated. The capacity to zero in on relevant information in diverse domains explains why general intelligence predicts life outcomes better than any other psychological measure. Development and functioning are not separate: systems develop through functioning, generating *complexification* (simultaneous increase in differentiation and integration) and *self-transcendence* (emergence of qualitatively new capacities).

**Neural implementation and consciousness.** [Episode 32](../episodes/summary-32-rr-in-the-brain-insight-and-consciousness.md) grounds the theory in neuroscience. Two scale-invariant neural processes implement relevance realization: *self-organizing criticality* (neurons oscillating between synchronous compression and asynchronous particularization) and *small world networks* (connectivity optimizing the trade-off between efficiency and resiliency). Empirical evidence correlates these patterns with general intelligence, providing the neuroscientific anchor.

Consciousness is then reconceived as *salience landscaping* — the multi-level process by which relevance realization generates perspectival knowing. A four-level process (featurization, foregrounding, figuration, framing) creates dynamically evolving landscapes of salience. Consciousness has ACT properties: *aspectuality* (hereness — things indexed to agent), *centrality* (togetherness — features unified into gestalts), and *temporality* (nowness — timing matters). Consciousness mediates between participatory knowing (the coupling of relevance realization) and procedural knowing (affordance obviation).

Insight is reconceived as structural transformation: not content addition but regular network to small world network conversion via neural avalanche — sudden optimization through structural reorganization. The "aha!" moment is the felt sense of this reorganization. And at the deepest level, relevance realization is fundamentally *affect-laden caring* about resource allocation under finitude — connecting to Heidegger's *Sorge* (care) as constitutive of being-in-the-world.

**The ontological trajectory.** This arc constructs a coherent non-Cartesian ontology of mind with the following commitments:

1. *Participatory knowing is ontologically primary.* The agent-arena relationship is the ground of all cognition and meaning, not internal representation mirroring external world.
2. *Relevance realization is the core of intelligence.* Not computational logic but dynamic attunement to what matters, evolved through self-organizing processes.
3. *Sub-representational, participatory contact precedes categorization.* Enactive demonstrative reference (salience-tagging) precedes and grounds all representational, propositional knowing.
4. *Transjective relationality replaces subject-object dualism.* Mind and world are mutually constitutive through dynamic coupling; relevance exists only in this relationship.
5. *Process ontology replaces substance ontology.* Mind is ongoing self-organization rather than a container of static representations.
6. *Finite constraint is constitutive, not merely limiting.* Bio-economical trade-offs are what generate intelligence, not obstacles to overcome.
7. *Caring and meaning are intrinsic, not added.* Affect-laden resource allocation is fundamental to intelligence; cognition and emotion are unified.

What gets carried forward is the scientific grounding for what the ancient traditions knew experientially: that meaning depends on participatory attunement between knower and known, that self-transformation and world-disclosure are the same process, that wisdom cannot be reduced to propositional knowledge, and that intelligence is inherently self-organizing and self-transcending. The cognitive science of relevance realization provides the ontological foundation for the remaining arcs — the recovery of the sacred, the reverse engineering of enlightenment, and the construction of wisdom — by showing that these are not anti-scientific commitments but implications of how cognition actually works.
