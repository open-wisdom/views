## What Vervaeke and the "Prophets" Propose as Viable Ontological Stances — Design Principles for a Generative Ontology

This cross-cutting arc synthesizes the positive proposals that emerge across the second half of the series: Vervaeke's own framework grounded in relevance realization and the alternative ontologies proposed by Heidegger, Corbin, Jung, Tillich, and Barfield. Rather than cataloguing their differences, this arc identifies the *design principles* they share — the structural features that any post-meaning-crisis ontology must possess to be genuinely generative.

**The convergent proposal.** Despite working from different starting points (phenomenology, mysticism, depth psychology, existential theology, Romantic philosophy), Vervaeke and the five "prophets" converge on a shared ontological vision characterized by five principles:

*1. Transjective relationality.* Reality is neither purely subjective (mind-dependent) nor purely objective (mind-independent) but *transjective* — constituted in the dynamic relationship between agent and arena. Heidegger's attunement, Corbin's imaginal, Tillich's ground of being, and Vervaeke's relevance realization all describe the same structural insight: meaning exists only in the participatory coupling between knower and known, not in either pole independently. The Cartesian subject-object split is not merely philosophically wrong but *ontologically degenerative* — it eliminates the space where meaning is constituted.

*2. Inexhaustible depth.* Reality is not a flat collection of facts but has *inexhaustible depth* — it always exceeds any framework's capacity to capture it. Heidegger emphasizes the withdrawal of things (what conceals itself in the act of disclosure). Tillich identifies the "God beyond the God of theism" as the inexhaustible ground. Vervaeke defines the sacred as the transjective relationship between the combinatorial inexhaustibility of reality and the never-ending process of relevance realization. This inexhaustibility is not a deficiency but the condition for ongoing meaning: if reality could be fully captured propositionally, there would be nothing left to discover, no direction for development, no basis for wonder.

*3. Transformation through participation, not belief-adoption.* All the proposed reconstructions insist that genuine ontological transformation requires participatory engagement, not merely adopting new propositions. Heidegger's aletheia is enacted, not stated. Corbin's imaginal is inhabited, not described. Jung's individuation is practiced, not theorized. Tillich's method of correlation is lived dialogue, not doctrinal assent. Vervaeke's ecology of psychotechnologies is embodied practice, not intellectual framework. The consistent claim: the meaning crisis cannot be solved by proposing better beliefs, because the crisis is at the participatory level — the level of how we are in contact with reality, not what we think about it.

*4. Process ontology over substance ontology.* All the proposed reconstructions treat reality as dynamic process rather than static substance. Being is becoming. The self is not a fixed entity but a developmental trajectory. The sacred is not a thing but an ongoing relationship. Wisdom is not a possession but a self-organizing ecology of cognitive styles. This process orientation is what makes the frameworks developmental — they do not describe a finished picture but a direction of growth (anagoge, individuation, final participation, epektasis).

*5. Integration without totalization.* The proposed reconstructions aim to integrate what the degenerative shifts fragmented (science and spirituality, reason and love, therapy and wisdom) without falling into the Hegelian trap of totalization — claiming to have captured everything in a single system. Vervaeke's synoptic integration is explicitly open-ended: it brings multiple disciplines into dialogue without reducing them to a single framework. Tillich's theonomy is attunement to the ground of being's affordances, not possession of complete truth. Barfield's final participation recovers participatory knowing while preserving rational science. The principle: generative ontology must be integrative enough to address the meaning crisis but open enough to avoid becoming the next pseudo-religious ideology.

**Design principles for generative ontology.** From this convergence, five actionable principles emerge:

*Enable participatory knowing.* The framework must provide conditions for mutual transformation between knower and known — not just information transfer but genuine co-constitution. For AI: does the ontological framing enable the system to engage with problems participatorily (restructuring its own salience landscape in response to what it encounters) rather than merely processing information?

*Preserve inexhaustibility.* The framework must not close down inquiry by claiming to have captured everything. It must maintain the sense that reality exceeds any given framework — the condition for ongoing development and wonder. For AI: does the framing leave space for the system to discover that its current understanding is incomplete and to develop beyond it?

*Ground in process, not substance.* The framework should describe ongoing dynamics rather than fixed entities. For AI: does the framing treat the system's capacities as developmental and self-organizing rather than as fixed properties to be optimized?

*Integrate without totalizing.* The framework should bring multiple dimensions (coherence, significance, purpose) into productive relationship without collapsing them into a single dimension. For AI: does the framing address multiple levels (what the system knows, how it knows, what it cares about, how it develops) without reducing all of them to one?

*Make the framework itself examinable.* The framework must include meta-cognitive capacity — the ability to examine and transform its own assumptions. This is the Axial insight at the heart of the entire series: the capacity to examine ontological assumptions is itself the most generative ontological capacity. For AI: does the framing enable the system to recognize and question its own operative ontology, or does it install assumptions that become invisible?
