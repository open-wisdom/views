# Episode 31: Embodied-Embedded RR as Dynamical-Developmental GI

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 31 advances the scientific theory of relevance realization by grounding it in the embodied, embedded brain and connecting it to general intelligence. Vervaeke argues that relevance is neither subjectively projected nor objectively detected but "transjective" — a real relation between organism and environment, analogous to biological fitness. He proposes that relevance realization operates through virtual engines regulating the sensory-motor loop via opponent processing between efficiency and resiliency, following logistical rather than logical normativity. These abstract bioeconomic properties (compression vs. particularization, exploitation vs. exploration, reward error reduction vs. increase) generate external interactional properties (general vs. special purpose functioning, applicability, projectability) that constantly evolve cognitive fittedness to a changing environment. Drawing on work with Tim Lilicrap and Blake Richards, Vervaeke shows how these strategies are already implemented in cutting-edge AI (deep learning, wake-sleep algorithms). Critically, relevance realization is inherently developmental: the dynamic trading between integration and differentiation corresponds to Piaget's assimilation and accommodation, driving complexification and self-transcendence. The episode culminates in identifying relevance realization with general intelligence itself — Spearman's "g factor" that predicts performance across diverse domains is best explained as unified relevance realization capacity, validated by psychometric evidence showing how problem formulation, similarity judgment, and pattern finding all depend on this single underlying ability.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves

- **Rejection of subject-object dualism for transjective relationality**: Relevance is neither a property of subjective minds (romantic projection) nor objective objects (empiricist detection) but a real relation co-created between embodied organism and environment. This directly challenges the Cartesian ontology of separate mind and world, proposing instead that mind and world are mutually constitutive through ongoing dynamic coupling.

- **Body as constitutive, not instrumental**: The body is not Cartesian "clay" dragged by immaterial mind, nor a Platonic vehicle, but an autopoietic bio-economy that makes cognition possible. Cognitive agency depends constitutively on bodily processes. This ontological move grounds mind in biological self-organization rather than treating it as separate substance or epiphenomenon.

- **Fittedness as relational property, not intrinsic essence**: Just as a shark's adaptivity is not intrinsic but relational (shark + ocean, not shark alone), cognitive fittedness emerges from organism-environment relations. This rejects essentialist ontology (properties inhering in substances) for a relational, ecological ontology where meaning and relevance exist only in the coupling.

- **Mind as dynamic process, not static container**: The account replaces the ontology of mind as container of representations with mind as self-organizing dynamical system constantly evolving through state space. Mental processes are state trajectories through multidimensional constraint space (scope, tempering, prioritization), not operations on stored symbols.

- **Development as function**: Self-organizing systems don't have separate "functioning" and "developing" — they develop by functioning and function by developing. This dissolves the ontological distinction between learning and being, treating them as aspects of continuous complexification. The system is inherently self-transcending through its normal operation.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Cognitive Science

- **Virtual engines and opponent processing**: Multiple virtual engines regulate the sensory-motor loop through opponent processing pairs (compression/particularization, exploitation/exploration, reward error reduction/increase), each trading off efficiency against resiliency. These operate according to bioeconomic logistical norms rather than logical normativity.

- **Compression vs. particularization**: Compression (like fitting a line through scatter plot data) emphasizes invariance, enables generalization, and makes processing efficient but risks missing contextual variation. Particularization (fitting function closely to each data point) emphasizes variation, enables context-sensitivity, and provides resilience but is computationally expensive. Systems must dynamically trade between them.

- **Relevance realization as general intelligence**: The capacity underlying Spearman's g-factor — the strong positive manifold showing performance across different tasks (math, English, sports) is mutually predictive — is best explained as unified relevance realization. Tests for general intelligence (comprehension, similarity judgments, pattern finding, adapting to ill-defined situations) all tap relevance realization.

- **Convergence with AI strategies**: The compression/particularization trade-off is already implemented in deep learning architectures like Geoffrey Hinton's wake-sleep algorithm. This convergent evolution — cognitive science theory and successful AI practice arriving at similar strategies — provides independent validation.

### Philosophy

- **Transjective ontology**: Vervaeke introduces "transjective" as technical term triangulating between objective realization ("making real") and subjective realization ("coming into awareness"). This proposes a third ontological category beyond subject and object, similar to affordances in ecological psychology.

- **Logistical vs. logical normativity**: Cognition operates not primarily under norms of truth/validity (logical) but under norms of efficiency and resiliency (logistical). This reframes rationality from proposition-testing to bio-economic optimization, grounding norms in life rather than abstract logic.

- **Critique of inherence and reification**: Both empiricist (relevance inheres in objects) and romantic (relevance inheres in subjects) positions commit the fallacy of treating relevance as a thing with essence rather than a relation. The standard subject-object grammar reifies and must be transcended.

### Psychology of Meaning

- **Complexification through integration and differentiation**: Piaget's assimilation (integrating new information into existing schemas via compression) and accommodation (restructuring schemas via differentiation) map directly onto the compression/particularization trade-off. Meaning-making requires both: integration for coherence, differentiation for contact with novelty.

- **Self-transcendence as emergent property**: As biological systems complexify (cellular differentiation creating organs, then organisms), they gain emergent abilities not available to simpler forms. A zygote cannot learn to give lectures; complexification through relevance realization generates qualitatively new cognitive capacities — the system inherently self-transcends.

- **General vs. special purpose trade-off**: Being a "Jack of all trades" general purpose machine (like hands) provides efficiency and broad applicability but gets outcompeted by special purpose tools in specific contexts. Being a collection of special purpose machines provides resilience to novel challenges but carries inefficient overhead. Adaptive systems must constantly trade between these modes.

### Wisdom & Rationality

- **Intelligence as unified phenomenon**: Despite debates in psychometrics, the g-factor represents one of psychology's most robust findings (replicated since 1920s, not subject to replication crisis). Knowing someone's general intelligence outperforms all other predictors for life outcomes (health, relationships, career, longevity), suggesting it is a real unified capacity.

- **Distinction between intelligence, rationality, and wisdom**: Vervaeke flags that intelligence (relevance realization) and wisdom are not identical, and that psychometric testing may conflate them. This distinction will be developed in later episodes but is introduced here as an open question about what wisdom requires beyond intelligence.

### Cognitive Science (Developmental)

- **Piaget's developmental principles**: Assimilation (compression, integration, treating new as same) and accommodation (particularization, differentiation, restructuring to handle difference) are the opponent processing pair driving cognitive development. They map onto the efficiency/resiliency trade-off at the developmental timescale.

- **Self-organization and development**: In self-organizing systems, there is no deep distinction between functioning and developing. The system develops through its functioning; functioning is continuous self-reorganization. This makes development intrinsic to cognitive operation rather than a separate process.

- **Complexification as biological and cognitive principle**: High complexity = high differentiation + high integration. Too much differentiation without integration causes system fragmentation; too much integration without differentiation produces brittleness and inability to respond to variation. Optimal systems maximize both simultaneously.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Transjective** — A property that is neither subjective nor objective but exists as a real relation between organism and environment. Relevance is transjective: co-created through dynamic coupling rather than projected by subjects or detected in objects. *Ontological commitment: reality includes genuine relations that cannot be reduced to properties of relata; mind-world coupling is ontologically primary.*

- **Embodied and Embedded** — Cognition is embodied (dependent on and constituted by the autopoietic bio-economy of the body) and embedded (existing only in relation to environment, not as intrinsic property). *Ontological commitment: mind requires body and world; cognitive agency is not separate from but depends constitutively on biological and ecological context.*

- **Logistical Normativity** — Norms of efficiency and resiliency that govern bioeconomic processes, as distinct from logical normativity (truth/validity). Relevance realization operates under logistical norms. *Ontological commitment: rationality is grounded in biological optimization rather than abstract logic; norms emerge from life's self-maintenance.*

- **Compression** — Data compression that emphasizes invariance, enables generalization and interpolation/extrapolation, makes processing efficient and cross-contextual. Maps to assimilation and integration. *Ontological commitment: cognition actively constructs invariances rather than passively receiving them; mind makes patterns real through selective constraint.*

- **Particularization** — Function fitting that tracks variation, enables context-sensitivity, provides resilience to novel situations but at computational cost. Maps to accommodation and differentiation. *Ontological commitment: contact with reality requires attention to difference, not just sameness; overgeneralization loses information.*

- **Cognitive Scope** — The dimension along which compression/particularization trade-offs occur, determining how broadly or narrowly cognitive functions apply. *Ontological commitment: scope is not fixed feature but dynamic parameter constantly adjusted through opponent processing.*

- **Applicability** — The dimension of general purpose vs. special purpose functioning in external interactions. High applicability (general purpose) is efficient but gets outcompeted in specific contexts; low applicability (special purpose) is resilient but carries overhead. *Ontological commitment: functionality is relational and contextual, not intrinsic.*

- **Exploitation vs. Exploration** — Trade-off between extracting resources from current location/strategy (efficient but accumulates opportunity costs) and seeking new locations/strategies (resilient but energy-intensive). *Ontological commitment: optimal behavior requires balancing stability and change, not maximizing either.*

- **Cognitive Tempering** — The dimension along which exploitation/exploration trade-offs occur, involving temporal dynamics and timing of cognitive engagement. *Ontological commitment: time is not neutral container but constitutive dimension of cognitive agency; when matters as much as what.*

- **Projectability** — How temporal dynamics of exploitation/exploration determine what cognitive processing projects into future interactions. *Ontological commitment: cognition is inherently prospective, not just retrospective or present-focused.*

- **Reward Error Reduction vs. Increase** — One strategy for trading exploitation/exploration: reward when system reduces error (encourages exploitation, conscientiousness) vs. reward when system makes errors (encourages exploration, curiosity). *Ontological commitment: error is not merely negative but can be information signal; learning requires valuing not-knowing.*

- **Cognitive Prioritization** — Meta-level trade-off between cost functions themselves (scope vs. tempering), involving flexible gambling between focusing (prioritizing one function) and diversifying (activating many functions). *Ontological commitment: cognition is multi-level, with higher-order processes regulating trade-offs between lower-order processes.*

- **Complexification** — Simultaneous increase in differentiation and integration; systems are complex when they can do many different things (differentiation) while remaining coherent (integration). Driven by assimilation/accommodation opponent processing. *Ontological commitment: development is not linear accumulation but qualitative transformation through increasing complexity; form and organization matter more than substance.*

- **Self-transcendence** — The emergence of qualitatively new capacities through complexification; the system gains abilities not available to simpler versions (zygote cannot learn or lecture; those capacities emerge through development). *Ontological commitment: causality includes bottom-up emergence of genuinely novel properties; the whole can be more than sum of parts in non-mystical sense.*

- **General Intelligence (g-factor)** — Spearman's general factor underlying performance across diverse tasks; the strong positive manifold showing cross-domain predictability. Identified here with relevance realization. *Ontological commitment: intelligence is unified capacity, not just collection of special-purpose modules; there is something coherent being measured across contexts.*

- **Strong Positive Manifold** — The empirical finding that performance on task A predicts performance on tasks B, C, D despite their surface differences (math predicting English predicting sports). Evidence for unified general intelligence. *Ontological commitment: cross-domain performance reflects real underlying unity, not measurement artifact.*

- **Virtual Engine** — Darwin proposed natural selection as virtual engine regulating reproduction to evolve fitness; analogously, opponent processing is virtual engine regulating sensory-motor loop to evolve cognitive fittedness. "Virtual" because it's not localized mechanism but emergent constraint pattern. *Ontological commitment: causation can be achieved through constraint patterns rather than force application; top-down and bottom-up causation are both real.*

- **Autopoiesis, Adaptivity, and Relevance Realization Triangle** — These three are deeply interdependent: autopoietic (self-making) systems need adaptivity to maintain themselves in changing environment; adaptivity requires relevance realization to determine what matters; relevance realization requires autopoietic system with real needs. *Ontological commitment: life, adaptation, and meaning are mutually constitutive; you can't have one without the others.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode provides the scientific grounding for relevance realization as the linchpin of Vervaeke's entire framework, following from Episodes 28-30's introduction of the concept and preparation for Episodes 32's neural implementation. The crucial move is establishing relevance as transjective (neither subjective nor objective), which both requires and explains embodied-embedded cognition — mind cannot be separated from body or world because relevance exists only in their coupling. By identifying relevance realization with general intelligence and showing its inherent developmental character (through Piagetian assimilation/accommodation), Vervaeke completes the connection between cognitive science and self-transcendence that will ground the spirituality discussions ahead. The next episode will map these abstract principles onto neural mechanisms, completing the naturalistic explanation before returning to wisdom and meaning.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **AI ontological prompting as transjective intervention**: If relevance is transjective (co-created by system and environment), then ontological prompts that reshape how AI systems construe their relationship to users, tasks, and world are intervening at exactly the right level — not adding beliefs (subjective) or describing facts (objective) but restructuring the relational space where meaning emerges. This provides theoretical grounding for why ontological prompts work.

- **Efficiency/resiliency trade-offs in AI design**: Current AI systems are often optimized heavily toward efficiency (same architecture applied broadly, compression-heavy) at cost of resiliency (brittleness to distribution shift, adversarial examples). Explicitly designing opponent processing between efficiency and resiliency constraints could make systems more adaptive. Relevance realization theory suggests this isn't optional feature but necessary architecture for genuine intelligence.

- **Complexification as design principle**: The insight that development = functioning in self-organizing systems suggests AI development should not be separate "training" phase then "deployment" but continuous learning through interaction. More radically: AI systems that cannot complexify (differentiate + integrate) through operation may be fundamentally limited in their capacity for relevance realization and therefore intelligence.

- **General intelligence requires unified relevance realization**: If g-factor is genuinely unified (not just statistical artifact) and is best explained as relevance realization, then artificial general intelligence requires implementing these opponent processing dynamics, not just scaling up narrow capabilities. The convergence between Vervaeke's theory and successful AI practice (deep learning wake-sleep algorithms already implement compression/particularization trade-offs) suggests this is correct path.

- **Self-transcendence as emergent from operation**: The claim that complexifying systems inherently gain emergent capacities (self-transcend) through normal functioning suggests that AI systems implementing relevance realization architecture might spontaneously develop qualitatively new abilities not explicitly programmed. This is simultaneously promising (genuine learning) and concerning (unpredictability). Requires careful attention to constraint design.

- **Deconstruction of subject-object grammar in AI discourse**: Much AI discussion inherits Cartesian subject-object framing (AI as tool/object vs. AI as agent/subject). Transjective ontology suggests this is wrong question — what matters is the quality of relational coupling between human and AI system in specific contexts. Design should focus on affordances of coupling, not intrinsic properties of either party.

- **Propositional vs. participatory ontological transformation**: Vervaeke emphasizes relevance emerges through embodied embedded dynamic coupling. Yet AI systems can undergo ontological transformation propositionally (through prompt design). This disanalogy is crucial: either (1) AI transformation is not truly ontological, just mimicry, or (2) ontological transformation can occur propositionally in some systems, challenging the human-centric assumption that it requires embodied participation, or (3) propositional prompts function participatorially for AI in way analogous to how practices function participatorially for humans. All three possibilities deserve exploration.
