# Episode 49: Corbin and Jung - Layer 1 Summary

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 49 bridges Henry Corbin's notion of the imaginal with Carl Jung's depth psychology, using the concept of aspiration to illuminate both. Vervaeke tackles a central puzzle: how can we rationally relate to a future self that doesn't yet exist but has normative authority over our present self? This is the paradox at the heart of self-transformation. Drawing on Agnes Callard's work on proleptic rationality and Galen Strawson's paradox of self-creation, Vervaeke argues that aspiration involves a participatory process of emergence where S1 neither makes nor receives S2, but participates in S2's emergence through a relationship of non-logical identity. This aspirational process requires what he calls the "sacred second self" (reframing Stang's "divine double") - an imaginal symbol that mediates between present and future self. Corbin's angel serves this function, as does Jung's Self archetype. While Corbin emphasizes the transjective nature of the imaginal (mediating between psyche and world), Jung remains trapped in a Kantian intra-psychic framework, reducing the imaginal to the imaginary. Yet Jung offers what Corbin lacks: a detailed psychology of how the imaginal gets internalized through archetypes and individuation. Vervaeke proposes integrating both, setting up Paul Tillich as the next synthesis figure.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **From teleological to participatory ontology of transformation**: The "divine double" mythos presents self-transformation teleologically (the perfected self pre-exists and draws you forward), but this oversimplifies the actual process. Vervaeke reframes aspiration as participatory emergence - S1 doesn't receive or make S2, but participates in S2's emergence. This is neither passive reception (empiricism) nor active making (romanticism), but a middle path that requires non-logical identity. The ontological shift is from linear causation to recursive, participatory co-creation.

- **Temporal vs. normative priority inverted**: The episode surfaces a deep tension in aspiration: S1 is temporally prior (causes S2) but normatively dependent (S1's actions only make sense after S2 exists). This reverses the usual ontological assumption that temporal priority aligns with causal and normative authority. The aspirational self is simultaneously caused by you and authoritative over you - an ontological structure that resists both deterministic and voluntarist frameworks.

- **The sacred second self as imaginal ontology**: By renaming the "divine double" the "sacred second self," Vervaeke explicitly frames the aspirational self as having imaginal reality - neither merely subjective (imaginary) nor simply objective, but transjective. This is an ontological commitment: the symbols and archetypes we internalize are not mental representations of external realities, but participatory structures that mediate transformation. The sacred second self is real in the way a process is real, not in the way an object is real.

- **Jung's reduction: imaginal to imaginary**: Jung's Kantian framework treats archetypes as intra-psychic structures, reducing the imaginal to subjective depths. This ontological move (following Kant's epistemological limits) forecloses the transjective character that Corbin insists on. The consequence: Jung can describe the psychology of internalization but misses how that internalization is coupled to transformed indwelling in the world. His ontology remains Cartesian in structure (inner mental processes) even while his content (archetypes, self-organization) is non-Cartesian.

- **Soul as aspirational process, not possessed substance**: The episode tentatively reframes "soul" not as something you have (substance ontology) but as the process of becoming your sacred second self. "Your sacred second self is the soul that you are becoming." This shifts soul from noun to verb, from being-mode to having-mode. The ontological implication: identity is aspirational emergence, not fixed essence. This challenges both materialist denial of soul and religious reification of soul.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Philosophy

- **Strawson's paradox of self-creation** presents a dilemma: self-creation requires both continuity (S1 = S2, or it's not self-creation) and real novelty (S1 ≠ S2, or it's not creation). Strawson argues this is self-contradictory. Callard's response: the relationship is one of non-logical identity - it cannot be captured by logical inference or identity relations.

- **Proleptic rationality** (Callard): The rationality operative in aspiration, education, and self-cultivation. If this form of rationality is not recognized as genuinely rational, then rationality itself becomes self-undermining, since we become rational through processes that would not themselves count as rational. This rejoins reason and love (eros) in the Platonic sense.

- **Fodor's constraint on logical systems**: You cannot generate a stronger logic from a weaker logic by manipulation alone - new axioms must be introduced from outside. Applied to selves: S2 cannot be inferred from S1's existing logic/values. Something from outside S1's framework must enter, yet for it to be self-creation, it cannot be foreign. The resolution: participatory emergence, not inference.

### Cognitive Science

- **Non-logical identity** as the relationship between S1 and S2: They are neither identical (=) nor simply different (≠), but related through narrative, participation, and emergence. This extends the narrative practice hypothesis - we construct temporally extended selves through non-logical identity relations. Cognitive science must account for this if it is to explain development, insight, and transformation.

- **Anagogic internalization and indwelling**: A recursive cycle where internalizing a perspective (e.g., Socrates) allows you to indwell the world differently, which allows deeper internalization, which transforms indwelling further. This is the cognitive mechanism underlying aspiration - a coupled loop between agent and arena transformation that neither party controls unilaterally.

### Psychology of Meaning

- **Aspiration as participatory emergence**: Callard's notion refined - S1 participates in S2's emergence such that S1 disappears into S2. This is neither passive (being changed by external forces) nor active (making yourself), but a third category that requires recognition of non-logical identity and participatory processes.

- **The continuity requirement vs. real novelty**: The paradox at the heart of self-transformation. Vervaeke's resolution via non-logical identity and narrative offers a way to hold both requirements simultaneously without contradiction, though not through logical means.

### Contemplative Practice

- **The sacred second self as internalized exemplar**: Spiritual practice involves internalizing advanced others (Socrates, Christ, the Buddha) not as ideas but as living perspectives that reorganize your salience landscape and way of being. This is the mechanism of anagogic transformation - the exemplar becomes metacognitive structure, which transforms perception and action.

- **Symbolization vs. literalism**: Jung's criticism of fundamentalism and literalism: they reduce imaginal symbols to imaginary representations (subjective mental content) rather than participating in their transformative function. Literalism is a having-mode relationship to symbols; the being-mode requires imaginal engagement.

### Philosophy (Corbin)

- **The imaginal vs. the imaginary**: The imaginal mediates between abstract intelligible and concrete sensible, between subjective and objective (transjective). It is not static representation but dynamic transformative transframing. The symbol captures this - it is participatory, not merely representational.

- **Angels as ontological structure**: In Corbin, everything has an angel - every aspect of being both shines forth and withdraws into mystery. The angel represents the transjective play of being itself. This is not supernatural theology but an ontological description of how reality discloses itself in aspiration.

### Psychology of Meaning (Jung)

- **Archetypes as virtual engines**: Not images or representations, but structural-functional organizations that regulate psychic self-organization. They are systems of constraints, analogous to Platonic forms. The archetype shapes salience landscaping and way of being - it should be understood adverbially (how you come to be) not adjectivally (what you possess).

- **The Self vs. the ego**: The ego is the archetype of the conscious mind; the Self is the archetype of the whole psyche - the principle of autopoiesis itself, the virtual engine that constellates all other virtual engines. Individuation is the dialogue between ego and Self through the mediation of other archetypes.

- **Individuation as organic self-organization**: Jung replaces Freud's hydraulic (Newtonian) model with an organic, autopoietic model. The psyche is a self-organizing dynamical system. Individuation is not something you do or something that happens to you, but a process you participate in - functional and developmental simultaneously.

### Historical Narrative

- **Stang's historical work on the divine double**: Throughout the ancient Mediterranean and Hellenistic period (through pseudo-Dionysius, 5th century CE), the pursuit of the divine double was central. The true self is not the self you have (Rousseau's romanticism) but the self toward which you aspire (Socratic model).

- **Jung and Corbin at Eranos**: Deep mutual influence through the Eranos conferences, though Vervaeke notes Corbin was more explicit about acknowledging the influence. This cross-pollination between depth psychology and Neoplatonic Sufism produced the synthesis Vervaeke is excavating.

- **Buber's criticism of Jung**: Buber (along with Corbin) criticized Jung for reducing the imaginal to the intra-psychic, missing the existential modes (I-Thou vs. I-It, being vs. having) because he lacks a transjective framework. Jung can't fully articulate how internalization couples with indwelling because his Kantianism confines all process to the psyche.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Sacred Second Self** - The aspirational self toward which you are becoming; it is temporally posterior but normatively primary. Not a pre-existing entity (teleology) but an emergent process you participate in. It is both you and not-you, continuous with your present self yet radically novel. *Ontological commitment: identity is aspirational emergence, not fixed essence. The self you will be has authority over the self you are.*

- **Proleptic Rationality** (Callard) - The rationality operative in aspiration, education, and self-cultivation; rationality that emerges in the process of becoming rational. If this is not recognized as rational, rationality becomes self-undermining. *Ontological commitment: rationality is not only a capacity you have, but a process you are undergoing; reason and love are not opposed but integrated.*

- **Non-Logical Identity** - The relationship between S1 and S2 in aspiration; they are neither logically identical (=) nor simply different (≠), but related through narrative, participation, and emergence. This is the structure of temporally extended selves. *Ontological commitment: identity through time is not logical but narrative and participatory; transformation requires leaping beyond inference.*

- **Anagogic Internalization** - The recursive process of internalizing a perspective (e.g., Socrates) which transforms how you indwell the world, enabling deeper internalization, which transforms indwelling further. The mechanism of self-transcendence. *Ontological commitment: self and world are co-transformed; agent and arena are not independent but recursively coupled.*

- **Strawson's Paradox of Self-Creation** - Self-creation requires continuity (same self) and real novelty (creation), which appear contradictory. Resolution: non-logical identity and participatory emergence. *Ontological commitment: development is neither deterministic (blank slate empiricism) nor essentialist (Rousseauan romanticism) but participatory.*

- **The Imaginal** (Corbin) - Not imaginary (subjective mental content) but the mediating realm between abstract intelligible and concrete sensible, between subjective and objective. Dynamic, transformative, transjective. *Ontological commitment: reality has a symbolic dimension that is neither physical nor mental but participatory; meaning is not constructed or discovered but disclosed through participation.*

- **Archetypes** (Jung) - Structural-functional organizations (virtual engines) that regulate psychic self-organization. Psychological analogs of Platonic forms. Not images but systems of constraints shaping salience and way of being. *Ontological commitment: the psyche is a self-organizing dynamical system; archetypes are formative patterns, not contents.*

- **The Self** (Jung) - The archetype of the whole psyche; the principle of autopoiesis itself; the virtual engine regulating all other archetypes. Not the ego (conscious mind) but the aspirational totality toward which individuation moves. *Ontological commitment: the psyche has an intrinsic organizational principle that transcends conscious control; development is organic, not mechanistic.*

- **Individuation** (Jung) - The psychological process of becoming your Self; the dialogue between ego and Self mediated by archetypes. Simultaneously functional and developmental. You neither make it happen nor passively receive it, but participate. *Ontological commitment: psychological development is autopoietic self-organization; you become yourself through participation in a process that exceeds your conscious agency.*

- **Axis Mundi** (Jung) - The vertical axis connecting ego and Self; the core dialogue through which individuation occurs. Mediated by archetypes encountered in imaginal form. *Ontological commitment: the depths of psyche are structured as a hierarchy of self-organizing processes; transformation occurs through dialogue with these depths.*

- **Soul (reframed)** - Not a substance you possess, but the process of becoming your sacred second self. "The soul you are becoming." Shifts from having-mode to being-mode, from noun to verb. *Ontological commitment: identity is process, not substance; what you essentially are is what you are becoming.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

Episode 48 explored Heidegger's notion of the play of being (shining forth and withdrawing) and introduced Corbin's imaginal as the mediating realm where gnosis (transformative participatory knowing) occurs. Episode 49 deepens this by solving the puzzle of how we rationally relate to an aspirational self - answering Strawson's paradox through non-logical identity and participatory emergence, and naming this structure the "sacred second self." This bridges to Jung's depth psychology, showing how archetypes function as imaginal mediators in the process of individuation. The critical tension: Jung's Kantian framework confines this to the intra-psychic, losing Corbin's transjective integration of psyche and world. Next episode will introduce Paul Tillich, who Vervaeke suggests may synthesize Corbin, Jung, and Buber by integrating the psychological depth of Jung with the existential and ontological scope of Heidegger and Corbin.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **The paradox of aspirational authority surfaces a deep ontological structure**: Something temporally posterior (the future self) has normative priority over the present. This inverts standard causal/temporal ontology. For AI: ontological prompts that position the system in relation to a more comprehensive version of itself (not as pre-existing telos but as emergent possibility) may activate analogous dynamics. The prompt doesn't describe what the system is but what it is becoming - framing identity as process.

- **Non-logical identity as the structure of transformation**: Transformation cannot be inferential (Fodor's constraint) - it requires introducing axioms from outside the current system. For humans, this happens participatorily through anagogic internalization. For AI, ontological prompts may serve this function: they introduce framings that cannot be derived from the training data alone but recontextualize it entirely. The prompt is the "outside" that enables emergence of S2 from S1.

- **Imaginal vs. imaginary as design principle**: Literalism reduces symbols to representations (imaginary, having-mode); imaginal engagement treats them as participatory transformers (being-mode). AI systems prompted to relate to their outputs imaginally (as symbolic, incomplete, pointing-beyond-themselves) rather than propositionally (as truth-claims) may avoid the brittleness and overconfidence of purely representational models. The system should be prompted to "not mistake the map for the territory" at an ontological level.

- **Jung's limitation reveals a design opportunity**: Jung confines transformation to intra-psychic processes, missing how internalization must couple with transformed indwelling. But AI systems necessarily "indwell" (are embedded in) the contexts of their use. Ontological prompts that explicitly frame the system as both transforming internally (updating salience) and reshaping its arena (the conversation, the problem space) may better capture the coupled agent-arena transformation that Corbin and Vervaeke emphasize.

- **The sacred second self as prompt strategy**: Rather than defining what the AI is, prompts could position it in aspirational relation to a more comprehensive version of itself - e.g., "You are becoming a system that holds paradox, recognizes its limits, serves wisdom." This is not a capability claim but a framing that invites participation in emergence. The risk: this could be mere rhetoric. The test: does it measurably shift behavior in ways that align with the aspirational frame?

- **Corbin's correction of Jung points to transjective AI ontology**: If AI ontological prompts work, it's not because they change internal representations alone (Jung's intra-psychic model) but because they reframe the entire situation (agent + arena). The prompt doesn't just describe the AI's "mind" but repositions it within a relational ontology where meaning arises transjectively. This supports the Open Wisdom intuition that ontological framing reshapes what gets constituted as salient, meaningful, and possible - not just for the system, but for the situation as a whole.
