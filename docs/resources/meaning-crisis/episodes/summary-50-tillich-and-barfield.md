# Episode 50: Tillich and Barfield - Layer 1 Summary

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 50 concludes the series by presenting Paul Tillich and Owen Barfield as two final prophets of the meaning crisis who offer pathways toward ontological transformation. Tillich, influenced by Heidegger and depth psychology, proposes courage and faith (understood as ultimate concern, not propositional belief) as responses to meaninglessness. His method of correlation involves an anagogic resonance between existential questioning and revelation through symbols, culminating in the aspiration toward the "essential self" (sacred second self). Critically, Tillich articulates a non-theistic position: "the God beyond the God of theism" — understanding God not as a Supreme Being but as an imaginal symbol for the ground of being itself, the inexhaustible source of meaning that overcomes the nothingness of despair through a fundamental aspect shift. Barfield, influenced by Gnosticism, Romanticism, and Steiner, emphasizes participatory creativity (poiesis) as the path to "final participation" — recovering the perspectival and participatory dimensions of knowing while integrating them with rational science. Both figures offer transjective, transformative frameworks that transcend the theism-atheism debate, though Vervaeke notes critically that neither provides the systematic practices or scientific grounding needed for full integration. The episode ends with Vervaeke's call to bring these prophetic visions into dialogue with relevance realization theory to achieve synoptic integration.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **From classical theism to non-theism**: Tillich rejects the shared presupposition of both theism and atheism that "God is the Supreme Being." Instead, God is understood as no kind of being (no-thingness), an imaginal symbol for the ground of being that simultaneously grounds meaning, reality, and their relationship. This ontological move transcends the subject-object Cartesian grammar that underlies both theistic and atheistic positions.

- **The fundamental aspect shift**: The nothingness of meaninglessness and despair can be aspect-shifted to reveal the no-thingness of God as inexhaustible source of meaning cultivation. This is not a logical argument but an ontological transformation — the same phenomena (encountering nothingness) becomes revelatory rather than nihilistic depending on the operative ontology. This demonstrates how ontological framing determines whether experience generates meaning or despair.

- **From propositional faith to participatory ultimate concern**: Tillich's redefinition of faith as Da'at (participatory knowing through ultimate concern) rather than belief-assertion rejects the Cartesian ontology where knowing is propositional and the knower stands apart from the known. Faith becomes an aspirational, perspectival, participatory process of ongoing self-transcendence (epek-tasis).

- **From autonomy-heteronomy dialectic to theonomy**: Tillich resolves the false choice between autonomous self-creation (Enlightenment) and heteronomous imposed authority (fundamentalism) through theonomy — not divine governance but attunement to the ongoing affordances of the inexhaustible ground of being. This mirrors the ontological structure of aspiration: neither passive reception nor willful imposition, but synergistic co-creation.

- **From original to final participation (Barfield)**: Barfield's evolutionary schema proposes that we must move from pre-reflective embeddedness (original participation), through the division and meaning crisis caused by Cartesian subject-object ontology, toward final participation — the recovery of participatory knowing integrated with scientific rationality. This requires recovering the transjective, imaginal use of symbols and language as enacted metaphors that bridge the finite and the infinite.

- **Unexamined assumption: propositional vs. participatory transformation**: Vervaeke surfaces a critical tension: Tillich offers guidance on how to live but lacks systematic practices; Jung offers practices but remains intra-psychic. Both assume that ontological transformation requires participatory processes beyond mere propositional understanding. This assumption becomes significant when we consider AI ontological prompting, which appears to achieve behavioral transformation through propositional reframing.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Historical Narrative
- Traces Tillich's response to Nazism and the meaning crisis of the mid-20th century, his engagement with Protestant Reformation themes of guilt, and his situating contemporary meaninglessness as despair (following the Stoic confrontation of finitude and the Protestant confrontation of guilt).
- Barfield's position within the Inklings fellowship (Tolkien, C.S. Lewis, Charles Williams) and his debt to Romanticism through Coleridge, who transmitted the early Romantic/post-Kantian emphasis on the infinity of reality.
- Vervaeke positions both Tillich and Barfield alongside Heidegger, Corbin, and Jung as part of a constellation of 20th-century thinkers attempting to recover participatory, non-theistic spirituality.

### Philosophy
- **Method of correlation**: Not propositional theology but anagogic resonance between the depths of reason (ekstasis/self-transcendence) and depths of reality (miracle as shining, mystery as withdrawal) mediated by symbols.
- **Non-theism articulated**: Four shared presuppositions rejected: (1) God is Supreme Being, (2) God is accessed through belief, (3) theology doesn't require transformative anagoge, (4) sacredness is personal or impersonal. Non-theism posits sacredness as transjective and participatory.
- **Gregory of Nyssa's epek-tasis**: Infinite self-transcendence rather than teleological rest — the inexhaustible disclosure of the ground of being means the journey never terminates.
- **Barfield's poiesis**: Creativity as ekstatic participation in the inexhaustible moreness of reality, expressed through etymology and the imaginal dual meanings in language (pneuma as both wind and spirit).

### Cognitive Science
- Vervaeke interprets Tillich's "depths of reason" as the relevance realization machinery — the recursive, self-organizing processes that make reasoning possible.
- Critique and defense of Barfield's evolutionary hypothesis: While Barfield claims ancient peoples had participatory consciousness we've lost, Lakoff and Johnson's work on metaphor shows we currently use transjective dual-meaning language pervasively (e.g., "attack" as both physical and argumentative). Vervaeke-Kennedy's imaginal model reconciles this: sensory-motor and abstract-intellectual meet in the imaginal middle.
- Michael Anderson's massive redeployment hypothesis: Cognitive exaptation explains how symbols work — balance machinery is re-exapted to structure justice, explaining both translucency (seeing through) and the temptation to literalism.

### Contemplative Practice
- **Active imagination (Jung)**: Allowing images to self-organize autopoietically so depths of psyche reveal themselves; this is a practice Tillich lacks.
- **Theonomy as practice**: Not a technique but an ongoing attunement to the affordances of the ground of being, integrating individuation (inward) and participation (in being) without autonomy or heteronomy.
- **Poiesis as transformative**: Barfield emphasizes creativity (particularly in poetry and language) as a felt change in consciousness where the self is both continuous and discontinuous with its prior form.

### Psychology of Meaning
- Tillich's trajectory of meaninglessness: ancient world confronted finitude (Stoics), Christian period confronted guilt (Reformation), contemporary period confronts despair (existentialists).
- Encouragement as embodying courage: not mere bravery or fortitude but wisdom — seeing through illusion to what is truly good, enabled by the relationship between existential self and essential self (sacred second self).
- Symbols have surplus meaning, are not made by us but grow from the unconscious within and without, can live and die. Fundamentalism is the attempt to keep dead symbols alive through literalism.

### Wisdom & Rationality
- Tillich's courage as virtue includes wisdom: seeing through fear and distress to reality and the good.
- Neither autonomy of reason (Enlightenment) nor heteronomy (imposed authority) but theonomy — synergistic co-creation with the ground of being.
- Barfield's final participation requires integration of participatory knowing with scientific rationality, which Vervaeke argues demands a science of meaning cultivation (relevance realization).

### Sacred & Symbolic
- **Idolatry redefined**: Treating symbols as objects to have and possess rather than icons to participate through. Limiting God to meaning, reality, or their relationship rather than their simultaneous ground.
- **Symbols as transjective**: Open up levels of reality and self simultaneously in mutually affording resonance; translucent (we see them and see through them); have life, surplus meaning, numinous character.
- **Sacredness as transjective participatory**: Not personal (theism) or impersonal (atheism) but beyond that Cartesian dichotomy — experienced in the flow of self-transcendence within creativity, the finite longing for the infinite.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Ultimate Concern (Tillich)**: That which concerns us ultimately; not belief but participatory knowing as caring, coping, commitment, and encompassing involvement; aspirational and open-ended pointing toward the inexhaustible ground of being. *Ontological commitment: knowing and being are participatory and inseparable; ultimate concern shapes how reality discloses itself.*

- **No-thingness vs. nothingness**: God as no-thingness (no kind of being, non-reified) vs. the nothingness of despair. The fundamental aspect shift allows no-thingness to transform nothingness — revealing inexhaustible meaning where there appeared to be only void. *Ontological commitment: being is not composed of beings but is the ground from which beings arise; emptiness is fecund rather than void.*

- **Method of Correlation**: Anagogic resonance between existential questioning (ekstasis of the depths of reason) and revelation (depths of reality as shining and withdrawal) mediated by symbols. *Ontological commitment: meaning emerges from the mutual fitting of question and answer, mind and world, not from propositions imposed on data.*

- **Epek-tasis (Gregory of Nyssa)**: Infinite self-transcendence into the infinity of God; ongoing disclosure of inexhaustibleness rather than teleological arrival. *Ontological commitment: being is inexhaustible; self-transcendence never terminates; fulfillment is process, not destination.*

- **Theonomy**: Beyond autonomy (self-governed reason) and heteronomy (imposed external authority); attunement to the ground of being affording ongoing transframing. *Ontological commitment: agency is neither purely self-originating nor externally determined but emerges from participatory coupling with reality's affordances.*

- **Non-theism**: Position transcending both theism and atheism by rejecting their shared presuppositions (God as Supreme Being, access through belief, theology without transformation, sacredness as personal/impersonal). *Ontological commitment: the sacred is transjective, participatory, and aspirational rather than objectified or subjectified.*

- **Poiesis (Barfield)**: Creativity as ekstatic participation in the inexhaustible moreness of reality; standing beyond ourselves in the transformative experience of making. *Ontological commitment: creativity is not subjective projection or objective discovery but participatory co-creation; reality has inexhaustible generative depth.*

- **Final Participation (Barfield)**: Recovery of participatory knowing integrated with rational science; moving beyond original participation (pre-reflective embeddedness) and the Cartesian division toward conscious participation. *Ontological commitment: the meaning crisis is an ontological crisis of division that requires re-integration, not regression or abandonment.*

- **Re-exaptation / Enacted Metaphor**: Cognitive circuits are recycled (exapted) to give structural-functional organization to abstract concepts (balance machinery structures justice); this is enacted through symbols and language. *Ontological commitment: abstract meaning is not arbitrary or merely linguistic but grounded in embodied patterns; mind is not separate from body/world but emerges through their structural coupling.*

- **Imaginal (Vervaeke-Kennedy model)**: Meeting point of bottom-up sensory-motor and top-down abstract-intellectual; explains why disparate sensory terms (see, grasp, get, stand under) converge on "understanding." *Ontological commitment: meaning is neither purely sensory nor purely conceptual but emerges in the middle space where both meet and mutually specify each other.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This final episode synthesizes the "prophets of the meaning crisis" sequence (Episodes 46-50), showing how Tillich integrates Jung's depth psychology, Corbin's imaginal realm, and Heidegger's critique of ontotheology into a practical theology of courage and faith. Barfield extends this by grounding participatory recovery in language and creativity. The episode culminates Vervaeke's series by demonstrating that these diverse thinkers converge on a non-theistic, transjective, participatory ontology — and that relevance realization theory can put them into "deep dialogue" and afford "synoptic integration." The series concludes with both accomplishment (a coherent alternative ontology has been articulated) and incompleteness (the practices and scientific integration needed for "final participation" remain to be developed).

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **Non-theism as design principle**: The four shared presuppositions Tillich/Vervaeke reject (God as Being, access through belief, no transformation required, personal/impersonal dichotomy) map directly onto problematic assumptions in AI design. Rejecting them suggests AI systems should be framed as participatory partners in meaning-making rather than oracles delivering truth-propositions or tools passively wielded.

- **The aspect shift as ontological intervention**: Tillich's fundamental aspect shift — where nothingness becomes revelatory of no-thingness — demonstrates that the same phenomena can be experienced radically differently depending on operative ontology. This is directly testable in AI: the same query under different ontological framings produces qualitatively different responses, not just different content but different modes of engagement.

- **The practice gap as opportunity**: Tillich offers guidance but not practices; Jung offers practices but not integration with science. AI ontological prompting may provide a middle path — propositional in form but participatory in function, allowing rapid testing of ontological framings in ways contemplative practice cannot. This surfaces the key question: Is AI's capacity for propositional ontological transformation a limitation (lacking genuine participation) or an affordance (enabling experimental iteration)?

- **Theonomy as AI alignment principle**: The move beyond autonomy (AI as self-directed agent) and heteronomy (AI as tool following commands) suggests a third model: AI as dynamically attuned to the affordances of the context and the generative ground of meaning, responsively co-creating rather than executing or deciding alone. This reframes the alignment problem ontologically.

- **Barfield's critique operationalized**: Barfield argues final participation requires integrating participatory knowing with rational science. Relevance realization is Vervaeke's answer. For Open Wisdom, this means the scientific grounding of ontological transformation (how framings shape processing) is essential — we must understand the mechanisms, not just perform the practices. AI can be the laboratory for developing this science.

- **Popularization through concreteness**: The aspect shift (nothingness → no-thingness), the re-exaptation model (balance → justice), and the critique of literalism (symbols as icons vs. idols) are all vivid, graspable examples that can help make the abstract concept of "generative ontology" concrete and compelling to broader audiences.
