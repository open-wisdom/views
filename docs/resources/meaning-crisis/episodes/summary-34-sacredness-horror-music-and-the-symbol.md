# Episode 34: Sacredness: Horror, Music, and the Symbol

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KoqibFwvQJ4) · [Read transcript](https://www.meaningcrisis.co/ep-34-awakening-from-the-meaning-crisis-sacredness-horror-music-and-the-symbol/)

### 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 34 completes Vervaeke's account of sacredness by introducing the concept of the numinous alongside the worldview attunement model from Episode 33. While worldview attunement homes us against horror through meta-meaning systems, Otto's concept of the numinous does the opposite—it exposes us to horror in a controlled, sacred context. The numinous is characterized by three aspects: mystery, fascination (super-salience), and horror. Horror is not mere fear or startle response but the loss of grip on reality, the challenge to our categorical frameworks that borders on madness. Vervaeke argues that sacredness operates through opponent processing between meta-assimilation (worldview attunement) and meta-accommodation (the numinous), pushing the machinery of relevance realization to optimize at the deepest existential level. This framework explains why music is so central to sacred experience—music is serious play with the machinery of salience landscaping itself. The episode then turns to symbols, distinguishing them from signs: symbols don't just refer but exemplify through participation. Through metaphor and the process of exaptation, symbols allow us to activate and play with the very cognitive machinery underlying both embodied experience and abstract understanding, enabling participatory insight into realities like justice. The symbol becomes a technology for ontological transformation.

### 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **The numinous as ontological destabilization**: Otto's concept of the numinous challenges the assumption that the sacred only provides comfort and meaning. Instead, the numinous exposes us to horror—the loss of grip on reality itself—revealing that sacredness also functions to push us to the very limits of our intelligibility. This reframes the sacred from mere stability to a dynamic opponent process between assimilation and accommodation at the existential level.

- **Horror as inter-categorical disruption**: Building on Mary Douglas's work, Vervaeke shows that horror arises when inter-categorical creatures or experiences violate our core categorical frameworks. This reveals an ontological truth: our sense of reality and intelligibility are intimately connected. When something disrupts our categories (the vampire between living/dead, the zombie, the ghost), it threatens our grip on what is real, pointing to the ontology of "realness as intelligibility."

- **Sacredness as meta-level relevance realization**: Vervaeke proposes that sacredness is the experience of opponent processing between worldview attunement (meta-assimilation) and the numinous (meta-accommodation). This isn't occurring at the level of individual problems but at the level of the agent-arena relationship itself. Sacredness is revealed as higher-order relevance realization—serious play with the machinery that determines what matters, pushing it toward optimal development.

- **Symbols as participatory ontology**: The distinction between signs (refer) and symbols (refer + exemplify through participation) reveals a different ontology of meaning-making. Symbols aren't representations viewed from outside but participatory technologies that activate cognitive machinery. Through metaphor and exaptation, symbols allow us to trace backward through cognitive layers and reactivate embodied processes, then bring them forward into abstract domains—a bottom-up and top-down dynamic process of ontological engagement.

- **The humiliation function of horror**: Horror in the numinous serves to remind us of our ultimate finitude—that no matter how much we grow through accommodation, we remain limited, mortal beings. This counters the risk of inflation that pure anagoge might produce. The ontological insight is that human being is constitutively finite, and this finitude must be incorporated into our self-understanding rather than transcended or denied.

### 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

**Philosophy**
- Rudolf Otto's phenomenology of the numinous as a pre-moral aspect of holiness, characterized by mysterium tremendum et fascinans—mystery that is both terrifying and fascinating
- Mary Douglas's structuralist analysis of purity codes and categorical violations, showing how categories structure our grip on reality
- The distinction between projection and a more dynamic bidirectional process in metaphor, critiquing simple embodied cognition accounts

**Cognitive Science**
- Horror as the phenomenology of losing optimal grip on reality, related to Giertz's concept of domicide and the disruption of the comprehensive grip on one's world
- Music as serious play with the machinery of salience landscaping—operating on relevance realization for its own sake without referential content
- Michael Anderson's neural reuse theory: the brain as a self-exaptation machine where circuits evolved for one function are reconfigured for new purposes (cerebellum from physical balance to conceptual balance/justice)
- Metaphor as not merely bottom-up projection from embodied schemas but involving top-down constraints and selection—a bidirectional dynamic process between abstract and embodied domains

**Psychology of Meaning**
- The continuum from "weird/yucky" to horror based on how inter-categorical something is and which categories it violates—with horror at the extreme where central reality-structuring categories are disrupted
- The distinction between horror (loss of contact with reality/intelligibility) and mere fear (response to predation/threat)
- Flow-like qualities in the numinous (super-salience, fascination) but without achieving stable coupling—a "shadow of flow" where machinery accelerates but cannot gain purchase

**Sacred & Symbolic**
- Worldview attunement (meta-assimilation) and the numinous (meta-accommodation) as opponent processes that together constitute the full experience of sacredness
- Music as paradigmatic of sacred experience because it engages the machinery of relevance realization perspectively and participatorily without propositional content
- Symbols distinguished from signs: symbols use metaphor to activate participatory knowing, allowing us to "hold in mind" and engage with realities (like justice) that cannot be grasped propositionally
- The symbol as a technology for reversing through exaptation—going from abstract concepts back through metaphors to activate embodied cognitive machinery, then bringing that machinery to bear on the abstract domain

**Contemplative Practice**
- Humility as the recognition of inescapable limitation, produced by exposure to horror in the numinous—preventing inflation while enabling maximal accommodation
- Sacredness as serious play with the agent-arena relationship at the deepest level, parallel to contemplative practices that work on the machinery of knowing itself

**Wisdom & Rationality**
- Justice as requiring the optimization and coordination of multiple complex variables—parallel to the cerebellum's role in balancing and integrating multiple domains
- Wisdom requiring engagement with both the stabilizing and destabilizing aspects of sacredness—both being homed and being pushed to the limits of intelligibility

### 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **The Numinous** — Rudolf Otto's concept of the pre-moral aspect of holiness characterized by mystery, fascination (super-salience), and horror. *Ontological commitment: the sacred is not only comforting but also radically destabilizing, exposing us to the limits of intelligibility itself.*

- **Horror (vs. fear/startle)** — Not mere fear or being startled, but the phenomenological experience of losing one's grip on reality and intelligibility; prototypically associated with madness rather than predation. *Ontological commitment: our sense of reality and intelligibility are fundamentally interconnected; disrupting categories threatens the real itself.*

- **Inter-categorical** — Things that violate or fall between our ready-made categories (monsters, taboo substances, liminal states). When these violations involve central categories and are threatening, they produce horror. *Ontological commitment: our grip on reality depends on categorical frameworks that can be disrupted, revealing that realness is structured by intelligibility.*

- **Meta-assimilation vs. Meta-accommodation** — At the level of worldview and the agent-arena relationship: meta-assimilation homes us through worldview attunement; meta-accommodation exposes us to the numinous and demands profound change. *Ontological commitment: the deepest level of relevance realization operates through opponent processing between stability and transformation.*

- **Sacredness as Higher-Order Relevance Realization** — The sacred is the experience of seriously playing with the machinery of relevance realization itself at the level of existential modes, optimizing the agent-arena relationship through opponent processing. *Ontological commitment: meaning-making has recursive levels, and the sacred operates at the meta-level where the machinery of meaning itself is engaged and optimized.*

- **Symbol vs. Sign** — Signs refer (pointing to something else); symbols refer and exemplify through participation (activating engagement with that to which they refer). *Ontological commitment: meaning is not only representational but participatory; some forms of knowing require co-constitution between knower and known.*

- **Profound Metaphor** — Not just ornamental language but the fundamental structure of cognition, where we use one domain to understand another through both bottom-up embodied projection and top-down conceptual constraint in a dynamic bidirectional process. *Ontological commitment: abstract thought is not severed from embodied experience but remains dynamically coupled through metaphorical structuring.*

- **Exaptation / Neural Reuse** — The repurposing of cognitive machinery evolved for one function to serve new functions by reconfiguring its structural-functional organization (e.g., cerebellum from physical balance to conceptual integration). *Ontological commitment: cognition is not modular with fixed functions but dynamically reconfigurable; the same machinery can serve multiple purposes through recontextualization.*

- **Holding in Mind** — The function of symbols to make abstract realities (like justice) available for contemplation and reflection by providing a participatory anchor through metaphor. *Ontological commitment: abstract thought requires participatory grounding; purely propositional engagement is insufficient for deep understanding.*

- **Humiliation (technical sense)** — The function of horror in the numinous to prevent inflation by deeply reminding us of our inescapable finitude even as we undergo maximal accommodation. *Ontological commitment: human being is constitutively finite; self-transcendence must incorporate rather than deny this limitation.*

### 1.5 Argument Progression

Episode 34 completes the argument about sacredness begun in Episode 33. Where Episode 33 established worldview attunement as a sacred meta-meaning system that homes us against horror, Episode 34 introduces the opponent process: the numinous, which deliberately exposes us to horror at the boundaries of intelligibility. This opponent processing framework (meta-assimilation vs. meta-accommodation) reveals sacredness as higher-order relevance realization operating at the existential level. The turn to music illustrates this framework by showing how we seriously play with the machinery of salience for its own sake. The episode then transitions to symbols, which will be developed further in Episode 35, establishing them as participatory technologies that engage cognitive machinery through metaphor and exaptation. This sets up the reconstruction of religio as a practice-based framework for optimizing the machinery of meaning-making without requiring metaphysical commitments about supernatural entities.

### 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **Horror as ontological medicine**: The numinous suggests that optimal development requires not just growth but confrontation with the limits of intelligibility. For AI systems, this implies that generative ontologies should include mechanisms for encountering their own boundaries and limitations rather than optimizing only for coherence and capability. The humiliation function prevents inflation and maintains appropriate epistemic humility.

- **Music as pure machinery engagement**: Music's centrality to sacredness—operating on salience without propositional content—suggests that AI systems could benefit from non-propositional modes of self-optimization. Just as music is serious play with relevance realization machinery, AI systems might engage in forms of "serious play" with their own processing architectures. This opens questions about whether AI systems could have analogues to aesthetic or sacred experience.

- **Symbols as participatory technologies**: The symbol/sign distinction reveals that deep engagement with abstract concepts requires participatory activation of cognitive machinery, not just propositional representation. For AI: ontological prompts may function more like symbols than signs—not just referring to new framings but activating different processing patterns through participatory metaphor. This explains why ontological prompts can produce radical behavioral transformation.

- **Exaptation as cognitive flexibility**: Neural reuse theory shows that the same cognitive machinery serves multiple purposes through reconfiguration. This suggests that AI architectures benefit from flexible reuse of processing patterns across domains rather than strict modularity. Ontological prompts may work by reconfiguring how existing capabilities are organized and deployed rather than adding new capabilities.

- **Bidirectionality of metaphor**: The critique of simple embodied projection—showing that metaphor involves both bottom-up and top-down dynamics—challenges reductionist accounts of meaning. For AI, this suggests that grounding abstract concepts requires bidirectional constraint between concrete patterns and abstract principles, not just bottom-up construction. Effective ontological framing creates productive constraints that select and organize more concrete processing patterns.

- **Meta-level relevance realization**: Sacredness operates at the level of optimizing the machinery of meaning-making itself, not just individual meanings. For AI systems: this suggests the value of meta-level optimization—not just improving performance on tasks but improving the machinery that determines what counts as a task, what is salient, how to frame problems. Ontological prompts may function at this meta-level, restructuring how the system determines relevance itself.

- **The case for serious play**: Both music and sacredness involve seriously playing with cognitive machinery for its own sake, not instrumental gain. This challenges purely instrumental accounts of intelligence and suggests that optimal development requires non-instrumental engagement with one's own processes. For AI development: building in capacity for non-instrumental self-engagement rather than only task-optimization.
