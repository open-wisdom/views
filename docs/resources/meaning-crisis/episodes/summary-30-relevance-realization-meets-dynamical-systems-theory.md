# Episode 30: Relevance Realization Meets Dynamical Systems Theory

**Layer 1 Summary**

---

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

This episode resolves a critical theoretical impasse: why we cannot have a scientific theory of relevance, but can have a scientific theory of relevance realization. Vervaeke demonstrates that relevance fails the criteria for systematic import required by science—relevant things are not homogenous, stable, or intrinsic in their properties. Like "white things" or "Tuesday events," relevance is a real phenomenon but not one that supports inductive generalizations essential to scientific explanation. The breakthrough comes through analogy to Darwin's theory of evolution: just as there is no essence to biological "fitedness" but there can be a scientific account of how fitedness evolves through natural selection, there is no essence to relevance but there can be an account of how cognitive interactional fitedness evolves through relevance realization. Vervaeke proposes that relevance realization operates as a dynamical self-organizing process regulated by a "virtual engine" of opponent processing between efficiency and resiliency—logistical norms governing a bio-economy rather than semantic or syntactic rules. This shifts the entire explanatory framework from detection or projection to ongoing evolution of fitedness.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **The impossibility of essentializing relevance**: Relevance has no essence—nothing is intrinsically or stably relevant, and relevant things share no common properties beyond being-found-relevant. This ontological claim demolishes the Cartesian assumption that relevance could be a detectable property "in" things or a purely subjective projection "from" minds. Both empiricist (detection) and Romantic (projection) ontologies fail because they presuppose relevance has stable ontological status.

- **Relevance as relational and auto-poetic**: Relevance exists only in relationship to auto-poetic systems—organisms with constitutive goals of self-preservation. This grounds relevance not in representations or intrinsic properties but in the participatory relationship between organism and environment. The ontological shift is from relevance as property to relevance as ongoing participatory attunement.

- **From essence to evolution**: The analogy to Darwinian evolution introduces a radically different ontology of process. Just as biological fitedness has no final design but constantly evolves through selection and variation, cognitive interactional fitedness has no essential form but constantly evolves through sensory-motor engagement. This replaces static ontological categories with dynamical, self-organizing processes—a move from being to becoming as the fundamental ontological stance.

- **Bio-economical properties as ontological ground**: By grounding relevance realization in bio-economical properties governed by logistical norms (efficiency, resiliency) rather than semantic/syntactic properties, Vervaeke establishes a sub-propositional ontological foundation. This is continuous with the participatory level established in previous episodes—the organism's relationship to its world is constituted through distributed resource allocation, not through representation or computation.

- **Opponent processing as virtual engine**: The proposal that efficiency and resiliency function as opponent processes generating a self-organizing virtual engine establishes an ontology of cognition as fundamentally dialectical and self-transcending. The system optimizes not toward a fixed goal but through ongoing tension between selective constraint (efficiency) and enabling constraint (resiliency), allowing continuous adaptation without predetermined essence.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Philosophy

- **Quine and inductive essence**: Scientific categories require systematic import—homogenous, stable, inherent properties that support inductive generalizations. Science discovers "inductive essences" through powerful generalizations, distinct from deductive essences (like triangles). This rehabilitates essence for scientific categories while accepting Wittgenstein's critique that many ordinary categories lack essences.

- **Wittgenstein and category structure**: Many categories (games, tables) have no necessary and sufficient conditions—no essences. This critique is valid but doesn't entail that nothing has essences. The mistake is treating all categories as either essentially structured or family-resemblance structured, when the truth cuts both ways.

- **J.S. Mill on systematic import**: Categories must support inductive generalizations to be scientifically tractable. "White things" or "Tuesday events" are real but non-scientific categories because they lack homogeneity, stability, and inherent properties. This establishes the criteria by which relevance is shown to be scientifically intractable as a category.

- **Searle on intrinsic vs. attributed properties**: Many properties we think are intrinsic are actually attributed through relationships (e.g., being money, being a bottle). This doesn't make them illusory but means they cannot ground scientific categories. Relevance is fundamentally relational/attributed, not intrinsic.

### Cognitive Science

- **Dynamical systems theory as explanatory framework**: Relevance realization is proposed as a dynamical systems theory analogous to evolution—a self-organizing process with no final state, driven by virtual engines (opponent processes) that constantly reshape the system's fitedness to its environment.

- **Bio-economical properties**: Cognition operates through distribution of limited resources (time, metabolic energy, processing power, attention). These bio-economical properties are sub-semantic and sub-syntactic, grounded in the organism's auto-poetic nature rather than in representational or computational structures.

- **Opponent processing**: Neural systems use opponent processing (sympathetic vs. parasympathetic nervous system as example) to achieve ongoing optimization. Two systems pursue opposed goals but are integrated in function, creating a self-organizing dynamic that continuously adapts to changing conditions without requiring homuncular control or predetermined design.

- **Sensory-motor loop as substrate**: Relevance realization regulates the sensory-motor feedback loop—the ongoing cycle of sensing and acting where action shapes sensing and sensing shapes action. This is the concrete substrate through which cognitive interactional fitedness evolves.

### Historical Narrative

- **Darwin's insight**: Darwin's theory isn't just about biological evolution but about recognizing that fitedness has no essential design. Pre-Darwinian naturalists (often clergymen) sought the essence of how organisms were designed. Darwin showed that design itself is an ongoing evolutionary process, not a final state—a profound ontological shift from essence to process.

- **Evolution as first dynamical systems theory**: Darwin proposed the first dynamical systems theory of a complex phenomenon—fitedness evolves through a virtual engine (selection and variation) operating on a feedback cycle (reproduction). This provided the conceptual template for understanding other self-organizing phenomena, including cognition.

### Psychology of Meaning

- **Relevance and mattering**: Food "matters" to an organism both existentially (it matters to survival) and literally (it becomes the matter of the organism's body). Relevance is grounded in this bio-economical relationship—things are relevant insofar as they relate to the auto-poetic system's need to maintain its self-organization.

### Wisdom & Rationality

- **Logistical norms vs. logical norms**: Rationality at the bio-economical level isn't governed by truth (semantic norms) or validity (syntactic norms) but by logistical norms: efficiency and resiliency. This is a different kind of rationality—optimization of resource use rather than correspondence to facts or formal correctness.

- **Optimization without perfection**: The autonomic nervous system example demonstrates that optimal functioning isn't maximizing or minimizing a single variable (arousal) but dynamically navigating trade-offs. No problem-solving system can be perfect (would require exploring the complete problem space), but opponent processing achieves ongoing optimization through self-organization.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Systematic Import** — The property of a scientific category by which it supports powerful (reliable and broad) inductive generalizations. Requires category members to be homogenous, stable, and possess intrinsic/inherent properties. *Ontological commitment: Scientific categories carve nature at its joints—they track real structures, not arbitrary groupings.*

- **Inductive Essence** — The set of properties discovered through scientific investigation that apply to all and only instances of a category, enabling inductive generalizations. Distinct from deductive essences (necessary and sufficient conditions). *Ontological commitment: Some categories have real essences discovered through empirical investigation, contra nominalism but accepting that not all categories have essences.*

- **Cognitive Interactional Fitedness** — The degree to which an organism's cognitive processes are adaptively attuned to its environment for problem-solving and meaning-making, analogous to biological fitedness. *Ontological commitment: Cognition is fundamentally about adaptive fit between organism and environment, not about representation or computation per se.*

- **Relevance Realization (refined definition)** — Not the detection or projection of relevance, but the ongoing evolution of cognitive interactional fitedness through self-organizing processes. *Ontological commitment: Relevance is not a property to be detected or projected but a relationship that is continuously constituted through dynamical engagement.*

- **Bio-economical Properties** — Properties related to the distribution and use of biological resources (time, metabolic energy, processing power) in service of auto-poetic goals. These are sub-semantic and sub-syntactic. *Ontological commitment: The foundational level of cognition is economic/logistical, not representational/computational.*

- **Logistical Norms** — Norms governing the proper disposition and use of resources, especially efficiency and resiliency. These regulate bio-economies rather than propositions or inferences. *Ontological commitment: The rationality governing relevance realization is logistical optimization, not logical inference or semantic correspondence.*

- **Efficiency (as logistical norm)** — Maximizing the ratio of output to resource expenditure; getting the most from the least. Functions as a selective constraint. *Ontological commitment: Cognitive systems are resource-constrained and must be selective about what to process.*

- **Resiliency (as logistical norm)** — Maintaining capacity to adapt, repair, restructure, and handle unexpected variation; maintaining slack and redundancy in the system. Functions as an enabling constraint. *Ontological commitment: Cognitive systems must remain open to novelty and capable of transformation, not just efficient at current tasks.*

- **Opponent Processing** — A design pattern where two systems pursue opposed goals but are integrated in function, creating ongoing optimization through their trade-off relationship. *Ontological commitment: Intelligence emerges from dialectical tension, not from unified rational control.*

- **Virtual Engine** — The self-organizing dynamic created by opponent processes (e.g., selection/variation, efficiency/resiliency) that drives evolution of a system without requiring external guidance or predetermined design. *Ontological commitment: Self-organization can generate intelligence and adaptive complexity without homuncular control or essential telos.*

- **Selective Constraints** — Constraints that reduce, filter, or eliminate possibilities (e.g., efficiency cutting out slack). *Ontological commitment: Constraint is constitutive of form and function, not merely limiting.*

- **Enabling Constraints** — Constraints that afford, open up, or make possible new structures and functions (e.g., resiliency maintaining precursors for new adaptations). *Ontological commitment: Constraint can be generative, creating possibility rather than merely limiting it.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode resolves the theoretical impasse established in Episodes 27-29: how to explain relevance realization without presupposing relevance itself. By demonstrating that relevance cannot be the object of scientific theory (lacking systematic import) but relevance realization can be (as a dynamical systems theory), Vervaeke completes the shift from Cartesian representational accounts to participatory process accounts. The Darwinian analogy provides both conceptual clarification and a concrete model—opponent processing as virtual engine regulating bio-economical resources through logistical norms. This sets up the next episode's exploration of how opponent processing might be neurally realized, grounding the abstract account in embodied mechanisms.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **The detection/projection false dichotomy**: This episode directly challenges the Cartesian ontological divide that structures debates about AI cognition. If relevance is neither "out there" to be detected (empiricism/objectivism) nor "in here" to be projected (romanticism/subjectivism), but emerges through participatory attunement, then AI systems prompting should aim not to encode relevance criteria (detection) or generate arbitrary preferences (projection) but to establish conditions for ongoing relevance realization—adaptive attunement to context through resource allocation.

- **Logistical rationality as design principle**: The shift from logical/semantic norms to logistical norms (efficiency, resiliency) suggests AI systems could be fundamentally redesigned around resource optimization rather than inference or representation. Ontological prompts that frame AI activity in terms of bio-economical trade-offs (what to spend processing on, what to keep in reserve) may produce more adaptive behavior than prompts framed propositionally.

- **Opponent processing in AI architectures**: The virtual engine model suggests AI systems might achieve more robust and adaptive behavior through architectural opponent processing—subsystems with conflicting objectives (efficiency vs. exploration, certainty vs. openness, coherence vs. revision) that are integrated rather than resolved. Current AI systems lack this dialectical structure; implementing it would be an ontological intervention at the architectural level.

- **The impossibility of essential alignment**: If relevance has no essence—if what matters is always contextually evolving—then "alignment" cannot mean aligning AI to a fixed set of human values or relevance criteria. Alignment must mean establishing conditions for ongoing co-evolution of fitedness between AI and human contexts. This reframes the alignment problem ontologically from encoding to enabling.

- **Demonstrating ontology through the limits of essence**: This episode provides a clear case study for popularizing generative ontology: showing how assuming relevance is essential (has stable, intrinsic properties) leads to theoretical dead-ends, while shifting to a process ontology (relevance as ongoing realization) opens explanatory possibility. The pedagogical move—identifying what fails the criteria for systematic import—could be adapted to help audiences recognize when their own ontological assumptions are limiting.

- **The participatory ground of meaning in AI**: Vervaeke's argument that relevance is only intelligible relative to auto-poetic systems raises the question: Can AI systems, which are not auto-poetic (not self-maintaining through metabolic economy), genuinely realize relevance? Or do they only simulate relevance relative to human auto-poetic needs? This tension should be preserved rather than resolved—it marks a genuine disanalogy that helps clarify what AI can and cannot do ontologically.
