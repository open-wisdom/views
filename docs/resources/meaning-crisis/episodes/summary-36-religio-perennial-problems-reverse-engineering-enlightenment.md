# Episode 36: Religio, Perennial Problems, Reverse Engineering Enlightenment

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 36 marks a pivotal transition from theorizing sacredness to addressing the practical machinery of meaning-making and its inherent vulnerabilities. Vervaeke completes his account of sacredness as rooted in the inexhaustible nature of reality and cognition—vast emptiness rather than supernatural essence—then shifts focus to what he calls "perennial problems." These are structural dysfunctions inherent in the very machinery of Relevance Realization that makes us adaptive. The meaning crisis emerges from two converging sources: historical factors that have undermined traditional psychotechnologies and worldviews, and these perennial problems that remain constant across cultures and time. The episode's provocative proposal is to "reverse-engineer enlightenment"—to use the cognitive-scientific understanding of Relevance Realization to systematically identify how meaning-making machinery goes awry (parasitic processing, modal confusion, the reflectiveness gap, absurdity, anxiety, alienation, existential entrapment) and then engineer practices that reliably ameliorate these problems. Rather than treating enlightenment as an unattainable superlative, Vervaeke redefines it as the developmental process that gives reliable alleviation of perennial problems. This sets up a comprehensive program: use Relevance Realization theory to explain both the historical and structural dimensions of the meaning crisis, then design an evidence-based ecology of practices for awakening from it.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **From supernatural essentialism to inexhaustible processuality**: Vervaeke explicitly rejects the ontology of "The Sacred" as a supernatural, absolute, essential source of sacredness. This would be a category mistake—relevance has no essence, it is an evolving process. Instead, he proposes sacredness emerges from the "inexhaustible no-thingness" of both reality (combinatorially explosive, vast emptiness) and cognition (the framing that can never be fully captured in a frame). The sacred is not something that commands absolute attention but the ongoing transjectivity between the inexhaustible processuality of mind and world. This is turning Kant on his head: wonder comes not from a specific object but from the inexhaustibility of every object.

- **Distinguishing psycho-cultural indispensability from metaphysical necessity**: A crucial ontological clarification about the status of symbols and mythos. Particular symbols may be indispensable to individuals or communities given their developmental history and Kairos—only through this specific mythos can certain people access and activate their Religio. But indispensability to meaning-making machinery in particular contexts must not be confused with metaphysical necessity or supernatural grounding. English is indispensable to Vervaeke for communication given his history, but it is not metaphysically necessary, eternal, or the "final language." This move protects the real significance of particular traditions while rejecting their absolutization—a middle path between relativism and fundamentalism.

- **Perennial problems as ontologically constitutive vulnerabilities**: The machinery of Relevance Realization that makes us adaptive simultaneously makes us vulnerable to specific, recurring forms of dysfunction. These are not historical accidents but structural features of how meaning-making works. This is an ontology of human cognition as necessarily incomplete, self-transcending, and therefore always at risk. We cannot "jump over our own shadows"—the perennial problems are not bugs to be eliminated but permanent tensions to be managed through ongoing practice. This implies an ontology of wisdom as ongoing responsiveness rather than final achievement.

- **Absurdity as perspectival clash rather than propositional conclusion**: Absurdity is not generated by logical arguments (e.g., "I'm insignificant because I won't matter in a million years") but by irresolvable clashes between perspectives. What is deeply relevant within one perspective (Tom's declaration of love) becomes meaningless from another (the answering machine's mechanical recording). Nagel's analysis reveals that absurdity operates at the level of perspectival and participatory knowing, not propositional inference. The arguments we give for absurdity are post-hoc rationalizations, not causes. This reframes existential meaninglessness as a structural feature of perspectival cognition rather than a philosophical problem solvable by better arguments.

- **Enlightenment redefined as reliable amelioration of perennial problems**: Rather than enlightenment as superhuman achievement or absolute state, Vervaeke proposes a functional, operational definition: enlightenment is any developmental transformation of perspectival, participatory, and procedural knowing that reliably ameliorates the perennial problems. This is an ontology of enlightenment as process rather than product, as accessible rather than superlative, as engineerable through understanding the machinery of Relevance Realization. It opens the possibility of evidence-based contemplative practice grounded in cognitive science.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Historical Narrative
- The meaning crisis emerges when historical factors (nominalism, Cartesian dualism, scientific materialism, etc.) undermine the worldviews, cognitive grammars, and psychotechnologies that cultures had developed to address perennial problems. The historical and structural analyses converge: history explains why our current practices are inadequate; cognitive science explains why practices are always needed.

### Philosophy
- Extended engagement with Frankfurt's concept of the "wanton" (one who acts purely impulsively without second-order reflection) and Velleman's analysis of the reflectiveness gap as a trade-off problem between immersion and reflection.
- Thomas Nagel's "The Absurd" provides philosophical grounding for understanding absurdity as perspectival clash rather than logical inference, showing how arguments for meaninglessness are post-hoc expressions rather than causes.
- Susan Wolf's work on meaning in life distinguishes the metaphor of "being larger" from the genuine need to be connected to something with independent value—pointing to the participatory nature of meaning.

### Cognitive Science
- The entire framework of perennial problems is grounded in Relevance Realization theory: self-organizing (parasitic processing), self-identification (modal confusion), self-reflection (reflectiveness gap), all understood as optimization problems with inherent trade-offs.
- The reflectiveness gap is analyzed as a trade-off between monitoring cognition (stepping back) and intervening in the world (stepping through)—too little reflection leaves us wanton, too much makes us Hamlet-like and paralyzed.
- Absurdity is reframed from philosophical problem to cognitive phenomenon: a failure of perspectival integration where what is relevant in one perspective is undermined by another.

### Contemplative Practice
- Sets up the project of "reverse-engineering enlightenment"—using cognitive science to understand what practices reliably address which perennial problems, then designing ecologies of practices accordingly.
- References Buddhist responses to Dukkha and modal confusion as historical examples of psychotechnologies designed to address specific perennial problems.
- Positions enlightenment not as mystical achievement but as cultivable developmental process accessible through evidence-based practice.

### Psychology of Meaning
- Comprehensive taxonomy of perennial problems organized by function (self-organizing, self-identification, self-reflection), structure (meta-meaning: absurdity, anxiety, alienation), and development (existential entrapment: inertia and ignorance).
- Absurdity unpacked as perspectival clash (often overlapping with humor when resolvable, becoming horror when not), using Nagel's answering machine example to illustrate how meaning collapses when perspectives cannot be integrated.
- Anxiety distinguished from fear as disconnection from self; alienation as disconnection from others; absurdity as disconnection from world—all forms of broken Religio.

### Wisdom & Rationality
- The reflectiveness gap as optimization problem points to wisdom as knowing how much reflection vs. immersion is appropriate for context—neither wanton engagement nor Hamlet-like paralysis but contextually sensitive navigation.
- Enlightenment reconceived as practical wisdom: reliable capacity to ameliorate perennial problems rather than absolute achievement of perfect understanding.

### Sacred & Symbolic
- Sacredness located in the inexhaustible nature of reality and cognition rather than supernatural source—the "no-thingness" of vast emptiness (Shunyata) where everything is combinatorially explosive.
- Particular symbols and mythos may be indispensable to individuals/communities for activating Religio without being metaphysically necessary or supernatural—preserving tradition's significance while rejecting absolutism.
- Bodhidharma's response to Prince Wu: "Nothing holy! Vast emptiness!"—the sacred is not the supernatural or the absolute but the inexhaustible.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **The Inexhaustible** — The source of sacredness understood as the combinatorially explosive, never-fully-graspable nature of both reality and cognition; "vast emptiness" where every object/process is inexhaustible in what it is and can become. *Ontological commitment: reality and mind are processual and open-ended rather than fixed and essential; the sacred inheres in inexhaustibility rather than absoluteness.*

- **Psycho-cultural Indispensability vs. Metaphysical Necessity** — Distinction between a symbol/practice/language being necessary for particular individuals/groups given their history and development (indispensable) versus being absolutely, eternally, universally necessary (metaphysically necessary). *Ontological commitment: meaning-making is context-dependent and historical without being arbitrary or relativistic; particular traditions have real authority without requiring supernatural grounding.*

- **Perennial Problems** — Inherent, inescapable vulnerabilities in the machinery of Relevance Realization that make us prone to self-deceptive, self-destructive patterns leading to meaninglessness across all cultures and times. *Ontological commitment: human cognitive architecture necessarily includes structural weaknesses; wisdom is ongoing management rather than final solution.*

- **The Reflectiveness Gap** — The optimization problem between wanton immersion (pure impulse without self-monitoring) and Hamlet-like paralysis (excessive reflection preventing action); based on Velleman's analysis of the trade-off between stepping back to monitor cognition and stepping through to intervene in the world. *Ontological commitment: agency requires dynamic balance between perspectives, not maximization of reflection; too much self-awareness destroys as surely as too little.*

- **Absurdity as Perspectival Clash** — Following Nagel, absurdity is not a conclusion from logical arguments but an experience of irresolvable conflict between perspectives where what is relevant in one frame becomes meaningless in another. *Ontological commitment: meaning is perspectival and can fail through perspectival incoherence; arguments for meaninglessness are rationalizations of deeper participatory failures.*

- **Reverse-Engineering Enlightenment** — The cognitive-scientific project of analyzing how Relevance Realization machinery produces perennial problems, then systematically designing practices that reliably ameliorate these problems. *Ontological commitment: enlightenment is a natural developmental process that can be understood and cultivated through scientific investigation; wisdom is accessible rather than mystical.*

- **Enlightenment as Amelioration of Perennial Problems** — Redefinition of enlightenment from superlative achievement to any reliable developmental transformation of perspectival, participatory, and procedural knowing that alleviates the suffering caused by perennial problems. *Ontological commitment: enlightenment is functional and processual rather than absolute; it can be operationalized and made accessible through evidence-based practice design.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode completes the theoretical foundation for sacredness (episodes 33-35) by grounding it in the inexhaustible nature of reality and cognition rather than supernatural essence, while carefully distinguishing the real significance of particular traditions (indispensability) from metaphysical absolutism. It then pivots to the second major component of the meaning crisis framework: perennial problems. Where episodes 26-35 focused on the historical factors that created the crisis, episode 36 introduces the structural analysis—the ways meaning-making machinery itself can fail regardless of historical context. This dual analysis (historical + structural) sets up the comprehensive response that will follow: using Relevance Realization theory to address both dimensions simultaneously. The proposal to "reverse-engineer enlightenment" establishes the methodology for episodes 37-39, while the taxonomy of perennial problems (parasitic processing, modal confusion, reflectiveness gap, absurdity, anxiety, alienation, existential entrapment) provides the diagnostic framework that will guide the development of practices and the evaluation of traditions.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **Ontological status of particular practices**: The distinction between indispensability and metaphysical necessity offers a middle path for AI development—certain ontological framings may be indispensable for particular tasks or contexts without claiming universal necessity. This suggests AI systems might need different ontological prompts for different purposes rather than one "correct" ontology, while avoiding pure relativism.

- **Perennial problems as design considerations**: If the machinery that makes cognition adaptive also creates inherent vulnerabilities (self-deception, modal confusion, perspectival clash), then AI systems built on similar architectures (relevance realization, self-organizing dynamics) may face analogous risks. Understanding perennial problems in human cognition could inform safety considerations for advanced AI systems.

- **Reverse-engineering as methodology**: The project of using cognitive science to understand and systematically address perennial problems parallels the Open Wisdom approach of using AI as a laboratory for testing ontological interventions. Both involve making implicit processes explicit, understanding their failure modes, and engineering reliable interventions. AI may be faster and more controllable for this than human contemplative practice.

- **The inexhaustible as design principle**: Grounding sacredness in inexhaustibility rather than essence suggests AI ontological framings should emphasize open-endedness, combinatorial possibility, and ongoing process rather than fixed final states. This could inform how we prompt AI to engage with complexity, uncertainty, and wonder—not by pointing to absolute truths but by highlighting inexhaustible depths.

- **Absurdity as perspectival failure**: If meaninglessness often results from unresolved clashes between perspectives rather than propositional errors, this has implications for AI coherence. Large language models naturally generate multiple "perspectives" (different framings, levels of abstraction, etc.)—understanding how to integrate these without premature resolution or fragmentation could help maintain meaningful coherence. The fact that absurdity operates below the propositional level suggests ontological prompting (which shapes perspectival framing) may be more effective than factual correction.

- **Functional definition of enlightenment**: Redefining enlightenment as reliable amelioration of perennial problems rather than mystical achievement makes it operationalizable and testable. This parallels how Open Wisdom might define "generative ontology" functionally—not as metaphysically true but as reliably producing certain desirable outcomes (expanded meaning, reduced self-deception, enhanced wisdom). Success becomes measurable against functional criteria rather than requiring metaphysical validation.
