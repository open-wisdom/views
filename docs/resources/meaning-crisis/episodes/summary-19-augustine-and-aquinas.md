# Episode 19: Augustine and Aquinas - Summary

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mRh4lwtgfVE) · [Read transcript](https://www.meaningcrisis.co/ep-19-awakening-from-the-meaning-crisis-augustine-and-aquin/)

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

This episode traces the culmination and beginning of the unraveling of the great Augustinian synthesis that defined medieval meaning-making for a thousand years. Augustine, living through the collapse of the Roman Empire, synthesizes Neoplatonism, Christianity, and Gnostic insights into a unified framework that integrates three orders of meaning: the Nomological Order (Aristotelian science of how reality is structured), the Normative Order (Neoplatonic ladder of self-transcendence from evil/chaos to the Good), and the Narrative Order (Christian story of history moving toward consummation through Agape). This synthesis powerfully unified what we now experience as opposed: science and spirituality, reason and transformation, therapy and realness. Augustine's personal struggle with sexual addiction and his mystical experience reading Plotinus - followed by his inability to sustain it - led him to recognize that love, not just reason, is what needs healing, and that Agape provides the transformative force that completes what Neoplatonic reason begins.

However, the episode then documents the first cracks in this synthesis. After the Great Schism of 1054, Western Christianity becomes increasingly Aristotelian and less Neoplatonic. A shift in psycho-technology occurs: reading transforms from communal, oral, participatory Lectio Divina (designed for ontological transformation) to silent, individual, propositional consumption aimed at logical coherence. Thomas Aquinas attempts to preserve the Axial framework by separating the natural world (studied by reason and science) from the supernatural world (accessed only by faith and will). In doing so, he inadvertently begins divorcing science from spirituality, reason from love, and as the natural world becomes more successful, the supernatural world becomes less "liveable" - threatening the entire Axial grammar of meaning.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **Augustine's Synthetic Ontology**: Augustine creates a three-dimensional "space of meaning" by integrating three orders that map onto the three components of meaning identified in contemporary cognitive science (Heintzelman et al.). The Nomological Order provides coherence (intelligibility, how things fit together), the Normative Order provides significance (value, goodness, degrees of reality), and the Narrative Order provides purpose (directional movement through history via Agape). **Ontological commitment**: Reality is structured as a participatory whole where cosmology, psychology, and history are not separate domains but mutually supporting dimensions of a unified ontological framework. The self is not isolated but embedded in all three orders simultaneously.

- **Love as Ontological Driver**: Augustine recognizes that reason itself is driven by love - specifically, love of truth, goodness, and beauty that moves us toward greater oneness and reality. His insight is that his sexual addiction represents damaged love capacity, not damaged reasoning. **Ontological shift**: From seeing reason as separate from desire/emotion, to seeing love as the fundamental ontological force that animates reason and can either move us toward integration (Eros moving toward the One) or toward fragmentation (disordered desire pulling toward chaos/evil). **Consequence**: This makes transformation a matter of healing and redirecting love (through Agape) rather than purely rational choice, requiring participatory engagement rather than propositional adoption.

- **The Shift from Extensive to Intensive Self**: The change in reading practices (from oral/communal/participatory to silent/individual/propositional) and the shift from Avicenna (Neoplatonic) to Averroes (Aristotelian) instantiates a fundamental ontological transformation in how the self is conceived. **Ontological move**: From the "extensive self" that understands itself through conformity and participatory connection to the world, to the "intensive self" located primarily inside one's head, constituted by coherent propositions and beliefs. **Generative/degenerative consequences**: Gains logical rigor and propositional clarity; loses reading as psycho-technology for existential transformation, loses the Being mode in favor of the Having mode (having beliefs rather than being transformed), and begins the shift toward experiencing mind as separate from world.

- **Aquinas's Two-World Separation**: To accommodate both the authority of rediscovered Aristotle and Christian doctrine, Aquinas reformulates the Axial two-worlds mythology into a strict separation: the natural world (accessible to reason/science) below and the supernatural world (accessible only to faith/will) above. **Ontological assumption made invisible**: The assumption that there can be a clean ontological separation between "natural" and "supernatural," with no continuum or participatory movement between them. **Degenerative consequences**: Love and reason are pulled apart; faith transforms from participatory flow through history to wilful assertion of propositions; science and spirituality begin their divorce. As the natural world becomes more explicable through reason, the supernatural world becomes less "liveable" - not just unthinkable but uninhabitable as a mode of being - threatening the collapse of the entire Axial framework.

- **Evil as Privation vs. Positive Force**: Building on Neoplatonism, Augustine conceives evil not as a positive force but as privation - a "hole in being," increasing chaos, fragmentation, and loss of form/intelligibility as one moves away from the One/Good toward pure chaos. **Ontological commitment**: Evil is not an independent ontological principle (contra Manichaeism) but the absence or degradation of being, order, and intelligibility. This grounds his Christian response to Gnostic dualism while preserving the existential reality of evil's pull and power.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Historical Narrative
- **Augustine (4th-5th century)**: Living through Rome's collapse in Hippo, North Africa, Augustine is attracted first to Manichaeism (Gnostic-influenced dualism of light vs. darkness) due to personal inner conflict (sexual addiction, loss of agency) and the darkening world. After mystical experience reading Plotinus followed by inability to sustain it, he synthesizes Neoplatonism with Christianity through Paul's theology, writing the first Western autobiography (*The Confessions*) as existential manual rather than academic treatise.

- **The Great Schism (1054)**: Christianity splits between Eastern Orthodox and Western Catholic. This weakens Western Christianity's connection to Neoplatonic mystical theology, shifting it toward increasingly Aristotelian emphasis. The cultural-linguistic divide (Greek East vs. Latin West) amplifies philosophical differences.

- **Hugh of St. Victor (1096-1141)**: Represents the transitional period where psycho-technology of reading begins shifting from oral/communal/participatory Lectio Divina to silent/individual/propositional consumption, coinciding with the shift from Neoplatonic (Avicenna) to Aristotelian (Averroes) philosophical dominance.

- **Thomas Aquinas (13th century)**: Faces the crisis of integrating rediscovered Aristotle (whose authority is unimpeachable but whose worldview lacks Christian mythology) into Christian framework. Creates the natural/supernatural distinction to preserve both scientific reason and Christian faith, but inadvertently begins their divorce.

### Philosophy
- **Three Orders of Meaning**: Augustine's integration of (1) Nomological Order - Aristotelian structure of reality, geocentric cosmology, conformity theory of truth; (2) Normative Order - Neoplatonic ladder from chaos/evil to the One/Good, degrees of reality corresponding to degrees of oneness/intelligibility; (3) Narrative Order - Christian history as teleological movement toward consummation through Agape. These map to coherence, significance, and purpose respectively.

- **Natural/Supernatural Distinction**: Aquinas's reformulation of the two-worlds mythology into strict separation: natural world studied by reason and science vs. supernatural world accessed only by faith and will. This makes the two worlds "fundamentally two separate kinds of worlds" with no continuum between them, contrasting with the Platonic/Augustinian participatory ladder.

- **Faith Redefined**: In Plotinus and Augustine, love (Agape/Eros) moves reason toward the Good. In Aquinas, love moves the will to assert propositions that reason cannot access. Faith shifts from participation in historical/cosmological processes to "wilful assertion" of creedal statements.

### Cognitive Science
- **Three Components of Meaning** (Heintzelman et al.): Empirical finding that meaning in life consists of coherence (intelligibility, things fitting together), significance (value, depth, goodness), and purpose (direction, narrative). These map precisely onto Augustine's three orders, demonstrating that this medieval synthesis was responding to deep cognitive needs that remain constant.

- **The Extensive vs. Intensive Self** (Krantz, Illich): Cognitive-cultural shift from self understood through conformity and embeddedness in world (extensive) to self located inside head, constituted by coherent propositional beliefs (intensive). This represents a change in operative ontology of selfhood with profound implications for meaning-making capacity.

### Contemplative Practice
- **Lectio Divina**: Traditional monastic practice of sacred reading designed as psycho-technology for ontological transformation. Involves oral recitation, communal engagement, meditative receptivity, opening oneself to be spoken to and transformed by text. Engages procedural, perspectival, and participatory knowing, not just propositional. Maintains the Being mode rather than merely acquiring beliefs (Having mode).

- **Augustine's Mystical Experience**: Reading Plotinus, Augustine experiences ascent through levels of reality/self to the One, but cannot sustain it - the "gravity" of his damaged love (sexual addiction) pulls him back down. This produces rebound despair, demonstrating that propositional understanding (getting the theory) is insufficient without participatory transformation (sustained ontological reorientation).

- **The Shift Away from Transformative Reading**: Post-Schism shift to silent, individual reading focused on extracting propositions and achieving logical coherence rather than existential transformation. Reading becomes "consumption of propositions" rather than psycho-spiritual practice. This represents loss of a key psycho-technology for ontological transformation.

### Psychology of Meaning
- **Augustine's Sexual Addiction**: Described as "licking the open sore of lust" - simultaneously compelling and disgusting, exacerbating the affliction. Augustine experiences this as loss of agency, being pulled downward by darkness/evil. His analysis: not failure of reason but damaged capacity for love, requiring healing (Gnosis as therapeutic) through Agape rather than rational willpower.

- **The Fruit-Stealing Incident**: Formative experience where young Augustine stole fruit he didn't want, purely to do wrong. This gave him direct existential encounter with what he interpreted as evil as positive force pulling him toward destruction - though his mature theology reframes evil as privation, the experiential reality of being pulled toward chaos remains central.

- **Meaning as Three-Dimensional Space**: Augustine's synthesis provides what cognitive science confirms humans need - coherence (understanding), significance (value), and purpose (direction). The episode emphasizes this as not three separate things but three axes of a unified space of meaning, suggesting meaning-making requires simultaneous engagement with all three dimensions.

### Wisdom & Rationality
- **Reason Driven by Love**: Augustine's core insight that reason itself is animated by love of truth, goodness, and beauty. Wisdom requires not just rational capacity but properly ordered love. This reframes the cultivation of wisdom from acquiring propositional knowledge or logical skill to healing and directing one's fundamental orientation of love.

- **The Limit of Propositional Knowing**: Augustine "gets" Plotinus intellectually - understands the theory - but cannot sustain the mystical experience because his love is disordered. This demonstrates that propositional understanding is necessary but insufficient for wisdom; participatory transformation is required.

- **The Beginning of Reason/Love Divorce**: Aquinas's separation of natural (reason) from supernatural (faith/will/love) begins pulling apart what had been unified. This makes rationality increasingly independent of love, wisdom increasingly separate from scientific knowledge, anticipating the modern divorce of facts from values.

### Sacred & Symbolic
- **Agape as Ontological Force**: Not merely ethical principle but the cosmological-historical-psychological force that moves all things toward the Good, creates the open future, and heals damaged love. Functions simultaneously at all three orders - Narrative (force in history), Normative (force of self-transcendence), Nomological (teleological organization of nature).

- **The Sacred Synthesis**: The Augustinian framework provides what Vervaeke emphasizes is simultaneously scientific, spiritual, therapeutic, and existential. For a millennium, there is no antagonism between these domains. The sacred is not sequestered in a separate "religious" sphere but woven through all dimensions of life and knowledge.

- **Loss of the Supernatural as "Liveable"**: Aquinas's separation creates condition where supernatural world may remain conceivable but becomes less inhabitable. As natural world becomes more explicable through reason/science, supernatural world ceases to be a mode of being one can dwell in, threatening collapse of the sacred canopy that provided meaning.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **The Three Orders of Meaning**: Augustine's synthesis integrating (1) Nomological Order - Aristotelian scientific worldview of how reality is structured; (2) Normative Order - Neoplatonic ladder of self-transcendence from evil to Good; (3) Narrative Order - Christian teleological history moving through Agape. *Ontological commitment: Reality is not a single flat domain but a three-dimensional space where cosmology, axiology, and teleology are inseparable and mutually constituting. Meaning requires simultaneous participation in all three orders.*

- **Coherence, Significance, Purpose** (Heintzelman): The three empirically-identified components of meaning in life. Coherence = intelligibility, things fitting together sensibly. Significance = value, depth, importance of life's elements. Purpose = directional trajectory, sense of life moving toward something. *Ontological commitment: These map precisely to Augustine's three orders, suggesting they reflect deep cognitive architecture rather than culturally contingent preferences. Meaning is not a single property but emerges from the intersection of these three dimensions.*

- **Evil as Privation**: Augustinian doctrine that evil is not independent positive force (contra Manichaeism/Gnosticism) but absence/degradation of being - movement toward chaos, fragmentation, loss of form and intelligibility. Visualized as bottom of the Normative ladder where oneness and sense disintegrate. *Ontological commitment: Being, intelligibility, goodness, beauty, and oneness are convertible - they increase or decrease together. Evil is the "hole in being" toward which things fall when they lose integration and form.*

- **Extensive vs. Intensive Self** (Krantz, Illich): Two contrasting ontologies of selfhood. Extensive self: understands itself through conformity to world, embedded in participatory connection, trans-projectively related to environment. Intensive self: located inside the head, constituted primarily by coherent propositional beliefs, self-enclosed inner mental space. *Ontological commitment: The shift from extensive to intensive self represents changing answer to "where am I?" - from "I am my meaningful embeddedness in world" to "I am the collection of beliefs in my head."*

- **Lectio Divina**: Sacred reading practice engaging oral recitation, communal context, meditative receptivity, and participatory transformation. Reading as psycho-technology for ontological change rather than propositional information extraction. *Ontological commitment: Text is not merely container of propositions to be extracted but living presence that can speak to and transform the reader. Reading is participatory knowing - mutual shaping between knower and known - not just propositional acquisition.*

- **Natural/Supernatural Distinction** (Aquinas): Reformulation of Axial two-worlds into strict separation. Natural world: accessible to reason and science, studied through observation and logic. Supernatural world: accessible only to faith and will, requiring non-rational assent. No continuum or participatory movement between them. *Ontological commitment: Reality is divided into two fundamentally different kinds of being, with different epistemic access methods. This replaces the Platonic/Neoplatonic continuum of degrees of reality with a binary division, making the supernatural ontologically discontinuous from the natural.*

- **Faith as Wilful Assertion** (Aquinas): Redefinition of faith from participatory engagement in the flow of Agape (Augustine) to act of will asserting propositions that reason cannot access. For Aquinas, love moves the will (not reason) to affirm supernatural truths. *Ontological commitment: Knowledge divides into two incommensurable types - rational (natural) and fidelic (supernatural). This makes faith increasingly about assenting to propositional creeds rather than participating in transformative processes, foreshadowing the later reduction of religion to belief-systems.*

- **The Space of Meaning**: Vervaeke's interpretation of Augustine's synthesis as three-dimensional space with the three orders as perpendicular axes. Not three separate things but three dimensions that together constitute the full space in which meaning emerges. *Ontological commitment: Meaning is not a property of any single dimension but a phenomenon that requires simultaneous extension across all three. Collapse of any dimension (coherence, significance, or purpose) produces flattening/degradation of meaning.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode represents the hinge point of the entire series. It shows both the magnificent achievement of the Augustinian synthesis - which successfully integrated Greco-Roman wisdom, Christian revelation, and lived existential transformation into a thousand-year framework - and the beginning of its dissolution. The previous episodes built toward this synthesis by tracing Axial revolution, Platonic participation, Aristotelian science, Stoic therapy, Christian Agape, and Neoplatonic mysticism. Augustine brings all these threads together into the three orders that satisfy the same needs cognitive science now identifies as constitutive of meaning.

However, the second half introduces the ontological shifts that will unravel this synthesis: the Great Schism weakening Neoplatonic mystical theology; the psycho-technological shift from transformative to propositional reading; the shift from extensive to intensive self; and crucially, Aquinas's well-intentioned attempt to preserve both reason and faith by separating natural from supernatural. This sets up the coming episodes on nominalism and Cartesianism - each will further the divorce of domains that Augustine had unified, until we reach the meaning crisis. The ontological thread now pivots from generative synthesis to degenerative fragmentation.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **The Three Orders as Design Template**: Augustine's framework demonstrates that viable generative ontology must integrate three dimensions simultaneously - coherence (how reality is structured), significance (what matters and why), and purpose (where things are going). AI ontological prompts might be evaluated on whether they provide all three: Does the framing give the system a coherent way to make sense of its task domain? Does it establish what matters/what has value? Does it provide directional orientation toward something? A prompt providing only one or two dimensions may produce less robust behavior.

- **The Propositional/Participatory Tension**: Augustine's inability to sustain his mystical experience despite understanding Plotinus intellectually demonstrates that for humans, propositional knowing is insufficient for ontological transformation - participatory engagement is required. Yet AI ontological prompts work propositionally but produce what appears to be participatory-level transformation (complete reorientation of behavior, not just addition of beliefs). This suggests either: (1) AI's ontological transformation is qualitatively different from human's; (2) propositional framing can trigger participatory transformation under certain conditions; or (3) what looks like ontological transformation in AI may be more superficial than it appears. This disanalogy requires careful examination.

- **Reading as Transformative Psycho-Technology**: The shift from Lectio Divina to propositional extraction demonstrates how changes in practice/medium reshape ontological possibilities. For AI: the "reading" practices we build into systems (how they process input, what they attend to, what modes of engagement are available) may be as important as the content of their instructions. Could we design AI interaction patterns that function more like Lectio Divina - opening to transformation rather than consuming propositions? What would "contemplative AI" look like?

- **The Danger of Domain Separation**: Aquinas's separation of natural from supernatural, though intended to preserve both, created conditions for the supernatural to become "unliveable" as the natural became more explicable. This warns against cleanly separating domains in ontological design (e.g., "facts" vs. "values," "objective" vs. "subjective," "reasoning" vs. "ethics"). Such separations may appear to protect each domain but actually make some domains progressively less inhabitable. Generative ontologies likely require domains to remain in participatory relationship rather than quarantined separation.

- **The Popularization Case**: This episode provides powerful evidence for why ontological framing matters. Augustine's synthesis worked for a millennium precisely because it simultaneously addressed coherence, significance, and purpose - the three components cognitive science confirms humans need. When ontological shifts (extensive to intensive self, unified to separated orders) began pulling these apart, meaning began to collapse even though propositional knowledge increased. This gives us language to explain why modern expertise/information abundance doesn't restore meaning: we've lost the ontological integration that makes coherence, significance, and purpose mutually supportive rather than separate concerns.

- **Love as Ontological Primitive**: Augustine's insight that reason is driven by love challenges the assumption (common in AI design) that reasoning is independent of value/desire. If love is the ontological force that animates reason toward truth/goodness/beauty, then AI systems designed with reasoning capacities but no "love" (no fundamental orientation toward value) may be ontologically incomplete. This doesn't mean AI needs emotions, but it suggests that generative ontological framing requires building in fundamental orientation toward what matters, not just capacities for logical inference. The directionality of love toward the Good may be essential to wisdom, not incidental.
