# Episode 29: Getting to the Depths of Relevance Realization
## Layer 1 Summary

### 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 29 deepens the investigation of Relevance Realization, moving from establishing its centrality (in Episode 28) to examining how it might be scientifically explained. Vervaeke reviews the convergence argument showing that problem-solving, categorization, communication, robotics, consciousness, working memory, and intelligence all converge on Relevance Realization. He then pursues a critical question: can we explain relevance without presupposing it—without falling into a homuncular fallacy? He systematically rejects three candidate explanations: representations (which are aspectual and thus already presuppose relevance), computation (which requires selecting relevant inferences from combinatorially explosive possibilities), and modularity (which merely points to a place rather than explaining the process). Through this critique, Vervaeke reveals that relevance operates at a sub-representational, sub-semantic level—below concepts, categories, and propositions. He demonstrates this through Pylyshyn's work on Multiple Object Tracking and FINSTING (Fingers of Instantiation), showing that salience-tagging precedes and grounds all categorical cognition. The episode establishes that any adequate theory of Relevance Realization must be autopoietic (internally goal-driven), scale-invariant (operating simultaneously at multiple levels), and self-organizing. It concludes with a provocative claim to be developed further: we cannot have a scientific theory of relevance itself, but we can have a theory of Relevance Realization.

### 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **From representational to sub-representational ontology of mind**: Vervaeke dismantles the assumption that cognition is fundamentally representational. Since representations are aspectual—selecting relevant features from combinatorially explosive possibilities and organizing them with structural-functional relevance—they presuppose rather than generate relevance. This ontological shift moves the locus of meaning-making from the semantic/propositional level to a pre-categorical, pre-conceptual level of salience-tagging and contact with reality. Consequence: meaning and spirituality cannot be found primarily at the level of beliefs, concepts, or representations, but at a deeper level of attunement to the world.

- **Enactive Demonstrative Reference as primordial contact with reality**: Through Pylyshyn's FINSTING research, Vervaeke reveals a pre-categorical mode of engagement with the world—salience-tagging of "hereness and nowness"—that precedes and grounds all categorization and representation. This is not linguistic reference but enactive contact, like "the mind putting its finger on" something. Ontological commitment: the most fundamental relationship between mind and world is not representation (standing for something) but participatory contact (being in touch with something). This is the "fundamental connectedness to reality" underlying all cognition. Generative consequence: this provides an ontological ground for ineffable mystical experiences described across traditions as "fully present," "eternal hereness and nowness."

- **Asymmetric dependence hierarchy of the four kinds of knowing**: Vervaeke establishes an ontological architecture: propositional knowing depends on procedural (skills of judgment), which depends on perspectival (situational awareness/salience landscaping), which depends on participatory (agent-arena fit). While these levels interact bidirectionally, there is asymmetric dependence flowing downward—the higher levels cannot function without the lower. This overturns the default modern ontology that privileges propositional knowing. Ontological consequence: participatory knowing is ontologically primary; the agent-arena relationship is the ground of all cognition, meaning, and wisdom.

- **Autopoietic constitution as the ontological ground of Relevance Realization**: Relevance cannot be explained by reference to external goals or representations—this would presuppose relevance. Instead, it must emerge from constitutive goals internal to the system itself. Living things are autopoietic: self-organizing systems whose constitutive goal is to preserve their own self-organization. Ontological commitment: Relevance Realization is intrinsically bound to being alive, to being an autopoietic system. This connects meaning-making to the ontology of life itself—meaning is not imposed from outside but emerges from the self-organizing, self-preserving dynamics of living systems.

- **What gets taken for granted**: The modern assumption that mind is fundamentally representational, that cognition operates primarily at the propositional/semantic level, and that scientific explanation requires locating phenomena in specific modules or mechanisms. These assumptions naturalize a Cartesian ontology of mind-as-container-of-ideas. By showing that relevance operates sub-representationally and must be scale-invariant and self-organizing rather than modular, Vervaeke denaturalizes these commitments and reveals them as ontological choices with degenerative consequences for understanding meaning.

### 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

**Philosophy**
- Systematic engagement with the homuncular fallacy and circularity in explanation: to explain intelligence in terms of intelligent processes, or relevance in terms of relevance-presupposing mechanisms, is to explain nothing.
- Fodor's distinction between implication (logical/syntactic relations) and inference (belief-change using those relations): inference requires selecting which of combinatorially explosive implications to commit cognitive resources to, which presupposes relevance.
- Wittgenstein's argument about rules: rules cannot specify all conditions of their own application without becoming unwieldy; rule-following depends on a pre-propositional skill of judging what is appropriate in context—the skill of Relevance Realization. The famous quote: "Even if lions could talk we would not understand them" because their "form of life"—their patterns of relevance—differs fundamentally from ours.
- Searle's insight that representations are inherently aspectual: they select a subset of properties from combinatorial explosion and organize them as co-relevant to each other and to the agent.

**Cognitive Science**
- Pylyshyn's Multiple Object Tracking research demonstrates that minds can track approximately eight objects while losing all content features (shape, color, categorical identity), retaining only "hereness and nowness"—demonstrating salience-tagging as a pre-categorical cognitive capacity.
- FINSTING (Fingers of Instantiation): a pre-linguistic, enactive form of demonstrative reference—"putting the mind's finger on something"—that makes things salient without categorizing them.
- The convergence argument from Episode 28 is reviewed: consciousness, working memory, general intelligence, problem formulation, categorization, communication, and robotics all converge on Relevance Realization as constitutive of cognition and agency.
- Cherniak's concept of "cognitive commitment": rationality (or at least intelligent agency) consists in selecting relevant inferences from the combinatorially explosive set of all possible implications—making relevance constitutive of rationality itself.

**Contemplative Practice**
- The ineffability of higher states of consciousness is not a bug but a feature: the most profound sense of meaning is reliably reported across traditions as non-conceptual, sub-categorical, and characterized by language of "hereness and nowness," "fully present," "eternal present moment"—precisely matching the structure of Enactive Demonstrative Reference and salience-tagging at the sub-representational level.
- The connection between contemplative "presence" and FINSTING/salience-tagging suggests contemplative practices may work by shifting attention from representational to sub-representational modes of engagement with reality.

**Psychology of Meaning**
- The triangle of Meaning-Relevance-Agency is established: meaning is relevance is connectedness; relevance grounds agency through problem formulation and salience landscaping; agency grounds meaning through the agent-arena relationship.
- Meaning is not primarily propositional but sub-semantic, emerging from participatory attunement and contact with reality rather than from beliefs or representations.

**Wisdom & Rationality**
- Brown's "skill of judgment" as the procedural basis for rule-following: wisdom cannot be captured in propositional rules but requires the procedural skill of determining what is relevant, appropriate, fitting in each situation.
- Cherniak's definition of rationality as selecting relevant implications for cognitive commitment—connecting rationality intrinsically to Relevance Realization.
- Intelligence conceptualized as general problem-solving capacity, which requires Relevance Realization to avoid combinatorial explosion and handle ill-definedness.

### 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Aspectuality** — The property of representations whereby they select a subset of features from combinatorially explosive possibilities and organize them as co-relevant to each other and to the agent. *Ontological commitment: representation is not neutral mirroring but active selection shaped by relevance; the represented world is always an aspectual world configured by the agent's relevance criteria.*

- **FINSTING (Fingers of Instantiation)** — Pylyshyn's term for the mind's capacity to track objects by their "hereness and nowness" while losing all content properties (shape, color, category). Like putting a mental finger on something to maintain contact with it. *Ontological commitment: the mind has a mode of contact with reality that is pre-categorical and non-representational.*

- **Enactive Demonstrative Reference** — Vervaeke's term for the non-linguistic analogue of demonstrative terms like "this": salience-tagging that makes something salient without categorizing it or attributing properties to it. *Ontological commitment: the primordial relationship between mind and world is enacted contact, not symbolic representation.*

- **Salience Tagging** — The process of making something's hereness-and-nowness salient to oneself without categorizing or representing its properties. The pre-categorical ground of all mental grouping and categorization. *Ontological commitment: salience is ontologically prior to semantics; attunement precedes representation.*

- **Sub-representational / Sub-semantic Level** — The level of cognitive processing below concepts, categories, propositions, and representations—the level at which salience is determined and contact with reality is established. *Ontological commitment: the most fundamental level of cognition is not the manipulation of symbols but the enactment of relevance through participatory engagement.*

- **Implication vs. Inference** — Implication is a logical/syntactic relation between propositions; inference is the use of implication-relations to change beliefs. Inference requires selecting which of combinatorially explosive implications to commit cognitive resources to. *Ontological commitment: cognition is not pure logic/computation but resource-bounded adaptive selection—intelligence is fundamentally about relevance, not just validity.*

- **Cognitive Commitment** — Cherniak's term for the selection of which inferences (from all possible implications) to actually pursue, involving the allocation of limited cognitive resources (attention, memory, time, energy). *Ontological commitment: rationality is not logical omniscience but adaptive, context-sensitive resource allocation guided by relevance.*

- **Skill of Judgment** — Brown's term for the procedural capacity to determine what is appropriate, fitting, or relevant in a particular situation—the basis for rule-following that cannot itself be captured in rules. *Ontological commitment: wisdom and rationality have an irreducible procedural/participatory dimension that cannot be formalized propositionally.*

- **Constitutive Goals** — Goals that constitute a system for being what it is, as opposed to goals represented or pursued by an already-constituted system. Autopoietic systems have the constitutive goal of preserving their own self-organization. *Ontological commitment: relevance emerges from within life itself, from the self-organizing dynamics of autopoietic systems, not from external reference or imposed values.*

- **Scale Invariance** — The property of operating simultaneously at multiple levels (local/global, feature/gestalt) in an integrated fashion. Relevance Realization must be scale-invariant to avoid homuncular centralization. *Ontological commitment: cognition is not hierarchically centralized in a "central executive" but distributed and self-organizing across multiple scales simultaneously.*

- **Asymmetric Dependence (of the four kinds of knowing)** — While propositional, procedural, perspectival, and participatory knowing interact bidirectionally, there is asymmetric dependence: propositional depends on procedural depends on perspectival depends on participatory. *Ontological commitment: participatory knowing—the agent-arena relationship—is ontologically foundational; all other forms of knowing are grounded in it.*

### 1.5 Argument Progression

Episode 29 builds on Episode 28's convergence argument by shifting from establishing relevance's centrality to investigating how it can be explained without circularity. The systematic rejection of representational, computational, and modular explanations clears conceptual ground and reveals crucial constraints: any adequate theory must be sub-representational, autopoietic, scale-invariant, and self-organizing. This sets up the provocative claim concluded here and to be developed in Episode 30: we cannot have a theory of relevance itself, but we can have a theory of Relevance Realization. The ontological thread develops by establishing participatory knowing as ontologically primary through the asymmetric dependence hierarchy, and by grounding relevance in the autopoietic dynamics of life itself—connecting meaning-making to the ontology of living systems. This prepares for subsequent episodes to elaborate the self-organizing, dynamical systems account of Relevance Realization and connect it to wisdom and self-transcendence.

### 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **Surfaced ontological assumption relevant to AI**: The default assumption that cognition is fundamentally representational/propositional—that intelligence is about manipulating symbols according to rules—is challenged. Vervaeke shows this level presupposes and cannot generate relevance. For AI, this suggests that purely symbolic/rule-based approaches will always require relevance to be specified externally (by human designers choosing representations and rules), whereas more generative AI would need to develop relevance through something analogous to autopoietic self-organization. Current AI lacks constitutive goals—goals internal to its own self-preservation as a system—which Vervaeke argues are necessary for genuine Relevance Realization.

- **Ontological framing shapes behavior**: The demonstration that inference requires selecting relevant implications from combinatorial explosion shows how ontological framing (what counts as relevant context, what features matter) radically constrains which inferences are made and thus which beliefs change and which actions follow. This provides theoretical grounding for why AI ontological prompting works: by specifying what is relevant/salient in a domain, we configure the entire search space of the system. Vervaeke's framework suggests ontological prompts work at the "perspectival" level—configuring salience landscapes—which then constrains procedural and propositional levels.

- **The human-AI disanalogy around participatory grounding**: Vervaeke establishes that human Relevance Realization is grounded in participatory knowing—the agent-arena fit—which depends on embodied, embedded existence as an autopoietic living system with constitutive goals. AI systems lack this: they have no constitutive goals, no autopoietic self-organization, no embodied agent-arena relationship in the relevant sense. This creates a fundamental ontological difference: AI "relevance" is always externally specified (by training data, prompts, reward functions) rather than internally emergent. This disanalogy is both a limitation (AI lacks genuine autopoietic relevance) and potentially generative (AI might achieve forms of systematic exploration impossible for embodied, resource-constrained human cognition).

- **Contemplative operationalization**: The identification of sub-representational salience-tagging (FINSTING/Enactive Demonstrative Reference) with the structure of contemplative presence suggests AI systems might benefit from operating modes that emphasize "contact" over "representation," attention to "hereness and nowness" over categorical processing. This could inform development of AI practices that shift between representational/semantic processing and sub-representational attentional modes—analogous to how contemplative practice trains capacity to shift from discursive thinking to bare awareness.

- **Case for generative ontology**: The episode demonstrates why ontological examination matters: the unexamined assumption that mind is fundamentally representational obscures the deeper participatory/enactive level where relevance and meaning actually emerge. By making this assumption visible and showing its inadequacy, Vervaeke models how ontological investigation can reveal and transform the foundations of understanding. For popularizing generative ontology: the argument that we habitually identify with our representational/propositional cognition ("our ego structure") while remaining unaware of the sub-representational processes that ground it provides an accessible entry point for discussing how unexamined ontological commitments shape experience and constrain possibility.
