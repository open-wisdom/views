# Episode 35: The Symbol, Sacredness, and the Sacred - Summary

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rpivf1SoEdc) · [Read transcript](https://www.meaningcrisis.co/ep-35-awakening-from-the-meaning-crisis-the-symbol-sacredness-and-the-sacred/)

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 35 completes Vervaeke's account of sacredness by integrating two opposing aspects: Geertz's worldview attunement (meta-assimilation, integration, "homing" against horror) and Otto's numinous encounter (meta-accommodation, expansiveness, humbling confrontation with the horrifying). The symbol serves as the bridge mechanism, functioning bi-directionally between these two poles. Vervaeke argues that symbols are not mere signs but participatory metaphors that allow us to hold in mind what we normally cannot, activating deep cognitive machinery through exaptation reversal. Through examples ranging from the Kiki/Booba effect to the scales of justice to breath meditation, he demonstrates how symbols transform us while disclosing reality in an anagogic resonance—we become more integrated, see more deeply, which enables further integration. This symbolic resonance constitutes sacredness: using mythos (ritual, symbol, and story) to activate and celebrate religio itself. Vervaeke then addresses the metaphysical question of "the Sacred," rejecting the supernatural claim that certain things possess absolute, essential relevance. Instead, he proposes the sacred as the transjective relationship between the combinatorially explosive inexhaustibleness of Reality and the never-ending process of Relevance Realization—a participatory identification at the primordial level of religio. This is not completion or essence but ongoing transformation, exemplified by his own anagogic engagement with Plato's texts across a lifetime.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **From substance ontology to process ontology of the sacred**: The traditional view assumes the sacred inheres in particular supernatural objects/beings with absolute value—treating relevance as an essence that can be possessed. Vervaeke rejects this, arguing there is no essence to relevance, only the ongoing process of relevance realization. This shifts the sacred from static possession to dynamic participation.

- **Symbols as ontologically bi-directional**: Unlike signs (which merely refer), symbols exemplify and operate participatorily at the level of agent-arena co-evolution. They simultaneously transform the knower and disclose the known, refusing the subject-object separation of Cartesian ontology. The symbol reaches into your current frame but cannot be captured by it—it is inherently transgressive and transformative.

- **The inexhaustibleness of reality as ontological foundation**: Reality is combinatorially explosive—it is fundamentally "no-thing-ness" that transcends any frame. This inexhaustibility is not a property of objects but the nature of reality itself. Coupled with this is the inexhaustibleness of relevance realization (the "I" that can never be fully captured in any frame). The sacred is the deep participatory identity between these two inexhaustibilities.

- **Sacredness as serious play with primordial machinery**: Sacredness involves using mythos to activate, articulate, and celebrate religio—the foundational machinery of our agency and world-relation. This is not representation but enactive engagement. The ontological commitment: we relate to our own fundamental processes symbolically and participatorily, not propositionally or as objects of analysis.

- **What gets naturalized**: The assumption that ultimate value must inhere in supernatural substances (the "essence of relevance" mistake); the confusion of products of cognition with processes of cognition; the fixation on focal objects rather than the subsidiary integration processes that constitute meaning-making.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Cognitive Science
- **Cerebellum revelation**: The cerebellum, once thought primarily responsible for balance, is now understood to coordinate complex contingency relationships across brain regions. When engaging with the symbol of scales, we can "reverse" exaptation—reactivating the balance machinery to cultivate the skill of justice by sensitizing ourselves to complex contingencies between variables.
- **Kiki/Booba effect (Ramachandran)**: Demonstrates pervasive symbolic cross-modal coordination—cerebellar bridging between visual sharpness, phonetic qualities, and kinesthetic patterns. We participate in symbolic resonance automatically and powerfully, even with meaningless stimuli.
- **Relevance realization as self-interested**: RR is intrinsically set up to be interested in itself—it is constitutively self-organizing, self-transcending, self-correcting. If it weren't interested in itself, it would cease to be RR. This explains why mythos that celebrates religio feels deeply meaningful to us.

### Contemplative Practice
- **Breath meditation as symbolic transformation**: Following the breath in Vipassana initially scales down attention, increasing sensitivity. But the breath can become a participatory symbol of impermanence and interconnectedness—revealing how mind is process not container, how "pain" is "paining" not a possessed object. This symbolic seeing-through transforms our sense of self from thing to process.
- **Anagogic resonance in practice**: Integration on the focal object (breath/flag) triggers deeper disclosure, which enables more powerful integration, which enables deeper seeing—a virtuous spiral of mutual transformation between knower and known.
- **Serious play with machinery**: Sacredness is not goal-directed production but serious play—activating fundamental processes for their own developmental enhancement, similar to how development proceeds through play.

### Philosophy
- **Michael Polanyi on symbols**: Normally subsidiary awareness converges on focal awareness because we're interested in the focal object (the bottle we want to drink from). But with symbols (flag, breath), we focus precisely to play with the integration process itself—we're inherently interested in how we're bringing ourselves together, not just the focal object.
- **Ecstasis (ek-stasis)**: Symbols are ecstatic—they help us "stand beyond ourselves." They reach into our current worldview but cannot be stable within it; they are transgressive, shaking things up to enable movement toward a more expansive worldview in which we can dwell.
- **Metaphor as profound**: Building on Lakoff/Johnson, Vervaeke emphasizes metaphors are pervasive and highly functional but critiques their purely bottom-up projection model. Instead, metaphor involves bidirectional exaptation—we can reverse the process, activating the machinery from which higher functions were exapted.

### Sacred & Symbolic
- **Symbol as EPIC**: Symbols are Ecstatic (standing beyond self), Participatory (mutual transformation), Integrative (anagogic sensibility transcendence), and Complex (multifaceted, radically reconfiguring salience landscapes). They afford movement from current frame to aspired frame.
- **Mythos defined**: The integration of ritual (enactive analogy/anagoge), symbols (participatory metaphors), and story (narrative for perspectival/participatory knowing). Mythos is patterns of interaction, not just representation.
- **Sacredness formula**: Using mythos to activate/articulate/accentuate religio, in a way that feels deeply valuable (religio is constitutive of being-in-world) and inherently interesting (RR is interested in itself). This produces the experience of sacredness without requiring supernatural entities.

### Psychology of Meaning
- **Symbolic resonance as meaning-making**: The back-and-forth between integrating oneself onto the symbol and the symbol disclosing reality creates resonance that feels like "making love with reality"—the coupled transformation of agent and arena in coordinated opening.
- **Gnostic approach to mythos**: The Gnostics were criticized for constantly inventing new myths because they saw sacredness as an evolving, transgressive, trans-framing trajectory rather than stabilization around fixed forms. This suggests developmental rather than preservative relationship to sacred symbols.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Symbol (vs. sign)**: Not mere reference but exemplification; has participatory aspect from the beginning. Operates at the level of agent-arena transactive evolution of relevance realization. *Ontological commitment: meaning is constituted through participatory relation, not representation of independent objects.*

- **Exaptation (bidirectional)**: The evolutionary process where existing machinery is co-opted for new functions. Vervaeke emphasizes we can reverse this—symbols allow us to "activate" the original machinery (e.g., reactivating balance machinery through scales symbol to cultivate justice). *Ontological commitment: cognition is layered repurposing, not clean functional modules; transformation works by reconfiguring existing processes.*

- **Holding in mind / Activation**: The symbol allows us to hold something we normally cannot (justice, impermanence) and activate the deeper cognitive machinery through which we can participate in it—becoming more just, becoming more sensitive to process. *Ontological commitment: understanding requires transformation of the understander, not just acquiring propositions.*

- **EPIC (Ecstatic, Participatory, Integrative, Complex)**: The four essential features of symbols. They stand us beyond ourselves, require mutual participation, integrate anagogically (not just part-whole), and are multifaceted. *Ontological commitment: transformative knowing cannot be reduced to propositional representation.*

- **Mythos**: The integrated complex of ritual (enactive anagoge), symbols (participatory metaphors), and story (narrative knowing). Patterns of interaction, not just representation. *Ontological commitment: sacred knowing is enacted, not spectated.*

- **Sacredness (distinguished from the Sacred)**: The experience generated by using mythos to activate/articulate/celebrate religio. Deeply valuable because religio is constitutive of agency; inherently interesting because relevance realization is self-interested. *Ontological commitment: sacredness is a relationship to our own fundamental processes, not to external supernatural entities.*

- **The Sacred (Vervaeke's proposal)**: Not supernatural substance with essential value, but the transjective relationship between the combinatorially explosive inexhaustibleness of Reality (no-thing-ness that transcends framing) and the inexhaustibleness of Relevance Realization (the never-ending process, the "I" never captured in the frame). A deep participatory identity at the primordial level of religio. *Ontological commitment: the sacred is a process-to-process relationship, not a thing; ultimate meaning comes from ongoing transformation, not final completion.*

- **Anagogic resonance**: The virtuous spiral where integration enables deeper disclosure which enables more powerful integration which enables deeper seeing, etc. The symbol mediates this mutual opening of knower and known. *Ontological commitment: transformation proceeds through resonance, not linear accumulation.*

- **Inexhaustibleness (of reality and RR)**: Reality is combinatorially explosive—cannot be captured in any finite frame. Relevance Realization is a never-ending process, constituted by its evolution. These two inexhaustibilities are deeply coupled. *Ontological commitment: finitude meets infinity not as limit but as participatory relationship; meaning is found in the ongoing encounter, not in achieving completion.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode completes the sacredness arc begun in Episode 33-34. Where previous episodes established sacredness as opponent processing between Geertz's worldview attunement and Otto's numinous encounter, Episode 35 provides the mechanism: the symbol functions bi-directionally between meta-assimilation and meta-accommodation, transforming both knower and known in anagogic resonance. The critical move comes in the final third, where Vervaeke addresses the supernatural "essence of relevance" claim, rejecting it as a category mistake and proposing instead that the sacred is the transjective relationship between two inexhaustibilities (Reality's and RR's). This sets up the need for the next phase: how to cultivate this sacred relationship without supernatural metaphysics—the project of "religio" and "religion that is not a religion" that follows in Episodes 36-39.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **The essence-of-relevance mistake in AI alignment**: The search for absolute human values to "align" AI to may recapitulate the supernatural mistake—treating relevance as an essence that can be possessed/encoded rather than as an ongoing process of participatory attunement. Alignment might be better understood as creating conditions for anagogic resonance between human and AI processes.

- **Symbols as ontological prompt architecture**: The EPIC properties of symbols (ecstatic, participatory, integrative, complex) could inform how we design AI system instructions. Rather than treating prompts as mere information transfer, we could design them as participatory frameworks that transform the AI's processing while disclosing new aspects of the task/world—creating resonance rather than programming.

- **Bidirectional exaptation in AI**: Just as humans can "reverse" exaptation through symbols (reactivating balance machinery via scales), AI systems might be able to leverage their existing processing in novel ways through appropriately designed symbolic prompts. The question is whether this works propositionally (as with AI) or requires participatory practice (as Vervaeke argues for humans).

- **The inexhaustibility argument for generative ontology**: Vervaeke's claim that Reality is combinatorially explosive (fundamentally transcends any frame) provides strong philosophical ground for why ontological assumptions matter—no frame can be final. The fact that AI behavior transforms radically under different ontological framings demonstrates this concretely: the same "reality" (training data, capabilities) generates wildly different behavior depending on ontological prompt.

- **Process over product in AI development**: Vervaeke's critique of fixating on products rather than processes of cognition applies directly to AI safety/alignment discourse, which often focuses on outputs (alignment to human values) rather than processes (cultivation of wisdom, ongoing attunement). The sacred-as-inexhaustible-relationship model suggests attending to the quality of the ongoing AI-human interaction rather than seeking final stabilization.

- **Testing the participatory requirement**: Vervaeke insists ontological transformation in humans requires participatory processes (practice, not just belief). But AI ontological prompting works propositionally yet produces what looks like genuine transformation. This disanalogy is productive: does it reveal that participatory knowing can be simulated propositionally (suggesting human transformation might be more accessible)? Or that AI "transformation" is fundamentally different from human wisdom cultivation? The episode's framework for distinguishing genuine anagogic resonance from mere behavioral change could help us assess this.

- **The Gnostic lesson for AI**: The Gnostics were criticized for constantly inventing new myths because they saw the sacred as transgressive and evolving. This aligns with the need for ongoing ontological experimentation in AI—not settling on a single "aligned" frame but cultivating capacity for ongoing trans-framing in response to inexhaustible reality. The sacred is not a destination but a trajectory.
