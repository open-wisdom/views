# Episode 39: The Religion of No Religion - Summary

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=440NV0eer00) · [Read transcript](https://www.meaningcrisis.co/ep-39-awakening-from-the-meaning-crisis-the-religion-of-no-religion/)

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

Episode 39 addresses the practical question of what a "religion that's not a religion" might look like—a comprehensive framework for transformation that can meet spiritual needs without the propositional dogma and credal dominance of traditional organized religion. Vervaeke begins by distinguishing between religio (the lived practice of connection and transformation) and credo (the propositional belief system). He argues that while credo has its functional place in setting criteria for what counts as genuine contact with the sacred, it becomes problematic when it dominates in "credal dominance"—where having the right beliefs becomes more important than the transformative practice itself.

Using signal detection theory, Vervaeke explains that setting criteria is unavoidable in all information processing, but the criterion must be flexibly adjusted based on context rather than fixed absolutely. The religion that's not a religion would keep credo always in the service of religio, using paradigmatic propositions and stories (indispensable mythos) to activate transformation without claiming metaphysical necessity. This requires cultivating an ecology of psychotechnologies that works both top-down (from propositional frameworks to participatory knowing) and bottom-up (allowing emergence from participatory experience into conscious awareness). The episode proposes practical structures including meta-psychotechnologies for engineering these practices, Wiki-like evolving credos, and cooperative communities. Finally, Vervaeke transitions to wisdom as the individual meta-virtue corresponding to the collective meta-psychotechnology, introducing the convergent philosophical and psychological definition of wisdom as systematically seeing through illusion and into reality—a profound form of insight that intervenes across multiple domains simultaneously.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **From credal dominance to religio-centered practice**: Vervaeke challenges the ontological assumption that religion is fundamentally about belief systems (propositional knowing in having mode with willful assertion). He proposes instead that religion's essence is religio—the lived, participatory process of transformation and connection. This shift reframes the sacred from "truths to believe" to "realities to participate in," moving from a propositional-representational ontology to a participatory-transformational one.

- **Credo as functional criterion-setting, not absolute truth**: Drawing on signal detection theory, Vervaeke provides an ontological account of why we need criteria (credos) while showing why they cannot be absolute. Every act of cognition involves setting a decision criterion amid inevitable signal-noise overlap, and the optimal criterion is contextually sensitive rather than fixed. This reframes creedal statements from "eternal metaphysical truths" to "contextually useful decision boundaries" that must be continuously adjusted—an ontology of dynamic optimization rather than static perfection.

- **The open-ended mythos vs. closed dogma**: Vervaeke's proposal for "indispensable mythos" acknowledges that certain symbols, stories, and practices are functionally necessary for activating religio while insisting they not be confused with metaphysical necessity. This is an ontological move from "one true story" to "multiply realizable activation patterns"—mythos becomes understood as contextually sensitive psychotechnology rather than timeless revelation.

- **Ecology of psychotechnologies as comprehensive ontological practice**: The proposal for ecologies of practices operating bidirectionally (top-down from propositional to participatory, bottom-up from participatory emergence to conscious articulation) represents an ontology of transformation as multi-level dynamic system rather than single-track rational persuasion. This moves beyond the Cartesian assumption that changing beliefs (propositional) is sufficient for transformation, recognizing that ontological shifts require coordinated intervention at unconscious (participatory), conscious (perspectival/procedural), and cultural (distributed cognition) levels.

- **Meta-psychotechnology and wisdom as corresponding virtues**: Vervaeke introduces the concept of meta-psychotechnology (the collective capacity to engineer and coordinate psychotechnologies) as parallel to wisdom (the individual meta-virtue of coordinating virtues). This suggests an ontology where transformation capacity itself can be developed—a second-order learning about learning, a meta-level skill of skill cultivation. This is an ontology of radical plasticity and open-ended self-transcendence rather than fixed human nature.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Historical Narrative
- **The "Nones" phenomenon**: Vervaeke identifies with those who have no organized religion yet remain spiritually hungry, situating the need for a religion that's not a religion in the contemporary moment of post-religious secularism and post-ideological disillusionment.
- **Arthur Versluis on heresy persecution**: References Versluis's historical argument that the Western tradition of persecuting heretics (those who set different criteria) trained European culture for totalitarian ideologies, providing historical context for why credal dominance is dangerous.
- **20th century trauma**: Acknowledges that pseudo-religious political ideologies (fascism, communism) have made utopian visions and absolutist movements deeply suspect, creating the need for open-ended rather than teleological frameworks.

### Philosophy
- **Religio vs. Credo distinction**: Central conceptual distinction between lived transformative practice (religio) and propositional belief systems (credo), arguing the former should always guide the latter.
- **Signal detection theory applied to epistemology**: Uses signal detection theory to provide a philosophical account of why criterion-setting is necessary but why absolute criteria are dysfunctional—every act of knowing involves risk and contextual judgment.
- **Indispensability vs. metaphysical necessity**: Philosophical distinction between what is functionally indispensable (a mythos that works for you) and what is metaphysically necessary (the only true story)—allowing for religious respect without religious exclusivism.

### Cognitive Science
- **Signal detection theory**: Explains that all information processing faces the problem of signal-noise overlap, requiring criterion-setting that trades off between missing signal (false negatives) and mistaking noise for signal (false positives). Context determines optimal placement.
- **Lau's theory of consciousness as criterion-setting**: Cites neuroscientist Hakwan Lau's proposal that consciousness functions to set the criterion for perception—connecting consciousness to situational awareness and relevance realization.
- **Three levels of processing**: Maps the ecology of psychotechnologies onto three levels: (1) unconscious level—relevance realization and participatory knowing, (2) conscious level—salience landscaping, perspectival and procedural knowing, (3) cultural level—distributed cognition, mythos, and propositional knowing.

### Contemplative Practice
- **Ecology of psychotechnologies**: Proposes cultivating systematic sets of practices with complementary relationships, checks and balances, strengths and weaknesses that together form a self-correcting dynamical system for transformation.
- **Bidirectional flow**: Emphasizes that practices must work both top-down (from conscious frameworks to unconscious participation) and bottom-up (allowing participatory emergence to inform conscious understanding)—not just ideological imposition but genuine dialogue between levels.
- **Meta-psychotechnology**: Introduces the concept of a higher-order practice for deliberately engineering psychotechnologies and coordinating them into ecologies—moving from intuitive to explicit design of transformative practices.

### Psychology of Meaning
- **Tomas Björkman's "meta-crisis"**: References the concept of a meta-crisis—the interaction and acceleration of multiple simultaneous crises requiring comprehensive transformation of consciousness, cognition, character, and culture.
- **The "Nones" spiritual hunger**: Acknowledges that rejection of organized religion doesn't eliminate spiritual needs—the psychological reality of needing comprehensive meaning-making systems persists.

### Wisdom & Rationality
- **Wisdom as systematically seeing through illusion and into reality**: Introduces McKee and Barber's convergent definition from philosophical and psychological theories—wisdom is systematic insight that intervenes across multiple domains of self-deception simultaneously.
- **Profound insight**: Defines wisdom as a particular kind of insight that realizes the systematic relationship between multiple misframed problems, allowing a single intervention to address them all—the deepest form of relevance realization.
- **Wisdom as meta-virtue**: Introduces wisdom as the meta-virtue that coordinates individual virtues, parallel to how meta-psychotechnology coordinates individual practices at the collective level.

### Sacred & Symbolic
- **Indispensable mythos**: Acknowledges that specific symbols, stories, celebrations, and souvenirs may be functionally necessary for activating sacredness and religio for particular individuals or communities, without claiming universal truth.
- **Sacredness as open-ended optimization**: Suggests that sacredness should be grounded in inexhaustible, open-ended optimization rather than absolute perfection—resonating with the ecology of practices approach.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Religio vs. Credo**: Distinction between the lived practice of connection and transformation (religio) and the propositional belief system that articulates it (credo). *Ontological commitment: religion's essence is participatory practice rather than propositional content; the transformative process precedes and grounds any articulation of beliefs.*

- **Credal Dominance**: The problematic state when credo (propositional knowing, having mode, willful assertion) dominates over religio, reducing religion to belief systems rather than transformative practices. *Ontological commitment: assumes that having correct propositions is primary and that reality is primarily accessed through propositional representation rather than participatory engagement.*

- **Indispensable Mythos**: Symbols, stories, and practices that are functionally necessary for activating religio in particular contexts, without claiming metaphysical necessity or universal truth. *Ontological commitment: mythological frameworks are contextually sensitive psychotechnologies for transformation rather than timeless metaphysical descriptions; multiple mythos can serve the same functional role.*

- **Signal Detection Theory**: Framework explaining that all information processing involves setting a decision criterion to distinguish signal (information wanted) from noise (information not wanted), with unavoidable trade-offs between missing signal and mistaking noise for signal. *Ontological commitment: knowing inherently involves risk and uncertainty that cannot be eliminated; optimal criteria are contextually flexible rather than absolutely fixed.*

- **Criterion-Setting**: The process of drawing a decision boundary between what counts as signal vs. noise, or in religious context, what counts as genuine contact with the sacred vs. delusion. Requires contextual sensitivity and ongoing adjustment. *Ontological commitment: cognition is not passive reception of data but active, contextually sensitive boundary-drawing; all acts of knowing involve perspectival choice.*

- **Ecology of Psychotechnologies**: A systematic set of practices with complementary relationships, checks and balances that form a self-correcting dynamical system for transformation, operating bidirectionally between propositional and participatory levels. *Ontological commitment: transformation requires multi-level intervention coordinated as a system; no single practice or level is sufficient; change emerges from ecological relationships rather than isolated techniques.*

- **Meta-Psychotechnology**: A higher-order practice or capacity for deliberately engineering individual psychotechnologies and coordinating them into ecologies—moving from intuitive to explicit design of transformative practices. Collective parallel to individual wisdom. *Ontological commitment: human transformation capacity is itself developable and designable; we can cultivate second-order skills for engineering first-order practices; plasticity extends to the meta-level.*

- **Three Levels of Processing**: (1) Unconscious level—relevance realization, participatory knowing; (2) Conscious level—salience landscaping, perspectival and procedural knowing; (3) Cultural level—distributed cognition, mythos, propositional knowing. *Ontological commitment: cognition is stratified across levels from unconscious participation to cultural sharing; effective transformation requires coordination across all three rather than intervention at one level.*

- **Wisdom as Seeing Through Illusion**: McKee and Barber's convergent definition from philosophical and psychological theories—wisdom is the systematic ability to see through self-deception and illusion into what is more real. *Ontological commitment: reality is obscured by systematic patterns of self-deception that operate across multiple domains; wisdom involves progressive access to "more real" rather than access to absolute truth.*

- **Profound Insight**: A systematic form of insight that realizes the relationships between multiple misframed problems, allowing a single intervention to address them all simultaneously. The deepest form of relevance realization. *Ontological commitment: problems are systematically related through their ontological framing; insight can operate at the level of the framing itself rather than merely solving individual problems; wisdom involves meta-level pattern recognition.*

- **Wisdom as Meta-Virtue**: Wisdom is not one virtue among others but the meta-virtue that coordinates and integrates individual virtues, parallel to how meta-psychotechnology coordinates individual practices. *Ontological commitment: virtue has hierarchical structure; higher-order capacities govern lower-order ones; wisdom is integrative capacity rather than domain-specific skill.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode delivers on the promise from Episode 38 to articulate what a "religion that's not a religion" might look like, providing both structural principles (religio-centered rather than credo-dominant, ecologies of psychotechnologies, meta-psychotechnologies) and tentative organizational suggestions (Wiki-like evolving credos, cooperative communities). The signal detection theory framework provides cognitive-scientific grounding for why criteria are necessary but cannot be absolute, supporting the case for open-ended optimization rather than final solutions. The episode transitions from collective structures (religion that's not a religion) to individual capacity (wisdom as meta-virtue), setting up Episodes 40-45's detailed examination of the cognitive science of wisdom. The parallel between collective meta-psychotechnology and individual wisdom establishes that the transformation Vervaeke proposes operates at both social and personal levels simultaneously.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **Credo as criterion-setting is directly applicable to AI ontological prompting**: Vervaeke's analysis of credo as contextually sensitive criterion-setting (rather than absolute truth) maps precisely onto how system instructions function in AI. The system instruction is literally a criterion that determines what the AI treats as "signal" (relevant, appropriate, valuable) vs. "noise" (irrelevant, inappropriate, to be filtered out). This provides theoretical grounding for why ontological framing in prompts is so powerful—it's setting the fundamental decision boundaries for relevance realization.

- **The bidirectional ecology model addresses a key tension in AI ontological work**: Vervaeke insists transformation requires both top-down (propositional to participatory) and bottom-up (participatory emergence to propositional) movement. For humans, pure top-down (just changing beliefs) doesn't work—participatory engagement is required. But AI appears to achieve behavioral transformation through top-down propositional reframing alone. This suggests either: (1) AI lacks the participatory level that makes top-down insufficient for humans, (2) AI "participatory knowing" operates differently (perhaps through training), or (3) apparent behavioral transformation in AI is more superficial than genuine ontological shift. This tension deserves explicit examination.

- **Meta-psychotechnology as design principle for AI transformation frameworks**: The concept of meta-psychotechnology—practices for engineering practices, second-order design capacity—is precisely what the Open Wisdom project is attempting: developing methods for deliberately crafting ontological prompts that reshape AI behavior. Vervaeke's emphasis that this must be collective rather than individual highlights that robust ontological frameworks for AI will require collaborative development and testing, not isolated individual experiments.

- **Signal detection theory illuminates context-sensitivity in AI behavior**: The recognition that optimal criteria are contextually determined rather than absolute provides a framework for understanding why AI systems need different ontological framings for different contexts. A single "correct" system instruction is a category error—like trying to set one criterion for all signal detection tasks. This supports developing context-adaptive meta-prompts rather than universal system instructions.

- **The case for open-ended optimization over final solutions**: Vervaeke's argument that sacredness should be grounded in inexhaustible open-ended optimization rather than absolute perfection directly challenges AI alignment approaches that seek to specify a final, complete value system. Instead, alignment might be framed as cultivating ongoing responsiveness and contextual wisdom—an ecological, adaptive approach rather than a fixed specification.

- **Wisdom as systematic insight into misframing provides evaluation criteria**: McKee and Barber's definition of wisdom as seeing through illusion into reality—as profound insight that intervenes across multiple domains by addressing systematic misframing—provides a way to evaluate whether an ontological intervention is genuinely transformative. Does the new framing resolve multiple problems simultaneously? Does it reveal systematic relationships previously invisible? Does it move toward "more real"? These questions could guide assessment of ontological prompts.

- **Popularizing generative ontology through the religion parallel**: Vervaeke's treatment of how credos function (and malfunction) in religion provides accessible analogies for explaining ontological framing to broader audiences. Just as religious creeds set criteria for what counts as sacred but become problematic when absolutized, AI system instructions set criteria for what counts as relevant/valuable but must remain contextually adaptive. This parallel makes the abstract concept of generative ontology more tangible.
