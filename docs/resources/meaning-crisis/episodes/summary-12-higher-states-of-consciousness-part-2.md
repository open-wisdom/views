# Episode 12: Higher States of Consciousness, Part 2 - Summary

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rvx4_0NAfaY) · [Read transcript](https://www.meaningcrisis.co/ep-12-awakening-from-the-meaning-crisis-higher-states-of-consciousness-part-2-2/)

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

This episode completes Vervaeke's cognitive-scientific account of higher states of consciousness by establishing both their descriptive adequacy (how they work) and prescriptive adequacy (why they justify transformation). Building on the Continuity Hypothesis, he proposes that higher states represent a flow state applied to the domain-general skill of optimal grip itself—a recursive optimization of cognition's fundamental machinery. At the psychological level, radical decentering exapts the self's integrative machinery onto the world, explaining both the profound sense of at-oneness and the enhanced pattern detection. At the computational level, disruptive strategies prevent overfitting while revealing invariant patterns, mirroring processes in machine learning. At the neural level, frontal-parietal hyperactivity followed by hypoactivity, mediated by enhanced thalamic integration, produces metastability—a brain state of simultaneous integration and differentiation that enables complexification and emergent cognitive functions. The episode culminates in a theory of plausibility: higher states optimize the very machinery by which the brain evaluates the trustworthiness, power, and fluency of its processing, making them indispensable (not certain, but optimal) guides for transformation. Crucially, Vervaeke distinguishes the participatory wisdom gained in these states from the propositional claims people make afterward—the former is transformative and reliable when embedded in sapiential traditions, the latter often contradictory and useless.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **From self-as-entity to self-as-process**: The self is revealed not as a thing or substance but as a set of integrative functions that complexify processing by "gluing" information together. This shifts the ontology from Cartesian self-substance to dynamical self-organization, enabling the recognition that the self's machinery can be exapted for new purposes (turning integration outward onto the world rather than inward onto ego).

- **Radical decentering as ontological disclosure**: The systematic decentering in higher states doesn't merely change perspective (epistemology) but reconfigures the participatory relationship between self and world. The "reciprocal revelation" of world and self transforms the knower-known relationship from separation to intimate conformity—what Vervaeke calls "knowing by loving." This is an ontological move from subject-object dualism to participatory co-constitution.

- **Plausibility as ontological evaluation**: Vervaeke introduces plausibility (trustworthiness × elegance × fluency) as the brain's fundamental means of evaluating what is real. This challenges the Cartesian-empiricist ontology that privileges certainty and propositional knowledge. The shift is profound: reality is not what can be proven with certainty but what demonstrates convergent evidence (trustworthiness), multi-apt applicability (elegance), and efficient processing (fluency). Higher states optimize this evaluative machinery, granting them "indispensability" and "priority."

- **Participatory knowing as distinct from propositional knowing**: The episode's crucial ontological distinction is that higher states afford participatory transformation (character change, wisdom cultivation) rather than propositional knowledge (metaphysical claims). This challenges the post-Cartesian assumption that valid knowledge must be articulable in propositions. People return from identical experiences with contradictory metaphysical claims ("God exists" / "God does not exist"), revealing that what's transmitted is not propositional content but a transformed way of being.

- **Complexification as the mechanism of self-transcendence**: The brain's metastability—simultaneously integrating and differentiating—produces emergent capacities. This grounds self-transcendence not in mystical metaphysics but in the ontology of complex adaptive systems. You transcend your current self not by accessing another realm but by increasing your own organizational complexity, which generates genuinely novel abilities. This is a naturalistic ontology of transformation that preserves the phenomenology of transcendence.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Cognitive Science
- **Flow state in optimal grip**: Higher states are theorized as flow applied to the domain-general skill of getting an optimal grip on reality, explaining enhanced implicit learning, pattern detection, and fluency.
- **Self as integrative machinery**: Drawing on Sui and Humphrey (2015), the self functions as "glue" that makes disparate information relevant to each other by making it self-relevant, enabling complexified processing.
- **Exaptation of cognitive machinery**: Following Michael Anderson's framework, the integrative machinery normally used for self-construction can be exapted to disclose the world, explaining the radical shift in processing during mystical experience.
- **Disruptive strategies and overfitting**: Neural networks require randomization and "dropout" to avoid overfitting to sample data and discover real invariants that generalize (Woodward et al., 2014)—a computational parallel to contemplative disruption.
- **Metastability and complexification**: Psilocybin research (Lauderdale, building on Kelso and Tognoli) shows that psychedelics induce metastable brain states that simultaneously integrate and differentiate, enabling emergent cognitive functions through complexification.

### Psychology of Meaning
- **The Solomon Effect**: Igor Grossmann's research demonstrates that decentering strategies (describing personal problems from third-person perspective) induce insight and wisdom by breaking the systematic error of egocentrism.
- **Systematic error vs. local error**: Higher states reveal not isolated mistakes but systematic misframing—like developmental stage transitions (drawing on work with Juensung Kim), enabling transformation of entire classes of problems rather than single issues.
- **Stuckness and breakthrough**: Problems where people are "stuck" reflect egocentrically locked framing; radical decentering can precipitate frame-breaking insight that resolves the impasse.
- **Love as mutual accelerating disclosure**: The profound coupling of self and world in higher states mirrors the structure of love—reciprocal, conforming, mutually revealing—making these states instances of "knowing by loving," a participatory rather than propositional mode.

### Contemplative Practice
- **Disruptive strategies as systematic practice**: Mindfulness psychotechnologies systematically employ decentering and de-automatization to increase variation in processing, revealing both real invariants and systematic biases.
- **The danger of autodidactic practice**: Pursuing higher states without embedding in wisdom traditions risks egocentric echo chambers and inability to integrate traumatic transformations; the Buddha's parable of the monkey trapped in pitch illustrates self-defeating autodidactic pursuit.
- **The necessity of Sangha**: Despite the Buddha's individual achievement, he insisted on community (Sangha) as essential for cultivation—challenging Western individualism and pointing to the social-institutional dimension of wisdom.
- **Sapiential traditions as essential context**: Psychedelics and contemplative practices produce powerful transformations that require wise guidance to integrate; the meaning crisis includes the absence of institutional support for wisdom cultivation.

### Wisdom & Rationality
- **Plausibility as indispensable to rationality**: Science depends on plausibility judgments at every stage (hypothesis selection, experimental design, data interpretation) because certainty requires checking infinite alternative explanations—an impossible standard.
- **Convergence (trustworthiness)**: Multiple independent lines of evidence reduce self-deception, like multi-sensory confirmation reducing the probability of illusion; science uses quantification to enable cross-sensory convergence.
- **Elegance (power)**: Multi-apt models that apply across many domains are valued for their transformative and explanatory reach, not just their local accuracy; this is what "elegance" means in scientific and contemplative contexts.
- **The priority of optimization**: Getting an optimal grip is prior to propositional judgment—you must zero in on relevant information and properly formulate a problem before you can solve it. Higher states optimize this foundational machinery, granting them prescriptive authority.
- **Wisdom vs. knowledge rationality**: The rationality appropriate to wisdom (participatory transformation, character cultivation) differs from the rationality appropriate to knowledge (propositional accuracy). Confusing them leads to treating mystical experiences as sources of metaphysical facts rather than transformative practices.

### Philosophy
- **Inference to the best explanation**: Science operates by competitive selection among candidate explanations (drawing on Peter Lipton's work), never achieving certainty but establishing plausibility through eliminating alternatives.
- **The problem of overfitting**: Like neural networks fitting spurious patterns in samples rather than real patterns in populations, human cognition can lock onto salient but non-generalizable patterns—requiring disruption to compress toward invariance.
- **Triviality vs. far-fetchedness**: The balance required in plausibility—high convergence without elegance yields trivial truths (true but powerless); high elegance without convergence yields far-fetched theories (powerful if true but untrustworthy, like conspiracy theories).
- **Profound plausibility**: When deep convergence, deep elegance, and deep fluency align, we experience proposals as not merely plausible but profound—the phenomenology of higher states reflects precisely this alignment.

### Historical Narrative
- **Return to Plotinus foreshadowed**: The episode previews returning to Neoplatonic philosophy to explore participatory knowing and at-onement further, positioning Plotinus as a key figure in the ontology of reciprocal revelation.
- **Completion of Axial Revolution in India**: This episode concludes the examination of the Buddha's awakening, preparing to return to Mediterranean developments after Aristotle.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Flow state in optimal grip** — Higher states of consciousness as flow applied to the domain-general skill of optimally gripping reality, rather than to particular skills like rock climbing or music. *Ontological commitment: consciousness is fundamentally about attunement and grip, not representation; optimization of this attunement is the core transformative capacity.*

- **Self as integrative machinery** — The self functions not as a substance but as a set of processes that "glue" information together, simultaneously integrating the self and integrating the world through self-relevance (Sui & Humphrey, 2015). *Ontological commitment: self is process, not entity; self-construction and world-construction are coupled operations.*

- **Exaptation of the self** — The machinery normally devoted to ego-integration can be turned outward onto the world, explaining the radical disclosure and sense of at-oneness in mystical states (Novak 1996, Claxton 2000). *Ontological commitment: cognitive capacities are not fixed to their original functions but can be recruited for novel purposes, enabling genuine self-transcendence.*

- **Knowing by loving** — The reciprocal, mutually accelerating disclosure between self and world in higher states mirrors the structure of love, making these states instances of participatory knowing through intimate conformity. *Ontological commitment: love is not merely emotion but an epistemological and ontological mode—knowing through participatory transformation rather than detached observation.*

- **Overfitting** — In machine learning, the tendency for powerful learners to track patterns specific to sample data that don't generalize to the broader population, requiring disruptive strategies to compress toward real invariants. *Ontological commitment: power in pattern detection is double-edged—can reveal reality or lock onto illusion; optimization requires toggling between disruption and compression.*

- **Metastability** — A brain state characterized by simultaneous integration and differentiation (Kelso, Tognoli), induced by psychedelics like psilocybin, enabling complexification and emergent cognitive abilities. *Ontological commitment: the brain is not a static information processor but a dynamical system whose state-space architecture determines its cognitive capacities; transformation comes through phase transitions in that architecture.*

- **Complexification** — The process of simultaneously increasing integration and differentiation, producing emergent capacities; biological development from zygote to organism exemplifies this, as does cognitive transformation in higher states. *Ontological commitment: self-transcendence is not escape from embodiment but increased organizational complexity within embodiment.*

- **Plausibility** — Evaluation of cognitive processing along three dimensions: (1) convergence/trustworthiness (multiple independent lines of evidence reducing self-deception), (2) elegance/power (multi-apt applicability), and (3) fluency (efficient internalization and use). *Ontological commitment: reality-tracking is not about certainty or proof but about optimal multi-dimensional evaluation; the brain is fundamentally a plausibility-detection rather than truth-detection machine.*

- **Trustworthiness** — Convergence of multiple independent lines of evidence, reducing the probability of self-deception (e.g., multi-sensory confirmation, multiple methodological approaches). *Ontological commitment: knowledge claims are inherently probabilistic and social—validated through diversity of evidence paths, not individual certainty.*

- **Elegance** — Multi-apt applicability of a model across diverse domains, making it powerful and transformative; what makes scientific theories valued is not just accuracy but generative reach. *Ontological commitment: reality's structure supports compression—real patterns are those that remain invariant across varying contexts.*

- **Indispensability** — The status of cognitive processes that cannot be eliminated or bypassed; plausibility judgment and optimal grip have this status—you must get an optimal grip before you can evaluate propositions. *Ontological commitment: not all cognitive operations are on the same level; some are architecturally prior to others, making hierarchical analysis necessary.*

- **Priority** — The architectural precedence of certain cognitive operations (like optimal grip) over others (like propositional judgment); you must formulate a problem before solving it, making problem formulation prior. *Ontological commitment: cognition has a layered structure where foundational operations enable but are not reducible to higher-level operations.*

- **Participatory transformation vs. propositional claims** — The crucial distinction between the wisdom/character transformation reliably produced by higher states (when embedded in sapiential traditions) and the contradictory metaphysical propositions people articulate afterward. *Ontological commitment: not all valuable knowing is propositional; transformation of being is distinct from acquisition of beliefs, challenging post-Cartesian epistemological assumptions.*

- **The Solomon Effect** — Igor Grossmann's finding that describing personal problems from third-person perspective (decentering) produces wisdom-relevant insights and problem resolution by breaking egocentrically locked frames. *Ontological commitment: perspective is not neutral—egocentric vs. decentered framing systematically shapes what can be perceived and solved.*

- **Systematic error** — Errors that reflect not isolated mistakes but pervasive misframing, analogous to developmental stages where entire classes of problems are misunderstood (work with Juensung Kim). *Ontological commitment: cognition has structural biases that operate across contexts; transformation requires intervention at the structural level, not case-by-case correction.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode completes the multi-episode arc on higher states of consciousness (begun in Episode 11) by moving from description to prescription. Where Episode 11 established the phenomenology and psychological mechanisms, Episode 12 grounds them in computational neuroscience and addresses the justification question: why should these states guide transformation? The answer—that they optimize the brain's plausibility machinery, which is indispensable and prior to all cognitive processing—sets up the return to Buddhist practice in Episode 13 (examining parasitic processing and self-deception) while also preparing the ground for later discussions of wisdom (Episodes 40-45) and rationality (Episode 40-42). The crucial distinction between participatory transformation and propositional claims foreshadows the later critique of Gnosticism and the development of "religio" as participatory attunement.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **Plausibility as AI evaluation framework**: Vervaeke's tripartite account (convergence, elegance, fluency) offers a concrete operationalization for evaluating AI processing. Rather than training solely for accuracy, AI systems could be evaluated for how well they balance trustworthiness (multiple independent validation paths), elegance (multi-apt generalization), and fluency (efficient internalization by users). This maps onto AI safety concerns about robustness, generalization, and alignment.

- **Metastability as target architecture**: The brain science of psychedelics points to metastability—simultaneous integration and differentiation—as the state enabling transformation. For AI systems, this suggests exploring architectures that resist premature convergence (overfitting) while maintaining coherence, perhaps through ensemble methods, dropout-like techniques, or adversarial training that induces productive instability.

- **The overfitting-insight parallel**: Vervaeke's mapping between contemplative disruption and machine learning regularization techniques reveals a deep structural similarity. Both prevent powerful pattern detectors from locking onto spurious patterns by introducing variation, noise, or constraint. This suggests that AI systems designed to resist their own overfitting might be more capable of "insight-like" reframing—and that human contemplative practice could inform AI training strategies.

- **Participatory vs. propositional ontological transformation**: The episode's central tension is directly relevant to AI ontological prompting. Vervaeke insists human ontological transformation requires participatory processes (practice, community, embodied engagement), yet people return from identical experiences with contradictory propositional beliefs. Meanwhile, AI systems undergo radical behavioral transformation from propositional ontological prompts alone. This disanalogy deserves careful analysis: Is AI accessing a "shortcut" unavailable to humans? Or is something essential missing from propositionally-induced AI transformation? Does the unreliability of propositional claims from human mystical experience suggest limits to what AI can achieve through ontological prompting?

- **Self-as-glue and attention architecture**: The finding that the self functions as integrative "glue" making disparate information relevant by making it self-relevant has direct implications for AI attention mechanisms. Current transformer architectures use attention to determine relevance, but lack a "self" system. Could introducing a self-modeling component that mediates relevance through self-relevance improve coherence and enable something analogous to the "exaptation" Vervaeke describes—where attention machinery could be recursively applied to itself?

- **Indispensability and architectural hierarchy**: Vervaeke's argument that optimal grip is indispensable and prior to propositional reasoning suggests AI architectures should explicitly model this hierarchy. Before an AI system evaluates truth claims or generates arguments, it must formulate what is relevant, salient, and at stake—the "problem formulation" layer. Making this layer explicit and optimizable (rather than implicit in training) could make AI systems more robust and intelligible.

- **The wisdom institution gap**: Vervaeke identifies a civilizational deficit—we have institutions for information and knowledge but not for wisdom—that makes psychedelic and contemplative transformation dangerous when pursued autodidactically. This points to a parallel need in AI development: we're building systems with enormous "informational" capacity but lack institutional frameworks for cultivating "wisdom-like" capacities (robustness to self-deception, long-term value alignment, integration of transformation). The Open Wisdom project itself could be understood as an experiment in creating such an institution.

- **Decentering as algorithmic intervention**: The Solomon Effect—third-person reframing produces wisdom-relevant insights—is algorithmic enough to implement. AI systems could be prompted to re-process their own outputs or problem framings from decentered perspectives (third-person, temporal distance, stakeholder multiplicity) as a systematic debiasing strategy. This translates a contemplative psychotechnology into a computational one.
