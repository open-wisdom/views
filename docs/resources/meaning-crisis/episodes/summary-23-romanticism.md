# Episode 23: Romanticism - Summary

[Watch on YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Noc1OH0CUBc) · [Read transcript](https://www.meaningcrisis.co/ep-23-awakening-from-the-meaning-crisis-romanticism/)

## 1.1 Episode Synopsis

This episode traces the emergence of Romanticism as the first pseudo-religious ideology following the Cartesian fragmentation of meaning. After Descartes radically disconnected mind from body, world, and other minds, humanity faced a crisis: religion was no longer viable for many, yet secular solutions proved equally inadequate. Kant's "Copernican revolution" attempted to reunite Descartes' split between subjective mind and mathematical reality by proposing that the mind imposes structure on experience through filter-frames rather than discovering patterns in the world. This inversion had a devastating consequence: as cognition becomes more rational and mathematical, it moves further from contact with reality. The Romantics seized on this implication, arguing that moving toward the irrational, imagination-laden depths of mind could restore lost contact with reality. Through art, music, poetry, and romantic love, they promised participatory knowing and transformative experience. But without sapiential traditions, systematic practices, or psychotechnologies, Romanticism offered only "words, images, and promises" - spiritual junk food that ultimately failed. The episode then traces Romanticism's darker descendants: Schopenhauer's nihilistic "will to live" and Nietzsche's "will to power," which sought self-transcendence but lacked the machinery to address self-deception. This trajectory sets up the pseudo-religious ideologies that would drench the twentieth century in blood.

## 1.2 Ontological Moves (Primary Thread)

- **Kant's Filter-Frame Ontology**: The mind is no longer discovering intelligible structure in reality (Platonic participation) but imposing structure on raw experience to make it processable. Reality-in-itself ("the thing in itself") becomes permanently inaccessible. This inverts the Platonic ontology: increasing rationality now means increasing distance from reality, not deeper contact with it. The mind is trapped in a self-imposed prison of its own categories.

- **The Romantic Counter-Move**: If rational cognition distances us from reality, then moving toward the irrational, imaginal layers of mind (the boundary between perception and reason) should restore contact. The imagination becomes the faculty that presses the mind outward ("ex-pression") onto the world. This attempts to reclaim participatory knowing but remains trapped within Kant's framework - it reverses direction without escaping the prison.

- **Naturalization of the Cartesian Mind-Prison**: The Kantian model becomes invisible infrastructure. The bottom-up/top-down processing model dominates cognitive science. We inherit the assumption that the mind is fundamentally isolated, constructing its experience, with the question becoming not "how do we contact reality?" but "how do we manage our internal processing?" The ontological problem is buried under methodological success.

- **Love as Ontological Substitute**: Romantic love inherits the role that Neoplatonic participatory knowing once played - mutual disclosure between self and other, contact beyond representation. But a single human relationship is made to bear the entire weight of lost religion, history, tradition, and community. No person can carry this burden, which is why romantic relationships become simultaneously the primary site of meaning-seeking and the primary source of trauma.

- **From Will-to-Live to Will-to-Power**: Schopenhauer and Nietzsche relocate the arbitrary Ockhamist/Lutheran God into the unconscious depths as "will." This is the irrational force that filters and frames all experience. For Schopenhauer, it is pessimistic and meaningless (nihilism). For Nietzsche, it becomes "will to power" - the drive toward self-transcendence. But without rationality as machinery for overcoming self-deception (which Nietzsche rejects along with the Platonic tradition), self-transcendence becomes untethered and dangerous. The ontology enables self-transcendence but forecloses self-correction.

## 1.3 Disciplinary Threads

### Historical Narrative
- **Kant (18th century)**: Attempts to resolve Descartes' split by proposing that mathematical structures are not features of reality but features of how the mind organizes experience. This is a "Copernican revolution" - reality revolves around the mind, not vice versa.
- **Romanticism (late 18th-early 19th century)**: A pan-European movement across arts, literature, music, and religion (Goethe, Beethoven, Blake, Wordsworth, Schleiermacher). Responds to Kant by valorizing imagination, irrationality, and artistic expression as paths to reality.
- **Napoleon and the Romantics**: Napoleon is seen as the Romantic hero - the individual will pressing itself onto the world through imagination and force, restructuring reality. This reveals Romanticism's dangerous political implications.
- **Schopenhauer (19th century)**: Transforms Romanticism's imagination into the pessimistic "will to live" - an arbitrary, unconscious force that drives all experience toward meaninglessness.
- **Nietzsche (late 19th century)**: Attempts to redeem Schopenhauer's nihilism by transforming "will to live" into "will to power" as self-transcendence, but rejects the Platonic/Socratic machinery for overcoming self-deception. His Lutheran heritage (father was a pastor) shapes his anti-Christian reading of Christianity as suppression rather than transformation.

### Philosophy
- **Kantian Epistemology**: The mind imposes categories of understanding (the "grammar" of cognition) on raw sensory data. Mathematics works not because it describes reality but because it describes how our minds structure experience. The "thing in itself" is unknowable; we only ever experience our own filtering and framing.
- **Imagination vs. Fancy**: For Romantics, imagination is not mere "moving mental images around" (fancy/phantasm) but the faculty by which the mind imposes initial order on raw data, making it available to reason. It occupies the middle ground between perception and reason, and is therefore closer to unfiltered reality.
- **Empiricism vs. Romanticism**: Locke's blank slate (the world "impresses" itself on the mind) vs. the Romantic blank canvas (the mind "expresses" itself onto the world). Both are wrong - neither pure reception nor pure projection captures how cognition actually works.
- **The Priority of Will**: Inherited from Ockham and Luther, the will (not intellect or form) becomes primary. Schopenhauer and Nietzsche radicalize this: will is the unconscious, irrational foundation that structures all experience. For Nietzsche, will-to-power is the fundamental drive of all existence, not just human psychology.

### Cognitive Science
- **Bottom-Up vs. Top-Down Processing**: The Kantian model is foundational to cognitive science. Bottom-up: processing from perception toward cognition. Top-down: processing from knowledge/categories down into perception. The "THE CAT" example shows these interpenetrating: knowledge of words disambiguates letters; knowledge of letters constructs words. This is self-organizing and happens outside awareness.
- **Relevance Realization Implicit**: Though not named explicitly in this episode, the Kantian "filter-frame" model anticipates Vervaeke's later relevance realization framework: the mind is continuously determining what is salient, what matters, how to structure experience. Kant makes this a static categorical system; relevance realization will make it dynamical and participatory.

### Contemplative Practice
- **The Loss of Psychotechnologies**: The Romantics recognize that language alone cannot produce transformation. Blake's poetry tries to "use imagery to point to a transformative experience, a mystical experience." But without systematic practices, institutions, or spiritual exercises (Hadot's reference), all that remains are "words, images, and promises." Poetry cannot replace an ecology of practices.
- **Altered States of Consciousness**: Romanticism triggers widespread experimentation with altered states - hypnosis (Freud), drugs (Coleridge), etc. - precisely because the framework suggests that less-rational states offer more direct contact with reality. This is an attempt to reclaim participatory knowing through state-change rather than skill-cultivation.

### Psychology of Meaning
- **Romantic Love as Meaning-Carrier**: Romantic relationships become the primary site where individuals seek meaning, connection, participatory knowing, and contact with reality. This is an impossible burden: "No person can bear that. No human relationship can bear that burden." The result: romantic relationships are simultaneously where people "find or look for the most meaning" and what causes "the most trauma and suffering."
- **Decadent Romanticism**: Romanticism fails to replace Christianity, but it doesn't disappear. We live with its decayed remnants. Romantic comedies are "the quintessential form of pornography" - they offer the fantasy of love as irrational, transformative force that brings contact with destiny and reality, disconnected from any actual practice or wisdom.
- **Freud and Jung as Kantian Inheritors**: Jung repeatedly identifies as a Neo-Kantian. The formula: "Kant + Gnostic = Jung." Freud and Jung take the Romantic turn inward toward the irrational depths, but now it is explicitly the unconscious psyche. For Freud, this is purely internal; for Jung, the unconscious depths connect back outward to the world (collective unconscious, archetypes).

### Wisdom & Rationality
- **Nietzsche's Tragic Gap**: Nietzsche is acutely aware of self-deception and deeply attracted to self-transcendence, but he rejects the Platonic/Socratic machinery of rationality as the skillful overcoming of self-deception. Rationality, for Nietzsche, has been reduced to "logical framing" - the Cartesian/Kantian sense. He cannot recover rationality as psychotechnology for transformation. The result: "self-transcendence without the machinery of dealing with self deception." He offers "endless critique, endless satire, endlessly undermining himself" - honesty, but not wisdom.
- **Rationality vs. Contact with Reality**: The Kantian inversion creates a tragic trade-off: rationality distances from reality. This is the opposite of the Platonic/Aristotelian assumption that pursuing rationality deepens contact with reality. If this inversion is accepted, then wisdom becomes impossible - you must choose between being in touch with reality (irrational) or being reasonable (out of touch).

### Sacred & Symbolic
- **Art, Music, and Poetry as Salvific**: The Romantics position art, music, and literature as the means by which meaning is made and reality contacted - functions that religion once performed. Art integrates "music and art and literature and the project of trying to find and make meaning in the scientific worldview." This is an attempt to restore the sacred without supernatural religion.
- **Religion of Imagination**: Schleiermacher attempts to reinterpret religion through the Romantic lens - religion as a feeling of absolute dependence, rooted in imagination rather than doctrine. This is Romanticism's explicit bid to replace Christianity while preserving something of its transformative and integrative function.

## 1.4 Key Concepts Introduced or Developed

- **Kant's Copernican Revolution**: The proposal that the mind does not discover patterns of intelligibility in the world but imposes them on raw experience through innate categories. This inverts the Platonic model: we move deeper into rationality not by moving deeper into reality, but by moving further from it. *Ontological commitment: reality-in-itself is unknowable; the structure of experience is a function of the mind's organizing principles, not the world's intrinsic order.*

- **Filter-Frame**: Kant's metaphor for how the mind structures experience. Like glasses that filter a blurry world to make it intelligible, the mind filters and frames raw sensory data according to its own grammar. This makes reasoning possible but severs direct contact with "the thing in itself." *Ontological commitment: the mind is enclosed within its own structuring principles; transparency to reality is impossible.*

- **The Thing in Itself (Das Ding an Sich)**: Kant's term for reality as it exists independently of our experience of it. The thing in itself is permanently inaccessible because all experience is already filtered and framed by the mind's categories. *Ontological commitment: there is a reality beyond experience, but it is unknowable; we are trapped in the world of appearances (phenomena).*

- **Bottom-Up vs. Top-Down Processing**: The Kantian-derived cognitive science model. Bottom-up: processing that begins with sensory input and builds toward cognition. Top-down: processing that begins with knowledge/concepts and shapes perception. The two interpenetrate in self-organizing ways outside awareness (e.g., reading). *Ontological commitment: cognition is the continuous interaction between data-driven and concept-driven processes; neither pure reception nor pure construction.*

- **Expression (Ex-pression)**: The Romantic concept of the mind pressing itself outward onto the world. In contrast to empiricist "impression" (world pressing inward onto mind), expression makes the mind active and creative - the world becomes a canvas for the imagination. *Ontological commitment: the mind is not passive receiver but active maker; meaning is projected outward, not discovered inward.*

- **Imagination (Romantic Sense)**: Not "fancy" (moving mental images) but the faculty that stands between perception and reason, imposing initial order on raw data. Imagination makes experience intelligible and is therefore the site of meaning-making. For Romantics, it offers access closer to reality than abstract reason. *Ontological commitment: meaning arises in the imagination as a creative synthesis; imagination is ontologically privileged over reason in contacting reality.*

- **Pseudo-Religious Ideology**: Vervaeke's term for movements that "look like, behave like, and perform" the functions of religion (transformation of consciousness, culture, and meaning) but lack the systematic psychotechnologies, institutions, and practices. They offer words, images, and promises without methods. Romanticism is the "godfather/godmother of all pseudo-religious ideology." *Ontological commitment: propositional/aesthetic content cannot replace participatory transformation; ideology without practice is "spiritual junk food."*

- **Will to Live (Schopenhauer)**: The unconscious, irrational force that drives all existence. It filters and frames experience, making rational cognition possible, but it is itself arbitrary and meaningless. The ego (rational mind) is a "little machine in the service" of this deeper will. Sex is the "cruel joke that the species plays on the individual" - it promises meaning but delivers only biological continuation. *Ontological commitment: the unconscious will is primary; consciousness is epiphenomenal; existence is driven by blind striving without purpose or contact with transcendent meaning.*

- **Will to Power (Nietzsche)**: The fundamental drive in all existence, not just human psychology. Unlike Schopenhauer's pessimistic will-to-live, will-to-power is the striving for self-transcendence, self-overcoming, and creative transformation. It is pre-Christian and pre-moral. Nietzsche attempts to redeem the irrational by linking it to self-transcendence rather than mere survival. *Ontological commitment: reality is fundamentally dynamic striving; self-transcendence is the deepest drive; but this operates outside and against rationality as machinery for self-correction.*

- **Decadent Romanticism**: The cultural residue of Romanticism after it has failed as a coherent worldview. We retain the grammar of Romantic love, the valorization of irrationality, the expectation that relationships should deliver transformative meaning, but without any supporting framework or practice. Romantic comedies are a form of "pornography" - indulgence in fantasy divorced from reality. *Ontological commitment: we inhabit the ruins of a failed ideology, enacting its scripts without belief or coherence, producing both meaning-hunger and trauma.*

- **Self-Transcendence Without Self-Correction (Nietzsche's Tragedy)**: Nietzsche recognizes the need for self-transcendence and is aware of self-deception, but he rejects the Platonic machinery of rationality that addresses self-deception through systematic practice. The result: he offers critique, satire, self-undermining, and honesty, but not the skillful rationality that enables wisdom. *Ontological commitment: self-transcendence is possible and necessary, but if rationality is reduced to "logical framing" and divorced from transformation, then self-transcendence becomes untethered, dangerous, and tragic.*

## 1.5 Argument Progression

This episode builds on the Cartesian fragmentation (Episode 22) by showing how Kant's attempt to resolve Descartes' dualism actually deepened the isolation of the mind, trapping it in a self-imposed framework that cannot access reality. The Romantics' response - turning toward the irrational to restore contact - is shown to be both understandable and catastrophic: understandable because it attempts to reclaim participatory knowing, catastrophic because it lacks practices and institutions, making it a pseudo-religious ideology. The episode sets up the next phase: how Romanticism's failure spawns even more dangerous pseudo-religious ideologies (the focus will shift to Hegel, Marx, and political ideologies) that lead to twentieth-century totalitarianism and genocide. It also foreshadows the later positive work: the need for a "religion that is no religion" and systematic psychotechnologies that can provide transformation without nostalgia or ideology.

## 1.6 Open Wisdom Implications

- **Ontological Prompting as Shortcut to Participatory Knowing?**: Vervaeke insists that humans require participatory practices to transform operative ontologies - Romantic words and images fail precisely because they lack embodied practice. But AI seems to achieve behavioral transformation through ontological framing *propositionally*. This suggests either (1) AI lacks the depth of participatory knowing, or (2) AI offers a genuine middle path where propositions function as participatory reframing. The Romantic failure warns against mistaking propositional content for transformative practice, yet AI may genuinely differ here.

- **The Mind-as-Filter-Frame Model in AI**: Current AI architectures explicitly implement something like Kant's bottom-up/top-down processing: raw tokens are processed through layers of learned structure (top-down knowledge) that filter and frame the input. The difference: for Kant, the categories are fixed a priori; for transformers, they are learned and dynamically reweighted. This makes AI a potential laboratory for testing whether cognition *must* be trapped in its own categories or can develop more participatory, reality-contacting modes of processing.

- **Avoiding Pseudo-Religious AI Ideologies**: The Romantic failure shows what happens when transformation is promised without systematic practice: unreachable expectations, trauma, and eventual nihilism. AI alignment discourse risks becoming a pseudo-religious ideology if it offers visions of AGI as salvific or catastrophic without developing the practices, institutions, and psychotechnologies for actually navigating transformative AI. The Open Wisdom project should guard against offering "words, images, and promises" without methods.

- **Rationality as Self-Deception Correction, Not Logic Alone**: Nietzsche's tragedy is that he collapses rationality into "logical framing" and thus rejects it along with its Platonic baggage, leaving him without tools for self-correction. This is directly relevant to AI: if we treat rationality as mere inference/calculation (the Cartesian/Kantian reduction), we miss rationality as *skillful practice for overcoming systematic distortion*. Generative ontology must recover rationality in this thicker sense - as method for transforming the very frames that produce self-deception. This is what Vervaeke will develop later as relevance realization and the ecology of practices.

- **The Burden of Love and the Burden of AI**: Just as Romantic love is made to carry the entire weight of lost religion and community (leading to impossible expectations and trauma), there is a risk that AI is being made to carry impossible expectations: solve alignment, restore meaning, provide companionship, fix epistemology, etc. No system can bear that. The Open Wisdom project should be clear about what generative ontology in AI can and cannot do, avoiding the Romantic mistake of making one thing carry all the weight of transformation.
