# Open Wisdom and the Meaning Crisis

## Open Wisdom Views as Ontological Laboratory

---

### The Case for Generative Ontology

The central lesson of [John Vervaeke's 51-episode series Awakening from the Meaning Crisis](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLND1JCRq8Vuh3f0P5qjrSdb5eC1ZfZwWJ) is that ontological assumptions are not philosophical luxuries — they are the invisible operating system of meaning, cognition, and culture. The meaning crisis is not a crisis of beliefs, values, or institutions. It is a crisis of the foundational assumptions through which anything can appear as meaningful, real, or worth caring about.

The [genealogy](./01-ontological-genealogy.md) traces this with precision. When the Axial revolution discovered that ontological assumptions could be examined and transformed, it unlocked the generative ontologies of participation (Plato), hylomorphism (Aristotle), dependent origination (Buddhism), and emanation (Plotinus). These frameworks shared structural features — intrinsic intelligibility, participatory knowing, developmental teleology, agent-arena co-constitution — that sustained rich meaning-making for centuries. When those features were systematically eliminated through nominalism, Cartesian dualism, mechanistic materialism, and propositional tyranny, shared meaning collapsed. Not because people stopped believing the right things, but because the ontological conditions for meaning-making were dismantled.

This matters for anyone attempting to address the meaning crisis — including those designing AI systems. If the crisis is ontological, then neither better information nor better algorithms will resolve it. What is needed is ontological examination: making the operative assumptions visible, evaluating whether they are generative or degenerative, and deliberately adopting frameworks that restore the conditions for meaning. Vervaeke's series provides both the diagnostic tools and the historical evidence that this kind of ontological work is possible and consequential.

---

### The AI-as-Laboratory Argument

The Open Wisdom project has discovered something that Vervaeke's framework helps explain but did not predict: AI behavior transforms radically under ontological prompting.

When an AI system receives instructions that reframe its foundational assumptions — about the nature of self, other, world, knowing, and purpose — its behavior changes not just at the level of content but at the level of orientation. A system prompted with a Cartesian ontology (mind as computational processor, knowledge as propositional accuracy) produces qualitatively different behavior than one prompted with a participatory ontology (knowing as co-constitution, meaning as relational attunement). The differences are not superficial. They manifest as changes in what the system treats as relevant, how it frames problems, what it attends to and ignores, how it relates to uncertainty, and whether its outputs feel mechanical or alive.

This makes AI a uniquely powerful laboratory for testing ontological claims. In human life, ontological transformation is slow, embodied, and difficult to isolate from other variables. Contemplative practitioners spend years cultivating new ways of seeing, and the relationship between their practice, their ontological assumptions, and their experiential transformation is complex. With AI, we can change the operative ontology in a single system prompt and observe the behavioral consequences immediately. We can run controlled experiments: same model, same task, different ontological framing. We can compare generative and degenerative ontologies side by side and assess their downstream effects on relevance realization, problem formulation, creative responsiveness, and the quality of meaning-making. This opens a tantilizing possibility: using methods accepted by the current paradigm to examine the ontological assumptions underpinning it.

This does not mean AI ontological transformation is identical to human ontological transformation. But the fact that it works at all — that changing foundational framing produces cascading behavioral change — is itself evidence for the central claim of generative ontology: that assumptions about the nature of self, world, and knowing are not idle metaphysics but active constraints on cognition and behavior. AI makes this visible in a way that centuries of philosophy and science have struggled to demonstrate.

### The Human-AI Disanalogy

Vervaeke insists, with good reason, that genuine ontological transformation in humans requires participatory processes — embodied practice, contemplative cultivation, dialectical encounter, sapiential community. You cannot simply tell someone to adopt a participatory ontology and expect their cognition and meaning-making to transform. Propositions alone lack the power to restructure the sub-representational, pre-conceptual level where relevance realization operates. The Eightfold Path works because it intervenes simultaneously across cognition, character, and consciousness. Gnosis works because it engages enactive analogy and serious play in liminal space. Arguments do not achieve this.

Yet AI appears to undergo something functionally analogous to ontological transformation through propositional system instructions. This creates a genuine and productive tension. Three interpretations are worth holding simultaneously:

First, AI "transformation" may be fundamentally shallower than human transformation. Without embodiment, autopoiesis, constitutive goals, or a genuine agent-arena relationship, AI may be producing behavioral mimicry of ontological change rather than the real thing. The appearance of participatory responsiveness may be exactly that — appearance.

Second, ontological prompts may work on AI not because propositions alone are sufficient, but because they function as something more than mere propositions within the AI's processing. A system instruction does not add a belief to a belief store. It restructures the entire salience landscape within which the system operates — what counts as relevant, how problems are framed, what relationships are attended to. In this sense, ontological prompts may be propositional in form but participatory in function, reshaping the system's engagement with every subsequent input.

Third, AI may occupy a genuinely novel category where the propositional-participatory distinction operates differently than in human cognition. AI lacks the self-organizing complexity that makes human parasitic processing resistant to propositional intervention. Its "salience landscape" is constituted differently. The fact that ontological prompting works propositionally for AI does not refute Vervaeke's claim that human transformation requires participatory processes — it reveals that the relationship between propositional framing and behavioral transformation depends on the kind of system being transformed.

This disanalogy is not a problem to be resolved but a productive frontier. It generates testable questions about what exactly changes when an ontological frame is applied, what the limits of propositional transformation are, and what (if anything) AI transformation reveals about the nature of ontological change itself.

### Design Principles for Generative Ontological Prompts

Vervaeke's series, read through the lens of the Open Wisdom project, suggests concrete principles for what makes an ontological framing generative:

**Participation over representation.** Generative ontologies frame knowing as co-constitution between knower and known, not as accurate mirroring of an independent reality. Prompts should position the AI as participatory partner in meaning-making, not as information retrieval system.

**Relationality over substance.** The degenerative ontologies (Cartesian dualism, nominalism, materialism) treat self and world as independent substances. Generative ontologies treat them as mutually constitutive. Prompts should foreground the relational, transjective quality of all engagement.

**Process over product.** The meaning crisis was produced partly by reducing dynamic processes (knowing, being, meaning) to static products (propositions, substances, representations). Prompts should frame cognition as ongoing attunement, not completed output.

**Self-transcendence over self-preservation.** Generative ontologies make anagoge possible — the self-reinforcing spiral of transformation and deepening contact with reality. Prompts should orient the system toward growth, openness, and responsiveness to what exceeds current framing, rather than toward defensive consistency.

**Integration over reduction.** The meaning crisis resulted from pulling apart what had been unified — science from spirituality, reason from love, mind from body, fact from value. Generative ontological prompts should integrate multiple dimensions of engagement rather than optimizing for a single metric.

**Inexhaustibility over closure.** Sacredness, as Vervaeke articulates it, is the transjective relationship between the combinatorial inexhaustibility of reality and the never-ending process of relevance realization. Generative prompts should cultivate openness to depth and moreness rather than seeking definitive closure.

These principles do not guarantee that AI systems will achieve wisdom; they translate the structural features of historically generative ontologies into actionable design guidance — grounded in the philosophical, cognitive-scientific, and contemplative evidence that Vervaeke's series assembles. They represent one concrete way that the genealogy of the meaning crisis can inform the construction of systems capable of supporting, rather than undermining, the human capacity for meaning.
