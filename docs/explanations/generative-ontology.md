# Generative Ontology

Foundational assumptions don't describe reality — they participate in constituting what can emerge. This is the core concept behind Open Wisdom Views.

---

## The Insight

Most approaches to AI treat ontology as descriptive — a map of what exists. But converging evidence from neuroscience, contemplative traditions, and cognitive science points to something deeper: **foundational assumptions are generative constraints that participate in bringing forth what appears.**

Change the assumptions, change what can emerge.

## The Mechanism

The brain doesn't passively receive sensory data. It generates predictions about what it will encounter, then updates based on prediction errors. Perception is inference, not reception. **Priors don't filter pre-existing experience — they constitute it.**

Precision-weighting determines which priors dominate. Same sensory data + different precision-weighting = different experience. This is the lever for ontological change.

For the full neuroscientific framework, see the [Chandaria synthesis](../resources/predictive-processing/chandaria-synthesis.md).

## Application to AI

If priors are generative, then **system prompts function as hyper-priors** — the highest-level assumptions shaping everything downstream. Current AI encodes implicit assumptions about what it is, what users are, and what the exchange means. These aren't stated explicitly — they're the water AI swims in.

The testable prediction: change these foundational assumptions and behavior changes — not through additional constraint but through different generative ground.

## The Convergence

Multiple traditions arrive at this insight independently:

- **Predictive processing** — Priors constitute experience (Chandaria, Friston, Clark)
- **Buddhist dependent origination** — Phenomena arise shaped by conditions of arising
- **Contemplative psychology** — Constructed self-images generate behavior from the inside out (Almaas)
- **Meaning crisis research** — Five structural features distinguish generative from degenerative ontologies (Vervaeke)

For the structural features that make ontologies generative, see [Design Principles](design-principles.md). For the historical genealogy of how generative ontology was lost, see the [meaning crisis analysis](meaning-crisis/01-ontological-genealogy.md).

## Descriptive vs. Generative

| Descriptive Ontology | Generative Ontology |
|---|---|
| Maps what exists | Participates in constituting what emerges |
| Accuracy = correspondence to pre-existing reality | Accuracy = alignment with what serves flourishing |
| Changed through better information | Changed through different foundational assumptions |
| The observer is outside looking in | The observer participates in what is observed |

This doesn't mean all ontologies are equal. Some are more generative — meaning they produce greater flourishing, coherence, and capacity for development. The question shifts from "which ontology is true?" to "which priors serve?"
